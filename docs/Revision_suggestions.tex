\section*{Independent Review --- ACM TOMM Revision Cycle (Status Audit 2026-09-01)}

\subsection*{Overall Assessment}
The manuscript is internally consistent for the current fixed-budget
proxy12/proxy50 retrieval study, the corrected E4 detection/segmentation
evaluation, and the newly integrated MSLS real-place validation. The review
requests concerning true geographic place labels, matched operating points,
stronger baselines, margin-level evidence, and MixVPR transfer have now been
addressed in the manuscript with explicit scope limitations. The original
released checkpoint fails the non-constant-map diagnostic, but a new
mask-backed checkpoint has now completed the registered KITTI-360 run. The
result is a negative attribution-consistency diagnostic rather than evidence
that the learned unary map is valid.

\textbf{Recommendation:} The current revision is substantially strengthened
and all registered review experiments have been executed. The paper must keep
the unary-map result qualified as a negative attribution diagnostic, the proxy
results explicitly scoped as paired-scene retrieval evidence, and the MSLS
result scoped to its realized two-city, day/forward-view and cross-time
protocols.

\subsection*{Current Evidence That Is Already Adequate}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The released implementation now defines the sigmoid, average-pooling
    smoother, kernel, stride, padding, mean-field iterations, and temporal
    reuse. The unary architecture and checkpoint provenance are also documented.
    \item The proxy12 and proxy50 retrieval tables use the current CSV exports,
    and the manuscript qualifies transfer as attacker-dependent. The fixed
    \(\sigma_0=8\) comparison is no longer described as a matched-privacy
    advantage.
    \item E4 detection and segmentation use the final same-image manifests,
    indexed VOC targets, the official DeepLabV3 preprocessing,
    nearest-neighbour target resizing, and restored output logits. The mAP and
    mIoU results are integrated in the main paper.
    \item Official-source MSLS runs now provide true place clusters, two
    cities, 200 queries, a 1,000-image gallery, disjointness checks, capture
    dates, and the o2n/n2o cross-time condition split. The realized subset is
    still limited to day/forward-view samples under one attacker backbone, with
    empty season/weather fields.
    \item Matched-PSNR/effective-MSE results, the constrained attacker-aware
    diagnostic, margin subgroups, and the retrieval case study are integrated
    with the required qualifications. The proxy remains a visual-pair
    benchmark rather than a geographic benchmark.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Public Dataset Decision and Evidence Status}
The primary E1 dataset is Mapillary Street-Level Sequences (MSLS). Its
official release describes 1.6 million street-level images and provides raw
longitude/latitude, capture time, sequence IDs, view direction, unique place
clusters, and standardized cross-condition subtasks. The official release and
metadata are available at
\url{https://github.com/mapillary/mapillary_sls} and
\url{https://www.mapillary.com/dataset/places}.

Oxford RobotCar is retained as a future fallback because its official release has more
than 100 repetitions of a route over more than one year, GPS/INS ground truth,
and changes in weather, traffic, lighting, construction, and season. It
requires registration and is licensed for non-commercial academic use:
\url{https://robotcar-dataset.robots.ox.ac.uk/}.

The primary E5 diagnostic dataset is KITTI-360. The official release contains over
320,000 images, accurate geolocation, an IMU/GPS system, continuous vehicle
poses, and 2D semantic and instance labels. Its download requires registration
and an intended-use declaration under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0:
\url{https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti-360/}.

Kaggle mirrors are not treated as authoritative. They may be used only if the
downloaded files, metadata, checksums, and license terms are verified against
the official release. A mirror that omits GPS, sequence, condition, or label
metadata cannot pass the scientific-evidence gate.

The targeted Kaggle audit found one potentially useful fallback mirror,
\url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/creatorofuniverses/oxfordrobotcar-iprofi-hack-23},
which describes a pre-processed Oxford RobotCar place-recognition subset and
claims GPS/INS coverage. It remains a convenience mirror rather than the
source of record because its file list and preprocessing must be compared with
the registered Oxford release. The Kaggle
\url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/greatgamedota/kitti360-3d-semantics}
entry is not currently acceptable for E5: its card describes fused labelled
3D point clouds and reports an unknown license, without establishing the
RGB--pose--2D-mask topology required by the validator. Mapillary Vistas
mirrors are also unsuitable for E1 because Vistas is a segmentation collection,
not the MSLS place/sequence benchmark. These mirrors may support smoke or
utility preparation, but none may supply paper-facing E1/E5 numbers without
the official-source verification gate.

\subsection*{Implementation and Paper-Writeback Status}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Keep the current monitoring proxy as the reproducible engineering
    benchmark and do not relabel its synthetic pairs as geographic places.
    \item The official-source MSLS JSONL manifest and real-place benchmark are
    complete. The manifest preserves unique-cluster place labels and records
    city, condition, GPS, sequence, and capture-time fields; all required
    disjointness, label, metadata, and output-schema gates passed.
    \item The KITTI-360 sequence-held-out manifest links images, semantic masks,
    poses, and pose-derived place cells. The released checkpoint failed its
    non-constant-map gate, while the mask-backed 0000-trained checkpoint passed
    the operational gate on held-out 0002. Its near-zero attribution agreement
    is documented as negative diagnostic evidence, not positive validation.
    \item E2--E4 and E6--E7 have been run and written back to the main paper or
    appendix. All numerical claims retain the protocol, attacker, gallery, and
    baseline limitations recorded in the table above.
    \item The manuscript is self-contained: implementation details are stated
    without repository-local paths, while released exports are described by
    their benchmark role rather than by internal filenames.
    \item The remaining optional work is broader MSLS viewpoint, illumination,
    season, and weather coverage or a new unary target/checkpoint designed to
    improve attribution agreement. The exploratory Manila s2w run is recorded
    separately but is too narrow to remove this limitation. Neither is required
    to support the current scoped results, and neither is represented as broad
    condition coverage.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Manuscript Writeback and Remaining Limits}
The main paper now contains the approved E1 protocol and true place-level
retrieval metrics, together with a clear separation between the proxy and
geographic benchmarks. The appendix contains the MSLS manifest schema,
KITTI-360 attribution protocol and negative agreement diagnostic, the detector
mAP table, margin subgroup analysis, matched-PSNR analysis, and the retrieval
case-study figure. Claims about the learned unary predictor remain limited to
checkpoint provenance and the negative attribution diagnostic; the operational
non-constant-map gate is not a sensitivity-accuracy guarantee. Claims about
cross-condition robustness likewise remain limited because the current MSLS
subset is day/forward-view with cross-time variation but no season/weather
metadata.

\textbf{Disposition:} E1--E4 and E6--E7 are complete with the scope
qualifications stated above. E5 is complete as an executed negative
attribution diagnostic: the operational gate passes for the mask-backed
checkpoint, but the attribution agreement is near zero and no independently
validated sensitivity-map claim is approved. Broader condition coverage and a
more predictive unary target remain future work rather than missing results in
this revision cycle.
