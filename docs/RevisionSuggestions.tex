\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This is a fresh review of the current submission package against the IEEE TIFS
author requirements and the standards a TIFS referee would apply. It does not
use any previous review file, the project's progress notes, or any earlier
completion judgment as input. Every statement below was checked against the
sources and the rendered PDFs named here; where a claim about the
implementation was needed, the code under \file{src/} was read directly.

The reviewed main source is \file{paper/main.tex}, SHA-256 prefix
\file{1df4fd3d1895d09b}; its PDF has 13 pages. The reviewed
\file{paper/supplementary.tex}, prefix \file{2074fabb40dfb5c9}, has six pages.
The bibliography \file{paper/ref.bib} (prefix \file{cca4491876ea1723}) holds 63
entries, of which 47 are cited. Six tables are generated from exports
(\file{paper/generated/}); the two real-place tables are among them.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: minor revision.} The previous cycle's
obstacles are gone. The reproducibility package is reachable without
credentials, the two documents no longer disagree about which experiment the
adaptive-attacker section reports, the statements about attacker--surrogate
relatedness now name trunks and match the checkpoints the code loads, the
utility frontier carries seeds and intervals, and the declared utility
tolerance has a measured reference scale rather than an assertion. Formatting,
length, abstract, numbering, responsible use and data terms are all compliant.
What remains falls into two groups. The first is a set of \emph{cross-document
and cross-table integrity defects} --- one pointer that promises material the
supplement does not contain, one hardcoded table number that now resolves to
the wrong table, one significance marker attached to a pooled cell that was
never tested, and one calibration sentence whose two numbers appear in no table
in either document. None of these changes a conclusion, but each is the kind of
thing a referee finds by following a reference, and finding three of them in
one reading will cost the paper credibility it has otherwise earned. The second
group is genuine scientific scope: the real-world evidence rests on a single
retrieval dataset, and the allocation null is established against
hand-specified placements and the authors' own two checkpoints rather than
against any independently trained selective sanitizer. Those two are honest
limitations rather than errors, and the paper should either address or state
them.

\subsection{Summary of the work}
The paper asks whether visual location-privacy mechanisms buy protection
through \emph{where} a bounded distortion budget is spent or through \emph{where
the perturbation points}. It proposes a protocol that holds delivered
distortion fixed and varies one design axis at a time --- spatial placement,
perturbation operator, direction --- and applies it to a CRF-inspired selective
sanitizer built as a testbed, whose released support map turns out to be
numerically constant. Across eight placement rules, three published-model
placements, two gradient-guided placements and four operators, no allocation
beats uniform spreading at the low-distortion operating point on a
place-labelled MSLS subset, against a weak and a strong retriever; on that
benchmark every difference from uniform is within $\pm0.012$ Top-1. A
surrogate-optimised direction transfers to four held-out attackers at the same
delivered distortion, shrinking by an order of magnitude against the one whose
trunk no surrogate shares. The paper then decomposes the direction effect,
measures its downstream cost over four budgets against two motivated
tolerances, repeats the utility measurement on the frames retrieval is scored
on, audits the serialized release, evaluates two adaptive attackers, and offers
a conditional first-order account checked against a controlled model with an
exactly known Jacobian.

\subsection{Strengths worth preserving}
\begin{itemize}
\item The delivered-MSE matching solved per frame through the pixel clamp, the
energy-conservation gate, and the constant-map diagnostic are reusable
methodological contributions, independent of this paper's conclusions.
\item The paper repeatedly reports against itself: high-budget placement gains
that qualify its own thesis, a controlled model in which placement is live, a
strong attacker on which most of the benefit is unreachable, a cross-dataset
pairing it names as such, and a tolerance at which its own operating point is
not affordable.
\item Inference is defensible and stated: the query is the unit with seeds
averaged within it, place-clustered intervals accompany the headline contrasts,
a primary family of four contrasts is Holm-corrected, everything else is
labelled exploratory, and a $\pm0.01$ Top-1 equivalence margin is fixed before
testing with ``no benefit detected'' distinguished from ``negligible''.
\item The tolerance calibration is the right kind of answer to a question about
an arbitrary threshold: it measures the spread between segmenters the community
treats as interchangeable, on the same images at the same working resolution,
and reports both a generous and a strict line with the verdicts that differ
between them.
\item The cluster-versus-geographic audit of the MSLS place labels
($99.3\%$ agreement at 25\,m, $77.5\%$ at 10\,m, and the frank statement that
the angular half of the original criterion cannot be reproduced) is more
diligence than this benchmark usually receives.
\item The adaptive-attacker section identifies that selecting on a floored
metric manufactures a null, and fixes it by selecting on mean reciprocal rank.
That observation generalises beyond this paper.
\end{itemize}

\section{Official submission requirements and package audit}

\subsection{Policy baseline}

are the venue's requirements, not the reviewer's:
\begin{itemize}
\item Initial regular papers: at most 13 double-column pages in 10-point type,
references included; revised papers up to 16.
\item Abstract 150--250 words, self-contained, without abbreviations,
references, footnotes, or displayed equations.
\item Single-anonymized review; authors visible; ORCIDs for all authors;
submission metadata must match the manuscript; EDICS selected.
\item Supplementary material recommended to stay within six double-column
pages; more requires Editor-in-Chief approval. Numbering must be unambiguous
across manuscript and supplement.

are required.
\end{itemize}
TIFS additionally publishes reproducibility guidance for deep-learning
submissions~\cite{tifsdl}: the paper itself, not only its code, must describe
architecture, preprocessing, training, model selection, data splits, software,
and hardware; and it raises that expectation for negative results that
challenge published work~\cite{tifsscope}.

\section{Major findings and required revisions}

\finding{R1}{Major}{A cross-document pointer promises material the supplement
does not contain}
\evidence
The adaptive-attacker section states: ``The Supplementary Material gives the
learning-rate sweep, the per-condition table, and the same analysis for
MixVPR.'' The supplement's corresponding section contains the per-condition
table and nothing else: a search of \file{supplementary.tex} for
``learning-rate sweep'' returns no match, and its adaptive-attacker section
does not mention MixVPR at all. Two of the three items a referee is sent to
find are absent. Both exist in the extended evidence report, so the material is
not lost --- the pointer is aimed at the wrong document.

A second pointer has the same problem. The strong-attacker paragraph states
that for the eight-placement comparison against MixVPR, ``Per-placement values
are in the Supplementary Material.'' No supplementary table carries MixVPR
against the eight placements on MSLS: Table~S4 is the three redistribution
controls per backbone, and Table~S5 is the placement-rule study pooled over six
backbones on the proxy corpus. The per-placement MixVPR breakdown the sentence
promises is not there.

This is the same defect class the previous cycle's supplement mismatch
belonged to, reintroduced by its fix: when the superseded adaptive-attacker
table was replaced, the sentence pointing at its contents was not.

\action
Repoint both sentences at the document that actually holds the material, or
move the material. For the first, either state that the learning-rate sweep and
the MixVPR analysis are in the extended evidence report and keep the
per-condition table reference on the supplement, or add both to the supplement
--- which the six-page ceiling makes the harder option. For the second, either
add a per-placement MixVPR table to the supplement, or say that the pooled
values are in Table~S5 and the per-placement values in the extended report,
whichever is true.

\acceptance
Every ``Supplementary Material'' pointer in the manuscript names material a
reader can find in the supplement, and every pointer to the extended report
names material that is in the extended report. A grep of the manuscript for
such pointers, checked one by one, resolves in every case.

\finding{R2}{Major}{A hardcoded cross-document table number now resolves to the
wrong table}
\evidence
The budget-sweep paragraph directs the reader to ``Supplementary Material,
Fig.~S1 and Table~S7.'' In the current supplement the budget sweep is
\textbf{Table~S6} (``Placement $\Delta$ versus uniform across budgets'');
\textbf{Table~S7} is ``Same-image utility on 200 indexed VOC segmentation
images and 200 COCO detection images'' --- an unrelated table in a different
section. Fig.~S1 is correct. The numbering shifted when two supplementary
tables were merged into one, and the hardcoded reference did not move with it.

The manuscript's own source carries a comment forbidding cross-document
\file{\textbackslash ref} into the supplement, precisely because LaTeX cannot
resolve one. The consequence is that this class of reference is invisible to
every automatic check the project runs: the build reports zero undefined
references, and the claim auditor verifies values rather than pointers, so
nothing in the toolchain can see that S7 is wrong.

\action
Correct the number to Table~S6. Then remove the class of defect rather than
this instance of it: either replace the hardcoded numbers with a description
that cannot go stale (``the budget-sweep table and figure in the Supplementary
Material''), or add a build-time check that extracts every \texttt{S$n$}
reference from the manuscript and confirms that the supplement's $n$th table or
figure carries the caption the manuscript expects.

\acceptance
Every numbered cross-document reference resolves to the intended float, and a
mechanical check exists that would catch the next renumbering.

\finding{R3}{Major}{A significance marker sits on a pooled cell that was never
tested}
\evidence
Table~I marks the \emph{learned support} row on the selective-map checkpoint as
$+0.039^{\ast}$, and its caption defines $\ast$ as ``the one cell significant
with the query as the unit of inference.'' The value $+0.039$ is the mean
paired difference pooled over six backbones, three seeds and both benchmark
scales. The test that produced the star is not a test of that pooled value: the
body states it is the VGG16 sub-cell, $+0.194$, 95\% CI $[0.028,0.361]$,
$p=0.05$, over 12 queries --- one cell among 98 comparisons in a family the
paper itself declares exploratory and uncorrected, at a $p$ equal to the
threshold, on the smallest query count anywhere in the paper.

A reader of Table~I alone --- which is how tables are read --- will conclude
that a pooled difference of $+0.039$ is statistically significant. It is not,
and the paper does not claim it is anywhere in the prose. The supplement's
Table~S5, reporting the same $+0.039$, carries no star, so the two tables
disagree about the same number.

This matters more than a typographical quibble because this is the paper's only
positive finding against the mechanism it audits, and the previous cycle
already softened the Contributions and Conclusion wording for exactly this
cell. The table is where the softening did not reach.

\action
Move the marker to where the test lives. Either drop the star from the pooled
cell and report the VGG16 result only in the body where its $n$, its interval
and its exploratory status are visible; or split the row so the marked quantity
is the tested quantity; or redefine $\ast$ in the caption as ``contains a
backbone sub-cell significant at $\alpha=0.05$ uncorrected, $n=12$'' and say
which backbone. The first is preferable and costs nothing.

\acceptance
No marker in either document asserts significance for a quantity that was not
the object of the test, and Table~I and Table~S5 agree about the status of the
$+0.039$ cell.

\finding{R4}{Major}{The proxy-versus-real calibration sentence quotes two
numbers that appear in no table, and attaches one to the wrong table}
\evidence
Both documents carry the sentence that calibrates the paper's most important
scope limitation --- that the effect on real place data is far smaller than on
the mined proxy. The manuscript: ``On real place data the protective effect is
far smaller than on the proxy (Top-1 drop $0.018$ versus $0.111$).'' The
supplement, introducing the wide 8-city table: ``The protective effect is
directionally consistent with the mined proxy and far smaller, a Top-1 drop of
$0.018$ against $0.111$.''

Neither number appears in any table in either document. Recomputed from the
released exports, the wide 8-city table the supplement's sentence introduces
has a primary-manifest mean drop of $0.0186$ (which prints as $0.019$) and a
ResNet18 cell of $0.0142$; the value $0.018$ matches the \emph{two-city}
ResNet18 cell ($0.0183$) exactly. The sentence therefore attaches a two-city
number to the eight-city table, or else quotes a mean without saying it is one.
The comparator $0.111$ is not derivable from any table in either document at
all; the proxy tables report Top-1 levels, not this drop.

Because the sentence is the paper's own statement of how much weaker the real
result is, a referee who tries to check it --- and this is precisely the
sentence a sceptical referee checks --- cannot.

\action
State what the two numbers are and where they come from. Give each one a unit
and a scope: which manifest, which backbone or which mean over which cells, and
which benchmark for the proxy figure. If $0.018$ is a mean over the primary
manifest's six backbones, say so and print $0.019$; if it is the ResNet18 cell,
say which table it is in. Add the proxy drop to a table, or cite the extended
report for it by name. Register both in the released verification tool so the
next edit cannot silently break them.

\acceptance
Every number in the proxy-versus-real comparison names its manifest, its
backbone or its aggregation, and can be located in a table in one of the
released documents.

\finding{R5}{Major}{The real-world evidence rests on a single retrieval dataset}
\evidence
Every real-place result in the paper --- the allocation null, the
published-model placements, the operator study, the direction transfer to four
attackers, the preprocessing robustness, the adaptive attacker, the serialized
release audit and the joint utility measurement --- is measured on subsets of
one dataset, the Mapillary Street-Level Sequences release. The paper is candid
about the resulting narrowness within that dataset: queries are ``almost
entirely forward-view and daytime with empty season and weather fields'', so
nothing here is a cross-condition robustness claim.

For a negative result whose thesis is that a design axis does not work, single-
dataset evidence is the most natural attack surface a referee has. The claim is
not that placement fails on MSLS; it is that placement fails in the
low-distortion regime these mechanisms target. One dataset, one capture
modality, and one place-label convention cannot separate those.

The bibliography already contains two standard alternatives that are cited
nowhere in either document: Tokyo 24/7~\cite{torii201524}, which is the
canonical day--night and viewpoint-variation benchmark and would directly test
the condition narrowness the paper concedes, and Oxford
RobotCar~\cite{maddern2017robotcar}, which supplies season and weather
variation. Their presence in the file suggests the authors considered them.

\action
Run the two comparisons the thesis rests on --- the eight-placement
energy-matched study and the direction-versus-isotropic contrast at the
operating point --- on one further place-labelled dataset, under the existing
protocol and gates, and report them with the same intervals. One backbone and
one budget is enough; the claim being tested is whether the null and the
direction effect survive a change of dataset, not a full replication. If that
cannot be done, state in the scope paragraph and the conclusion that every
real-place result is from one dataset under one capture condition, and confine
the regime claim accordingly.

\acceptance
Either a second place-labelled dataset carries the allocation null and the
direction contrast with intervals, or every statement about the regime rather
than the benchmark is explicitly restricted to MSLS under daytime forward-view
capture.

\finding{R6}{Moderate}{The allocation null is established only against
hand-specified placements and the authors' own two checkpoints}
\evidence
The placements tested are: the testbed's own learned map on two checkpoints,
uniform, spectral-residual saliency, Sobel edge magnitude, a centre-bias prior,
a fixed random field, a score-gradient map and its inverse, and three
off-the-shelf segmentation models used as background/foreground priors. Of
these, exactly one family is a \emph{learned sensitivity map produced by a
sanitizer}, and both of its checkpoints are the authors' own --- one of which
is degenerate, which the paper reports and correctly treats as a diagnostic
rather than a result.

The three published-model placements are the strongest external evidence in the
section, and the paper is right to add them, but they are segmentation models
repurposed as placement priors. They were not trained to place a privacy
budget, and no independently trained selective sanitizer is evaluated. The
conclusion that ``no placement rule beats uniform'' is therefore established
over a set of priors the authors specified plus their own mechanism, which is a
weaker statement than the framing implies.

\action
Either evaluate one third-party selective sanitizer with a released checkpoint
under the protocol --- the mask-guided baseline is already implemented and run,
so extending the same treatment to one more released mechanism is incremental
--- or state the limitation where the null is asserted: that the tested
placement set comprises hand-specified priors, gradient maps, published
segmentation priors, and this paper's own two checkpoints, and that no
independently trained sanitizer map was available to test.

\acceptance
The null's scope sentence names the class of placements it covers, and does not
generalise beyond it without evidence.

\finding{R7}{Moderate}{The conclusion's ``attainable'' is not reconciled with
the frontier's own verdict on the same operating point}
\evidence
The conclusion states: ``The practical reading is not that visual location
privacy is unattainable at low-distortion budgets. It is attainable, on an axis
this mechanism family does not optimise \dots\ a direction optimised against
three surrogate embedders from the released frame alone cuts a held-out
attacker's Top-1 from $0.197$ to $0.032$ at the same delivered distortion.''

The frontier section, on the same operating point, states that this paper's
operating point costs $0.110$ mIoU, ``more than twice even the generous
tolerance,'' and that at the strict tolerance the operating point's direction
arm is settled against. The conclusion's third qualification does say direction
is not free and costs more utility than isotropic noise at matched distortion,
so the information is present --- but it arrives after the claim, in weaker
language, and never states that the cited operating point is one the paper's own
utility analysis rejects at both tolerances it reports.

``Attainable'' is true at a fixed \emph{distortion} budget and false at either
\emph{utility} budget the paper motivates. A referee reading the conclusion
first, as many do, will take away the stronger reading.

\action
Qualify the claim in the sentence that makes it. For example: attainable at
matched distortion, at a utility cost that exceeds both tolerances this paper
motivates --- which is why the recommendation is to compare directional designs
at matched utility rather than matched distortion. This is a one-clause change
and it strengthens the paper's own argument for its recommendation.

\acceptance
No sentence asserting attainability is separated from the utility verdict on
the same operating point.

\finding{R8}{Moderate}{The tolerance calibration's reference class is narrow
and measured off-design}
\evidence
The declared tolerance now has a measured scale, which is the right answer to
the previous cycle's objection, and the two-tolerance presentation is
well-judged. The reference class deserves one paragraph of scrutiny it does not
yet receive. The six segmenters are all torchvision releases with
COCO-with-VOC-labels weights: two DeepLabV3 ResNet variants, two MobileNetV3
variants and two FCN ResNet variants. Three of the six pairs share a trunk with
another member, so the spread is measured over a more homogeneous family than
``published segmenters'' suggests --- one library, one training recipe, one
label convention.

They are also scored at the utility pipeline's $192\times320$ working
resolution, far below the resolution these models were trained and released
for. That is the correct choice for comparability with the tolerance, and the
paper is right to gate on reproducing the exported value; but it means the
measured spread is a spread between off-design operating points, which may
compress or inflate the gaps relative to the spread a practitioner would see.

\action
Add two sentences to the supplement's calibration paragraph: name the reference
class as one library's released weights rather than the segmentation literature,
note the trunk sharing, and state that the spread is measured at the pipeline's
working resolution rather than at each model's native one. If it is cheap, also
report the same six models' spread at native resolution as a sensitivity check
--- the earlier measurement in this project put DeepLabV3-ResNet50 at $0.782$
there against $0.697$ at working resolution, so the two scales are materially
different and a reader will want to know which one anchors the tolerance.

\acceptance
The calibration paragraph states the reference class, its homogeneity, and the
resolution at which the spread was measured.

\finding{R9}{Minor}{Abstract and conclusion enumerate different qualification
sets, and sixteen bibliography entries are uncited}
\evidence
The abstract announces ``Four qualifications follow'' and lists Top-5/10
against a strong retriever, attacker-side preprocessing, an adapting attacker,
and downstream utility cost. The conclusion announces ``Three qualifications
travel with it'' and lists preprocessing, the adapting attacker, and utility
cost, omitting Top-5/10. Each count is correct for its own list, but a reader
comparing the two sees a discrepancy and has to work out that the lists differ
rather than the count being wrong.

Separately, 16 of the 63 bibliography entries are cited nowhere in either
document. Uncited entries do not print and are not a compliance defect, but two
of them are the datasets R5 asks for, and one is a visual place recognition
survey~\cite{lowry2016visual} that would source the paper's uncited
characterisation of what the field does --- ``the field learns and ablates the
first while inheriting the second, in practice additive isotropic Gaussian
noise'' --- which is the premise the whole paper rests on and currently carries
no citation. A cited survey~\cite{masone2021survey} appears once, for
descriptors, not for this claim.

\action
Make the two qualification lists identical, or say in the conclusion that it
repeats three of the four. Cite a survey for the characterisation of the
field's default operator, or soften it to what the paper's own related-work
section demonstrates. Prune the uncited entries or put them to use.

\acceptance
The abstract's and conclusion's qualification lists agree; the premise about
the field's inherited operator carries a citation or is narrowed to the cited
works.

\finding{R10}{Minor}{One reproducibility sentence is broader than what the
released check covers}
\evidence
The supplement states that ``Recomputing every table in this document from the
released per-query exports needs no GPU and no imagery.'' That is accurate for
tables. It sits, however, immediately after the paragraph that points at the
released code, and a reader may take it as a guarantee over the paper's
numbers generally. Numbers quoted only in prose are outside it --- the
proxy-versus-real figures of R4, the KITTI-360 attribution values (Spearman
$-0.00024$, top-10 overlap $0.0497$, $0.0947$ of occlusion-reference energy),
and the interval endpoints quoted in the text for the third and fourth
attackers, among others.

\action
Narrow the sentence to what it covers and say what it does not: every table in
this document recomputes from the released exports without a GPU; figures
quoted only in the text are covered where the table they summarise is
reproduced, and the extended report identifies the rest.

\acceptance
The scope sentence distinguishes tabulated numbers from prose-only numbers.

\section{Presentation notes not tied to a finding}
\begin{itemize}
\item The operator table reports $+0.0100$ at the operating point while the
text says no difference ``exceeds $0.01$ in magnitude''. True as written, but a
reader checking it against the table sees equality; ``none exceeds $0.010$''
or ``none is larger than $0.010$'' avoids the second look.
\item The abstract counts ``eight placement rules'' where the MSLS section
reports seven comparisons against the uniform reference, the eighth being
uniform itself. Consistent on inspection, but the two counts invite a
reconciliation the reader has to do.
\item Table~I's caption defines $\ast$ before the body explains what was
tested; if R3 is resolved by redefining rather than removing the marker, the
caption should be self-contained.
\item The threat-model paragraph is a single unwrapped source line of roughly
900 characters. This does not affect the PDF, but it makes diffs of that
paragraph unreadable, and this manuscript's history shows that hand edits to
long lines are where its transcription defects have come from.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final assessment}

The science is in good order and the package is, with the exceptions below, one
of the more carefully instrumented submissions a referee is likely to see at
this venue: a reusable matched-distortion audit, a bounded negative result about
spatial allocation in the regime these mechanisms target, a direction result
whose limits are reported rather than hidden, and a utility analysis honest
enough to reject the paper's own operating point at the tolerance it motivates.
The previous cycle's four obstacles --- access, document synchrony,
architectural accuracy, and single-seed evidence --- are genuinely resolved,
and the fixes are visible in the sources rather than asserted.

What holds it at minor revision is that three of the defects found in this
reading were found by following a reference and discovering it did not resolve:
a pointer to a learning-rate sweep and a MixVPR analysis that are not in the
supplement, a table number that now names the wrong table, and a significance
marker on a quantity that was not tested. A fourth, the proxy-versus-real
calibration, quotes two numbers that appear in no table. Individually each is
small. Together they say something a referee will act on: that this manuscript's
automatic checks verify values but not references, and that the places where a
human must check by hand are where its errors now live. The two scientific
items --- one retrieval dataset, and a placement set that contains no
independently trained sanitizer --- are limitations rather than errors, and can
be resolved by evidence or by a sentence.

All findings above are open at the end of this review; none is claimed to have
been addressed by writing this document.
