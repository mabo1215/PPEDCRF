\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This is a fresh review of the current submission package against the IEEE TIFS
author requirements and the standards a TIFS referee would apply. It does not
use any previous review file, the project's progress notes, or any earlier
completion judgment as input. Every statement was checked against the sources,
the rendered PDFs, and --- where a claim needed testing --- against the
released per-query exports and manifest metadata, recomputed independently.

The reviewed main source is \file{paper/main.tex}, SHA-256 prefix
\file{32903e34a6742ab8}, 13 pages. The supplement,
\file{paper/supplementary.tex}, prefix \file{7dc4fdb4cee53734}, is six pages
and carries ten tables. The bibliography holds 47 entries, all cited.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: minor revision.} The package is in good
condition and the previous cycle's corrections hold: the second dataset's
direction contrast now prints both the query-level and the place-clustered
interval and the manuscript no longer claims an effect the clustered interval
does not separate; all thirteen placement conditions are reported; the place
count appears beside the query count.

This review's findings concentrate on the same addition, because it is the
newest evidence and it is now load-bearing. \textbf{The second dataset's null is
reported without a clean baseline or any demonstrated-power control.} Every
null in the paper on the first dataset is anchored by a clean Top-1 and by a
white-box arm that collapses it, so the reader can see the measurement could
have detected something. On KITTI-360 neither is reported: the exports contain
only perturbed arms, no clean condition appears anywhere in either document,
and the one arm that could serve as a positive control --- direction --- is
precisely the one the last revision correctly marked as not separated under the
prescribed unit. As it stands, ``the null replicates on a second dataset''
rests on a benchmark whose sensitivity has not been shown.

Two further findings sit beside it. The new benchmark's place label is
disclosed asymmetrically: the supplement reports that $100\%$ of positives
within $25$\,m carry the query's label, and does not report that $1{,}211$ of
the $1{,}816$ labelled positives --- two thirds --- lie \emph{beyond} that
radius. And after four tables were moved to the extended report over the last
two cycles, the supplement's opening paragraph no longer describes its own
contents.

None of this touches the first dataset's results, which remain well defended.
The corrections are cheap: one clean pass on an existing manifest, one number
printed beside another, and one paragraph rewritten.

\subsection{Summary of the work}
The paper asks whether visual location-privacy mechanisms buy protection
through \emph{where} a bounded distortion budget is spent or through \emph{where
the perturbation points}, and answers with a protocol that holds delivered
distortion fixed while varying one axis at a time. On a place-labelled MSLS
subset, against a weak and a strong retriever, no placement rule beats uniform
spreading at the low-distortion operating point --- eight placement rules,
three published-model placements, two gradient-guided placements and four
operators, every difference within $\pm0.012$ Top-1 --- while a
surrogate-optimised direction transfers to four held-out attackers at the same
distortion. The paper decomposes the direction effect, measures its downstream
cost against two motivated tolerances, repeats the utility measurement on the
frames retrieval is scored on, audits the serialized release, evaluates two
adaptive attackers, offers a conditional first-order account checked against a
controlled model with a known Jacobian, and repeats both central contrasts on a
second dataset built from KITTI-360 revisits.

\subsection{Strengths worth preserving}
\begin{itemize}
\item The allocation null is defended on two datasets, two mechanism
checkpoints, six attacker backbones, four operators and three published-model
priors, and it survives recomputation under a clustered unit of inference on
both. That is a well-supported negative result.
\item The previous cycle's self-correction is exemplary and should be kept
visible: a positive claim was withdrawn when the prescribed unit of inference
did not support it, with the point estimate left unchanged and both intervals
printed. Papers rarely show this.
\item The instrumentation continues to earn its keep: 388 recomputed claims, a
cross-document reference checker, and a table generator that now fails if a
condition present in an export is missing from the table it renders.
\item Inference is declared and followed: query as the unit with seeds averaged
within it, a named primary family of four contrasts Holm-corrected, everything
else labelled exploratory, and a $\pm0.01$ equivalence margin fixed before
testing with ``no benefit detected'' distinguished from ``negligible''.
\item The KITTI-360 construction records the two approaches that failed, which
is what lets a reader judge whether the one that worked was a reasonable
choice.
\end{itemize}

submissions~\cite{tifsdl} and raises that expectation for negative results

\section{Major findings and required revisions}

\finding{R1}{Major}{The second dataset's null is reported without a clean
baseline or any demonstrated-power control}
\evidence
A null result carries weight only in proportion to the demonstrated sensitivity
of the measurement that produced it. On the first dataset the paper meets that
standard carefully and repeatedly: clean Top-1 is quoted six times
($0.21$ for ResNet18, $0.79$ for MixVPR, $0.867$ in the controlled model), the
mechanism's own effect is shown against it, and a white-box arm collapses Top-1
to $0.000$--$0.055$, which demonstrates the benchmark is attackable and that
the protocol can register a large effect when one exists.

On KITTI-360 none of this is present. The manuscript reports the uniform
reference at $0.1512$ and the isotropic control at $0.1483$, and never states
what an unperturbed query scores. The released exports confirm the gap is in
the data and not merely in the prose: \file{kitti360\_rows/placement.csv}
contains thirteen conditions, all of them perturbed placements, and
\file{direction.csv} contains \file{isotropic} and \file{transfer\_3} only. No
clean or raw condition was run, and no white-box arm was run.

The consequence is specific. A reader cannot tell whether $0.1512$ under
uniform represents a small reduction from a clean $0.16$ or a large one from a
clean $0.60$. If clean Top-1 on this benchmark were already near $0.15$ --- and
a coarse place label over a 2{,}000-image gallery makes that possible --- then
there was little retrieval to destroy, every placement would be
indistinguishable from every other for want of anything to move, and the
replication would demonstrate nothing about allocation. The paper's own
sentence, ``that null replicates on a second place-labelled dataset'', would
then be unsupported in the strong sense a referee reads it.

The one arm that could have served as a positive control is the direction
contrast, and the previous revision correctly established that it is not
separated from zero under the prescribed clustered unit,
$[-0.137,+0.021]$. So the benchmark currently has neither an anchor nor a
demonstrated ability to detect an effect.

\action
Run one clean pass over the same manifest, gallery and seeds --- no
perturbation --- and report clean Top-1 for this benchmark wherever the
perturbed levels are quoted, as is done for every other benchmark in the paper.
This is minutes of work on hardware already used for these runs and needs no
new data. Add a white-box arm on the same manifest if it is equally cheap: it
is the power check the first dataset has and this one lacks, and it would show
the benchmark can register a large effect. Then state the reading honestly: if
clean Top-1 is well above the perturbed levels, the null is anchored and the
replication claim stands; if it is not, say that the second dataset has too
little retrieval to detect allocation effects and confine the replication claim
accordingly.

\acceptance
Clean Top-1 is reported for the second dataset wherever perturbed levels are,
and the replication claim is qualified by whatever that number shows.

\finding{R2}{Major}{The new benchmark's place label is disclosed
asymmetrically}
\evidence
The supplement states that of the gallery frames within $25$\,m of a query,
$100\%$ carry that query's label, and adds that ``the converse is permissive
--- a labelled positive may lie beyond $25$\,m, a place being a road stretch.''
The magnitude of that converse is not given. From the manifest metadata:
$605$ positives lie within $25$\,m and all $605$ share the label, while
$1{,}211$ labelled positives lie beyond it. \textbf{Two thirds of what counts
as a hit on this benchmark is farther from the query than the radius the
agreement figure is computed against.}

Reporting the exact figure in the strict direction and only an adjective in the
permissive one is the kind of asymmetry a referee notices, and it matters here
because it bears directly on R1: a permissive criterion inflates Top-1 for
every condition equally, which is harmless for the paired contrasts the paper
reads, but it also raises the floor and compresses the range in which any
placement effect could show itself.

The manuscript's treatment of the first dataset is the model to follow. There,
the cluster label is audited against the geometry in both directions and both
numbers are printed --- $99.3\%$ agreement at $25$\,m and $77.5\%$ at $10$\,m
--- with the shortfall named rather than characterised.

\action
Print both counts where the $100\%$ is printed: $605$ of $605$ within the
radius carry the label, and $1{,}211$ labelled positives lie beyond it because
a place is a road stretch. One clause. Then say what follows for the reading:
the criterion is exact in the strict direction and permissive in the other, so
absolute Top-1 is an upper bound and only the paired contrasts are
interpretable --- which the supplement already says, but without the evidence
that makes it concrete.

\acceptance
Both directions of the label audit are quantified wherever either is.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The supplement's opening paragraph no longer describes its
own contents}
\evidence
The supplement opens: ``This supplement carries the tables the manuscript's
claims rest on \dots\ Material that supports those tables rather than the
claims --- the matched-PSNR sweep's extensions, the white-box attacker's
five-backbone extension, the sequence-length and proxy50 studies, the
reference-targeted preprocessing table, the KITTI-360 unary diagnostic and the
implementation notes --- is in the extended evidence report.''

Neither sentence is now accurate. Over the last two cycles four tables were
moved from the supplement to the extended report to make room:
\begin{itemize}
\item the per-rule placement study behind the manuscript's Table~I;
\item the two-city E1 multi-backbone table;
\item the white-box per-backbone table;
\item the budget sweep table.
\end{itemize}
The first of these is the detail behind the paper's \emph{central negative
result}. A referee who wants to see the per-rule numbers that Table~I pools ---
the obvious thing to check --- is told by this paragraph that the supplement
carries the tables the claims rest on, finds it does not, and is not told where
it went except by a sentence four pages later. The inventory of what is in the
extended report lists six items and none of the four that were added.

Each individual pointer elsewhere in the supplement is correct: the sections
that lost a table say so and name the extended report, and the extended report
does contain all four, which I verified. The defect is confined to the opening
paragraph, but that paragraph is what tells a reader how to use the two
documents.

\action
Rewrite the opening to say what is where. Something close to: this supplement
carries the tables the manuscript reaches most directly; the per-rule placement
study behind Table~I, the two-city E1 table, the white-box per-backbone table
and the budget sweep are in the extended evidence report, together with the
material that supports those tables rather than the claims. The paragraph is
already the right length; it needs to be true rather than longer.

\acceptance
A reader following the opening paragraph can predict which document holds any
given table.

\finding{R4}{Moderate}{The paper now owns both halves of a methodological
lesson and states only the benign one}
\evidence
The manuscript reports, of the first dataset, that ``clustering the bootstrap
on the 277 places rather than the 400 queries widens intervals by
$1.08$--$1.25\times$ and moves no conclusion \dots\ as expected when $206$ of
the $277$ places contribute a single query.'' On the second dataset, clustering
widens the direction interval by $1.93\times$ and does move the conclusion,
because 227 queries come from 16 places.

Both statements are correct and both are in the paper. What is missing is the
sentence connecting them, which is a genuine methodological contribution the
work has earned: how much clustering costs is governed by queries per place,
it is nearly free when most places contribute one query, and it can reverse a
verdict when they contribute fourteen. A reader currently has to notice the
two numbers pages apart and derive the rule.

\action
Add one sentence where the second dataset's clustered interval is reported,
connecting it to the first dataset's $1.08$--$1.25\times$: the widening scales
with queries per place, $1.44$ there against $14.2$ here, which is why the same
procedure is nearly free on one benchmark and decisive on the other. It costs a
line and converts an apparent inconsistency into a stated result.

\acceptance
The relationship between queries per place and the cost of clustering is stated
once, where both numbers can be seen.

\finding{R5}{Minor}{Two placement-set sizes, reconciled by the reader}
\evidence
The abstract and the first dataset speak of eight energy-matched placement
rules; the second dataset reports thirteen. Both are correct --- the second run
included the two margin-oracle rules and the three published-model priors as
part of the same sweep --- but nothing says so, and a reader who counts is left
to work out whether the sets differ or one is a superset.
\action
One clause where the second dataset's count first appears, naming what the
thirteen contain relative to the eight.
\acceptance
The two counts are reconciled in the text.

\finding{R6}{Minor}{Instrumentation scope, now correctly stated, is worth
keeping correct}
\evidence
The artifact README now states that the checker covers every table cell in both
documents plus registered prose figures, and that roughly a third of the
manuscript's three- and four-decimal literals have no claim. That is accurate
and was a correction made last cycle. The scope will drift again as numbers are
added: the two conditions that went unreported for a cycle were invisible to a
checker that verifies only what is declared, which is why the generator now
reconciles its condition list against the export.
\action
No change now. When R1's clean baseline is added, register it as a claim in the
same pass, so the newest and most load-bearing number on the second dataset is
not the one outside the registry.
\acceptance
Any number added in response to this review is registered when it is printed.

\section{Final assessment}

The paper's central claim is in good shape and its handling of the last
cycle's correction was better than most published work manages: a positive
result was withdrawn when the paper's own unit of inference failed to support
it, with the estimate left standing and both intervals printed. That episode
should survive into the submitted version, because it is evidence about how the
rest of the numbers were treated.

What this review asks for is that the second dataset be held to the same
standard as the first. On MSLS the paper never states a null without also
stating what a clean query scores and showing, with a white-box arm, that the
benchmark can be moved. On KITTI-360 it does both without either. That is not
a claim that the replication is wrong --- the placement differences there are
small and mutually consistent, and the direction point estimate matches MSLS in
sign and size. It is that a referee cannot currently distinguish ``allocation
does nothing on this dataset'' from ``this dataset had nothing to detect'', and
one unperturbed pass over a manifest that already exists would settle it.

The other two findings are disclosure rather than evidence: a label audit
reported in one direction and characterised in the other, and an opening
paragraph that has fallen out of step with a supplement reorganised twice
around it. Both are minutes of work and both remove things a referee would
otherwise have to discover.

All findings above are open at the end of this review; none is claimed to have
been addressed by writing this document.
