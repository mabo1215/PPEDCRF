\section*{Major Findings and Required Revisions}

\subsection*{R1 --- Major (methodology): the paired tests treat seed replicates
of the same query as independent observations}

\textbf{Finding.} \texttt{analyze\_direction\_transfer.py} sets
\texttt{UNIT = ["query\_id", "seed"]} and runs an exact McNemar test over
those units; \texttt{analyze\_eot\_sweep.py} pools by \texttt{(query, seed)}
in the same way. That is where the paper's ``$n=1{,}200$ paired observations''
comes from: 400 queries $\times$ 3 noise seeds. The three seeds of a query
share the frame, the gallery, the place label and the query's intrinsic
difficulty; they differ only in the noise draw (isotropic control) or the
random start (gallery-free direction). A query whose clean margin is small
contributes discordant pairs in the same direction under all three seeds, so
the McNemar count of discordant pairs is inflated by up to a factor of three
relative to the independent evidence, and every reported $p$-value --- $10^{-41}$,
$10^{-26}$, $10^{-8}$ --- is computed on a sample whose effective size is
somewhere between 400 and 1,200.

The manuscript already knows the right unit: the proxy placement study uses
``cluster-robust'' tests, and the E1 real-place analysis uses a
\emph{place-cluster bootstrap} and, on that basis, reports only 5 of 18 cells
as individually significant. The direction, preprocessing, EOT, operator and
MSLS-placement tables do not follow that convention. The inconsistency is
internal, and a referee who reads the code will find it in minutes.

\textbf{Consequences.} The substantive conclusions survive: the placement
nulls become, if anything, more null; the ResNet18 direction effects
($|\Delta|\ge0.09$) are far outside any plausible correction. What is at
risk is the significance status of the marginal cells, all against MixVPR
or at the high budget:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The transfer table: MixVPR 1, 2 and 3 surrogates
  ($\Delta=-0.025$, $-0.028$, $-0.032$; $p\approx10^{-4}$).
  \item The preprocessing/EOT table: MixVPR unhardened JPEG-75 ($-0.0242$)
  and blur ($-0.0317$); the two cells already reported as non-significant
  stay non-significant.
  \item The operator table at MSE 241.5: correlated Gaussian ($+0.0167$,
  $p=0.067$) --- close to the boundary in either direction.
  \item The MixVPR placement paragraph: the oracle's $-0.0117$ at $p=0.039$,
  already discounted for multiplicity.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Required actions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recompute every paired test in the main text and supplement with the
  \emph{query} as the unit of inference. Two acceptable forms, to be used
  consistently: (a) a query-cluster bootstrap 95\% CI for $\Delta$ (resample
  queries with replacement, carrying all three seeds of each), matching the E1
  convention already in the paper; or (b) collapse each query to its
  seed-averaged hit rate under each condition and apply a paired test on the
  400 units (Wilcoxon signed-rank or a sign test), with exact McNemar
  retained only where a single seed is reported. Report both $\Delta$ and the
  CI; drop the raw $p$-values below $10^{-10}$, which communicate nothing.
  \item Replace every ``$n=1{,}200$ paired observations'' with ``400 queries,
  three seeds'' and state the unit once in the Experimental Protocol.
  \item Re-mark the significance indicators in Tables~2--4 and the
  preprocessing table, and re-read every sentence that leans on a marginal
  cell --- in particular ``a property of the objective rather than a shortage
  of data'', which is only defensible once the query-level test has been run.
  \item Data dependency: the per-query exports under
  \texttt{src/outputs/icme2027\_*} are not on the machine that produced this
  review; the analysis needs them mounted or re-fetched. No GPU is required.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R2 --- Major (positioning): the direction axis is an adversarial
perturbation used for privacy, and the paper does not cite that literature}

\textbf{Finding.} The ``direction'' result is a sign-gradient perturbation
optimised against an embedding, transferred through a surrogate ensemble,
hardened by expectation over transformations with a straight-through
backward pass, and evaluated against preprocessing and fine-tuning defenses.
Every one of those components has a named origin that a TIFS reviewer expects
to see, and none appears in the 50-entry bibliography:
\begin{itemize}
  \item adversarial perturbation as a \emph{privacy} mechanism against
  recognition: Oh, Fritz and Schiele (ICCV 2017), Fawkes (Shan et al., USENIX
  Security 2020), LowKey (Cherepanova et al., ICLR 2021);
  \item transferability through ensembles of surrogate models: Liu et al.
  (ICLR 2017), Dong et al. (CVPR 2018);
  \item expectation over transformation and the BPDA identity substitution:
  Athalye, Engstrom, Ilyas and Kwok (ICML 2018); Athalye, Carlini and Wagner
  (ICML 2018). The code docstring names BPDA; the paper does not;
  \item input transformations as a defense --- the exact attacker-side
  operation the preprocessing section tests: Guo et al. (ICLR 2018), Xu et
  al. (NDSS 2018), Dziugaite et al. (2016) on JPEG;
  \item the methodology standard for adaptive attacks that the
  fine-tuning section is implicitly measured against: Tram\`er et al.
  (NeurIPS 2020).
\end{itemize}
The paper cites Goodfellow et al. and Madry et al. once, in a sentence about
DP, which is not where the reader needs them.

Without this framing, the contribution reads as though the paper discovered
that adversarial perturbations transfer, and a reviewer will say so. With it,
the contribution becomes sharper and more defensible: the novelty is not the
perturbation but the \emph{controlled comparison} --- the same budget, the
same frames, the same delivered distortion, spent on allocation versus on
direction --- and the finding that the design variable this literature
optimises is inert exactly where it operates. That is a claim the
adversarial-ML literature does not make, and it is the claim the paper should
be seen to be making.

\textbf{Required actions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Add a Related-Work paragraph, ``Adversarial perturbation as a privacy
  mechanism'' (about 150 words), covering the five groups above and ending on
  the sentence that distinguishes this paper: those works optimise a
  perturbation against a recognizer; none asks whether the spatial allocation
  the location-privacy literature learns is doing any work, which is the
  question the protocol answers.
  \item Cite at the point of use: FGSM/PGD where the sign-gradient
  perturbation is introduced; Liu et al.\ where the surrogate ensemble is;
  Athalye et al.\ (both) where EOT and the identity backward pass are
  described; Guo et al.\ and Xu et al.\ at the head of the preprocessing
  section; Tram\`er et al.\ at the head of the adaptive-attacker section.
  \item One sentence in Contributions repositioning the third bullet
  accordingly. Add the entries to \texttt{ref.bib}. Text only; budget about
  0.3 column, to be recovered under R9.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R3 --- Major (evaluation): the EOT-hardened direction is
evaluated only against the transforms it was optimised over}

\textbf{Finding.} \texttt{eval/sanitizers.py} defines exactly five
attacker-side operators: \texttt{none}, \texttt{jpeg75}, \texttt{jpeg50},
\texttt{blur} ($\sigma=1$) and \texttt{denoise}. The hardened direction is
optimised in expectation over the last four and evaluated against the same
four. The paper says an attacker ``is free to choose a fifth'' and then does
not try one. This is the standard overfitting risk of EOT and the first thing
an adversarial-ML reviewer will ask for. The paper's own honesty about the
unhardened direction's failures makes the omission more visible, not less.

\textbf{Required action (D6).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Add held-out attacker-side transforms to \texttt{eval/sanitizers.py}:
  JPEG at quality 30; a $3\times3$ median filter; bilinear downscale to half
  resolution and back; Gaussian blur at $\sigma=2$; bit-depth reduction to 4
  bits; and a randomised composition that applies one uniformly chosen
  operator from the full set per query. Include one \emph{in-family}
  parameter not trained on (JPEG-60) to show interpolation as well as
  extrapolation.
  \item Extend \texttt{run\_direction\_transfer\_study.py} so that one
  optimised perturbation is released once at the target MSE and then embedded
  under a \emph{list} of evaluation sanitizers, instead of re-optimising per
  sanitizer. The hardened direction then costs one optimisation pass per
  (backbone, seed), and every held-out transform is an evaluation-only
  extension of the same run.
  \item Run the hardened and unhardened gallery-free directions against the
  held-out set on both backbones, three seeds, at delivered MSE $15.68$, with
  the isotropic control under the same transform as the paired reference.
  Report as a second block of the preprocessing table or a compact new one,
  with query-level inference per R1.
  \item Either outcome is publishable. If hardening generalises, the
  ``design variable'' claim gains the evidence it currently lacks; if it does
  not, the paper reports the boundary of the axis, which is consistent with
  its framing throughout.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R4 --- Moderate (evaluation): the utility cost of the hardened
direction is not measured}

\textbf{Finding.} The downstream-utility audit (per-image AP@50 and IoU
against the isotropic control at identical delivered MSE) was run on the
\emph{unhardened} surrogate direction. The paper now leads with the hardened
variant --- it is the bold column of the preprocessing table, and ``hardening
also helps when the attacker does nothing'' --- yet its utility cost is
unreported. Optimising over blur and JPEG typically pushes perturbation energy
toward lower spatial frequencies, which a detector may pay for differently. As
it stands, the recommendation ``compare directional designs at matched
utility'' is made about a variant whose utility the paper has not measured.

\textbf{Required action (D7).} Run \texttt{evaluate\_direction\_utility.py}
with the EOT objective (same four transforms, same surrogates, same delivered
MSE) on the same 200-image VOC and 200-image COCO manifests, three
independent runs, and add a ``Hardened direction'' row to the supplementary
utility table with the paired comparison against both the isotropic control
and the unhardened direction. One sentence in \S\,The Other Axis. This is
cheap --- 400 images, one perturbation each --- and fits a single consumer GPU.

\subsection*{R5 --- Major (presentation): a 13-page TIFS paper with no figures}

\textbf{Finding.} \texttt{main.tex} contains no active
\texttt{\textbackslash includegraphics}; the two figure environments it
carries are inside \texttt{comment} blocks, and the supplement has no figures
either. Every result is a table or a paragraph of numbers. Four results are
sweeps or grids that prose handles badly: the budget dependence across
$\sigma_0\in\{4,8,16,32,50\}$ on two checkpoints (currently a dense paragraph
with three numbers pulled out of a 56-comparison sweep); the surrogate-count
ablation on two attackers; the ten-cell preprocessing grid in two hardening
states; and the central ``what a budget buys'' comparison. A TIFS reader expects
to see the protocol and to see the headline result; here both must be
reconstructed from tables. This is also the single largest reason the paper
reads as harder than it is.

\textbf{Required actions.} All four figures below can be produced from
numbers already in the two \texttt{.tex} files; no experiment is required.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{F1, protocol schematic} (one column): the same frame and the
  same delivered distortion spent four ways --- the mechanism's own placement,
  an energy-matched redistribution, a different operator, and a direction ---
  feeding the same attacker. This is the figure that makes the three axes
  legible before any number appears.
  \item \textbf{F2, budget dependence} (one column): $\Delta$ versus uniform
  against $\sigma_0$ for each placement rule on both checkpoints, from the
  supplement's Budget Dependence table, with the operating point marked and
  the two significant edge-placement cells annotated. Replaces most of the
  ``Where placement does begin to matter'' paragraph.
  \item \textbf{F3, preprocessing and hardening} (one column, two panels):
  grouped bars of $\Delta$ per transform, unhardened against hardened, one
  panel per attacker, with the two non-significant cells marked. The table can
  then shrink to the hardened block or move to the supplement.
  \item \textbf{F4, what a budget buys} (optional, one column): Top-1 against
  delivered distortion, allocation conditions as one cluster near $1\times$ and
  $15.4\times$, direction at $1.22\times$.
\end{enumerate}
Page budget: the main text is at exactly 13 pages, so each figure must be paid
for. The sources are identified under R9 and amount to roughly 1.5 columns,
enough for F1--F3.

\subsection*{R7 --- Moderate (reproducibility): the direction optimiser's
hyperparameters are not stated, and two white-box numbers come from two
different optimisers}

\textbf{Finding.} The main text never states how the direction is computed
beyond ``sign-gradient''. From the code and the recorded runs: 20 sign-gradient
steps of size 1 pixel under an $\ell_\infty$ bound of 16, then \emph{rescaled
by a scalar} through the pixel clamp by bisection until the released frame
delivers exactly the target MSE ($15.68$); a uniform random start of $\pm1$
pixel for the gallery-free objective, without which that objective sits at its
own stationary point and leaves a third of MixVPR queries unperturbed (a bug
the repository found and fixed, and which the paper should disclose in one
sentence, because any re-implementer will hit it); and, under EOT, two
transforms sampled per step with the identity substituted on the backward
pass. Separately, the white-box figure in the Direction section ($0.0000$ at
MSE $19.19$, $1.22\times$) comes from the benchmark script's attacker-aware
variant at $\ell_\infty=8$ without MSE rescaling, while the white-box row of
the transfer table ($0.0033$ at MSE $15.68$) comes from the transfer script;
the two numbers are both correct and the reader is not told why they differ.

\textbf{Required actions.} One paragraph in the Experimental Protocol or at
the head of \S\,The Other Axis giving the optimiser, the bound, the rescaling
rule, the random start and its reason, and the EOT sampling; one clause
bridging the two white-box numbers. Restore the code URL in the abstract or
the first footnote --- review is single-anonymized and SPS encourages it ---
and name the commit or tag the numbers were produced from.

\section*{Minimum Revision Package}

A submission-ready manuscript requires, at minimum: R1 recomputed and every
significance indicator re-marked; R6 satisfied; R2's paragraph and citations
in place; at least F1 and F3 from R5; and R7's protocol paragraph. R3 and R4

decision.

\section*{Final Assessment}

The paper's argument is now complete: it locates the leverage, shows what the
defender must hold to reach it (nothing beyond the frame), what an adversary
who preprocesses or adapts can do about it, and what it costs the task. What
stands between it and a TIFS acceptance is not another experiment on the
science but the standard of evidence around it: tests computed on the right
unit, the adversarial-ML lineage acknowledged so the novelty is the
comparison rather than the perturbation, one held-out check on the defense
the paper recommends, and a presentation --- abstract, index terms, figures ---
that lets a reviewer see the result before reconstructing it from tables.
Sequencing matters in one respect: the figures cost pages, the compression
funds them, and both should be done together against the rendered PDF.
