\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This is a fresh, manuscript-based review of the current submission package against IEEE TIFS standards. It replaces the previous contents of this review file. The requested deliverable is the review itself; the recommendations below have \emph{not} been implemented in the manuscript, and proposed experiments have \emph{not} been represented as completed work.

The reviewed main source is \file{paper/main.tex}, last modified at 15:31 on 8 September 2026 local time, with SHA-256 prefix \file{c5be6a76dd961ba1}. Its PDF has 13 pages. The reviewed \file{paper/supplementary.tex}, modified at 15:26, has SHA-256 prefix \file{1833bbda9add3c61}; its PDF has four pages. The bibliography is \file{paper/ref.bib}; the operative generated tables are \file{paper/generated/tab_transfer.tex} and \file{paper/generated/tab_sanitize.tex}. These are the repository's actual filenames; there is no need to invent missing \file{appendix.tex} or \file{references.bib} files. \file{USAGE_codex.md} was unavailable, so the user's explicit TIFS target governs this review.

The assessment includes all pages of the main and supplementary PDFs, their sources, relevant experiment and analysis code, available D6 per-query exports, and official venue guidance accessed on the review date. Earlier manuscript snapshots and earlier completion judgments are not the basis for the verdict. In particular, the current abstract is approximately 245 whitespace-delimited source words, the supplement has the correct title, adversarial-privacy/EOT/BPDA references are present, the main transfer table uses query-level inference, and eight held-out preprocessing conditions have been added. It would be incorrect to report these improvements as still missing.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: not ready for TIFS submission in its current form; major scientific revision is required.} If assessed as a submitted paper, I would recommend rejection with encouragement to submit a substantially revised study, rather than acceptance subject to presentation changes. This is an independent scholarly judgment, not an editorial decision or a prediction of acceptance probability.

The subject fits TIFS: it concerns inference of private location information from released visual data. The potentially publishable contribution is a reproducible audit protocol that separates distortion, spatial weighting, and adversarial optimization under a clearly delimited retrieval threat. However, the central mechanistic explanation is mathematically overclaimed, directly relevant prior work is missing, negative results are repeatedly interpreted as equivalence, and the latest numerical tables disagree with the abstract and discussion. These issues affect the scientific argument, not merely its packaging.

\subsection{Summary of the work}
The paper studies a DCRF-inspired image sanitizer and controls that redistribute its perturbation weights while preserving nominal energy. It discovers that one learned support map is nearly constant, compares additional spatial rules and perturbation operators, and contrasts these with surrogate-optimized adversarial perturbations. On a custom MSLS subset, directional perturbations reduce retrieval accuracy more than the tested spatial reallocations at a low-distortion operating point. The study also examines downstream utility, preprocessing, EOT hardening, restricted attacker fine-tuning, and a synthetic model with a known Jacobian.

\subsection{Strengths worth preserving}
\begin{itemize}
\item The constant-map diagnostic is concrete and valuable: a plausible privacy--quality curve does not establish that a learned spatial map contributes anything.
\item Actual place-labeled MSLS experiments complement the explicitly labeled synthetic-identity proxy, and a dedicated VPR attacker, MixVPR, is included rather than relying solely on a weak classification backbone.
\item Delivered-MSE matching in the directional/operator experiments is an important improvement over comparing nominal noise scales. Independent inspection of the D6 exports confirms a very tight delivered-MSE range.
\item The paper reports unfavorable outcomes, including high-budget placement benefits, strong-attacker residual retrieval, utility losses, and failed attacker-training recipes.
\item Query-level confidence intervals, gallery-free optimization with a random start, EOT, and held-out transforms strengthen the current version. Their presence must be acknowledged before requesting further robustness work.
\item The manuscript explicitly disclaims formal differential privacy. A new DP proof is not a necessary revision unless the authors choose to change the claim.
\end{itemize}

\finding{R2}{Critical}{The theoretical allocation-versus-direction explanation is not valid as stated}
\evidence
Section III-H is titled ``Why Allocation Cannot Decide a Ranking.'' The introduction, contributions, and conclusion similarly say allocation determines displacement magnitude but cannot determine ranking. Equation (7), however, concerns only the \emph{expected squared embedding displacement}; it cannot establish this conclusion.

Let $\delta=D_w\varepsilon$, where $D_w=\operatorname{diag}(w)$ and $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I)$. Under local linearization,
\begin{align}
\Delta f &\approx JD_w\varepsilon,\\
\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta f\mid x,w)&=\sigma^2JD_w^2J^\top,\label{eq:review_cov}\\
\mathbb E\|\Delta f\|_2^2&\approx\sigma^2\sum_i w_i^2\|J_i\|_2^2.
\end{align}
Redistributing the weights generally changes the covariance orientation as well as its trace. Zero mean is not isotropy, and neither implies invariance of rank-crossing probability.

For a locally differentiable positive-versus-negative margin $m(x)$, with the relevant competitors fixed locally and $a=\nabla_xm(x)$,
\begin{equation}
m(x+\delta)\approx m(x)+a^\top\delta,
\qquad
v(w)=\sigma^2\sum_i a_i^2w_i^2.
\end{equation}
For a clean positive margin and $v(w)>0$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr\{m(x+\delta)<0\}\approx
\Phi\!\left(-\frac{m(x)}{\sqrt{v(w)}}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus spatial allocation can change a ranking probability even under the paper's own first-order assumptions. A two-coordinate counterexample suffices: for $a=(1,0)$ and $\sum_iw_i^2=E$, placing all energy on coordinate 1 gives variance $\sigma^2E$, while placing it on coordinate 2 gives zero margin variance. The empirical null does not contradict this calculation; it simply requires a more specific explanation.

\action
Retain the displacement identity but replace the claimed impossibility with a conditional analysis of margin distributions and covariance. State assumptions about local competitors, clipping, normalized embeddings, and higher-order errors. Treat the observed small placement effects as empirical observations on specified models and budgets. Change the subsection title and remove universal statements from the abstract, introduction, and conclusion.
\acceptance
The revised analysis predicts a quantity linked to retrieval, admits counterexamples, and is checked against measured margin changes. No conclusion follows from the trace identity alone that requires the full covariance or ranking distribution.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The gradient ``oracles'' and synthetic validation do not establish an optimal bound or a causal explanation}
\evidence
Section III-H identifies the measured top-decile concentration of 67.7\% with Jacobian-column norms. In \file{src/scripts/run_placement_rule_study.py}, however, \file{attacker_gradient_map} computes the gradient of a scalar positive-gallery cosine score and sums absolute RGB gradients. This is not $\|J_i\|_2$ for the full embedding Jacobian. The margin-gradient map is closer to the ranking objective but is still a heuristic spatial weighting.

Under the stated constraint $\sum_iw_i^2=E$, maximizing a linear form in $w_i^2$ allocates energy to the largest coefficient, unless support, smoothness, or amplitude restrictions are also imposed. Renormalizing a gradient-proportional map does not solve that optimum. The terms ``oracle,'' ``strictly correct,'' and ``bound'' therefore exceed what is shown.

The synthetic study itself reports substantial placement benefits and acknowledges a prediction near $-0.15$ where the real-image observation is approximately $-0.0008$. That discrepancy is evidence that the proposed mechanism is incomplete, not evidence that estimation error explains it. A nonlinear synthetic example also cannot rule out linearization error for the real networks. A near-zero clean retrieval rate creates a floor that can suppress absolute improvements without revealing the underlying mechanism.

\action
Rename the existing rules as positive-score-gradient and margin-gradient placements. Either implement a clearly constrained allocation optimum or explicitly disclaim optimality. Estimate actual Jacobian-column norms on a tractable subset, or consistently analyze scalar margin gradients instead. Compare predicted and observed margin variance, rank flips, clipping, and local approximation error; report the unexplained synthetic-to-real gap as such.
\acceptance
Every sensitivity statistic names the quantity actually measured. Any optimality/bound claim has a defined feasible set and proof or optimization certificate. The controlled study is presented as a diagnostic model with limited external validity.

\finding{R4}{Critical}{Directly relevant prior work challenges the novelty narrative}
\evidence
The revised related-work paragraph correctly credits adversarial privacy, surrogate transfer, EOT, and BPDA. Nevertheless, the abstract and several sections still assert that the location-privacy literature does not optimize perturbation direction and that additive isotropic Gaussian noise is almost universal.

Le et al., \emph{Rethinking Adversarial Examples for Location Privacy Protection}, already combine spatial masks with multi-model projected-gradient perturbations and evaluate transfer to an unseen recognizer. Their CAM-based and human-vision-based masks directly connect allocation and adversarial direction~\cite{le2022}. Their task uses scene/landmark recognition rather than the present gallery-ranking protocol, so the results are not interchangeable; the conceptual overlap still requires explicit treatment.

GeoShield additionally studies geolocation privacy through adversarial optimization with geographically revealing-region identification and global/local processing~\cite{geoshield}. Its vision-language threat differs from fixed-gallery retrieval. It is pertinent to broad field-level claims, not automatically a directly comparable baseline.

Neither work appears in the current bibliography. Section III-C's reference to a ``2024 survey'' is unnamed and uncited. The lack of a verified reusable implementation does not justify stating that a design variable has not been studied.

\action
Reposition novelty around \emph{matched-distortion auditing and its bounded empirical findings}. Add a focused comparison with prior mask-guided adversarial protection, specifying release object, attacker access, learning objective, spatial weighting, norm/utility matching, and evaluation metric. Cite the actual survey and document any code-availability limitation without treating it as evidence of conceptual absence. Reimplement a faithful mask-guided adversarial baseline where feasible; disclose any task adaptation.
\acceptance
The contribution remains meaningful after the closest prior work is described accurately. No claim of first discovering directional privacy survives merely by excluding related work from an artificially narrow method family.

\finding{R6}{Major}{The protocol does not yet isolate three independent design factors}
\evidence
The paper describes one-axis-at-a-time intervention, but spatial magnitudes and geometric direction are not inherently independent: changing a weight map changes the normalized perturbation vector. The iterative directional optimizer also produces nonuniform final magnitudes through repeated sign updates, projection, and clipping. Comparing that output with weighted Gaussian noise changes more than sign alignment.

The main operator comparison uses edge and uniform placements against ResNet18. It is not the full cross-product of every placement, operator, and both attackers. Claims that the result holds ``under any operator'' against either attacker should not imply this missing factorial experiment was run.

Moreover, matching $\sum_iw_i^2$ matches expected Gaussian energy before clipping, not each realized draw:
\begin{equation}
\|D_w\varepsilon\|_2^2=\sum_iw_i^2\varepsilon_i^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the statement in Section III-H that any delivered-MSE shortfall must be energy lost at the clamp is too strong without measuring pre-clamp realized energy. The delivered-MSE operator experiment resolves a confound for those tested pairs, but not retroactively for every nominal-energy placement comparison.

\action
Define operational factors precisely. Add matched-release controls retaining an optimized perturbation's magnitudes while randomizing its signs, retaining its signs while uniformizing magnitudes, and crossing representative placements with directional optimization. Hold model access, optimization effort, random starts, and delivered distortion fixed. Record both pre-clamp and post-clamp distortion. Repeat the essential factorial subset on a strong VPR attacker; label untested combinations explicitly.
\acceptance
The observed advantage can be attributed to the specified intervention, with uncertainty, rather than to a compound change in access, compute, sign, magnitude, and clipping.

\finding{R7}{Major}{Matched distortion does not establish a superior privacy--utility frontier}
\evidence
The current supplement reports a useful utility assessment, including EOT. Table IX shows mIoU falling from 0.6709 for isotropic noise to 0.6010 for the surrogate direction and 0.5025 for the EOT-hardened direction at the same MSE. These are material downstream costs. The claim that direction ``wins outright'' per unit utility because allocation's numerator is zero is unsupported: an undetected effect is not exactly zero, and a single operating point does not establish a frontier.

Retrieval is evaluated on MSLS, while utility is measured on separate VOC/COCO images. ``Same-image utility'' means each utility model evaluates its corresponding sanitized image; it does not establish joint retrieval privacy and task utility on the same MSLS releases. The selected MSE 15.68, about 36.18 dB on a 0--255 scale, is called quality-preserving without a task-level acceptance threshold. Conversely, 24 dB is called unusable without matching downstream evidence.

\action
Report privacy versus actual downstream utility over several measured budgets, for isotropic noise, representative spatial controls, gallery-free direction, and EOT direction. Define an application-motivated utility tolerance and compare at matched utility as well as matched distortion. Prefer a common annotated street-scene subset or explicitly restrict cross-dataset conclusions. Keep global mAP/mIoU and per-image paired summaries distinct; averaging per-image AP is not dataset-level AP.
\acceptance
Any deployment recommendation is supported by a frontier or explicitly restricted to a distortion-only observation. Claims about useful or destroyed image quality are tied to a prespecified criterion, with standard task metrics and uncertainty.

\finding{R8}{Major}{Restricted attacker fine-tuning does not justify the general adaptation claim}
\evidence
Section III-G evaluates useful but resource-restricted fine-tuning: most layers are frozen, the gallery embeddings remain fixed, and validation Top-1 selects the checkpoint. The claim that real systems do not re-embed galleries is an unsupported universal constraint, particularly for the evaluated 2,000-image gallery. A gallery-rebuilding attacker belongs to the natural threat space unless excluded explicitly.

Directional adaptation is selected on only 50 validation queries, starting at two correct for ResNet18. If all selected checkpoints equal the pretrained model, a held-out null follows by construction. Improved validation median rank indicates learning, but does not prove that a stronger attack cannot improve Top-1. The isotropic held-out improvements in supplementary Table X are descriptive: their reported $p$-values, 0.125 and 0.250, do not establish statistically significant improvements.

The final sanitizer includes EOT, whereas the fine-tuning code's directional cache calls the optimizer without an EOT configuration. The adaptation evidence therefore does not cover the complete hardened release mechanism. A white-box perturbation regenerated against each adapted encoder is also a different released object; its continued effectiveness is not evidence that an attacker failed to recover location from a fixed release.

\action
Define attacker knowledge, training data, computation, gallery-update access, and fixed-release evaluation explicitly. Test at least one stronger adaptation using the final hardened outputs, fresh release randomness, and re-embedded gallery features. Use a sufficiently informative, independent validation criterion and evaluate selected models on a larger place-disjoint test set. Keep fixed-release recovery separate from sanitizer reoptimization against a known target.
\acceptance
The abstract says which adaptation failed under which constraints. Either demonstrate resistance to the declared adaptive adversary or retain a bounded statement about the tested recipe; do not elevate a failed training run to general security.

\finding{R9}{Major}{The geographic benchmark and attacker generalization need sharper validation}
\evidence
The primary benchmark is a custom eight-city, 400-query, 2,000-gallery subset of the MSLS train/validation release, with correctness based on \file{unique_cluster}. This is stronger than the synthetic-identity proxy, but not automatically the official MSLS retrieval protocol. The original MSLS paper defines single-image positives using geographic distance and viewing-angle criteria~\cite{msls}. Matching a cluster label needs an empirical agreement check before being treated as equivalent to geographically accurate localization.

The manuscript already acknowledges predominantly forward-facing daytime images and sparse condition metadata. The decisive direction/EOT experiments target only ResNet18 and MixVPR. Six-backbone results for other experiment families do not establish six-backbone directional robustness. Pretraining overlap, city-level separation, gallery sampling, and preprocessing resolution can affect the interpretation of an ``unseen attacker.''

\action
Publish the manifest construction and an audit of cluster-based positives against GPS/heading-based positives, handling multiple valid references and near-duplicate views. State dataset roles, city/sequence splits, query/gallery overlap, model pretraining sources, and input preprocessing. Validate the key contrasts on an independent geographic split or second dataset and another held-out strong VPR architecture; a full expansion of every old proxy table is unnecessary. Report per-city effects and uncertainty instead of pooling away heterogeneity.
\acceptance
The paper labels the benchmark accurately as a custom subset where appropriate, and its main conclusion survives a meaningful independent geographic/attacker check or is narrowed to the current subset.

\finding{R10}{Major}{Top-1 failure is not equivalent to location privacy, and the final release boundary is incomplete}
\evidence
The threat model defines success at Top-$k$, but the central direction story relies on Top-1. The review's direct D6 calculation finds that under the unhardened full surrogate ensemble, MixVPR still has Top-5 0.8300 and Top-10 0.8600, despite Top-1 falling to 0.7317. ResNet18 Top-5/10 are 0.1025/0.1450. These values do not refute a Top-1 reduction; they show why it should not be presented as making location private.

The pipeline releases floating-point tensors in the experiment and later applies selected transforms. A real stored or transmitted image may involve integer quantization, codec settings, resizing, and color conversion. The manuscript should distinguish the optimization constraint from the final serialized-release constraint: post-optimization scaling in \file{release_at_mse} can increase a perturbation's amplitude, so the intermediate $\ell_\infty$ projection alone does not certify the final bound. Actual violation has not been established by this review.

The introduction motivates video, but the multi-frame experiment primarily evaluates the original PPEDCRF/noise mechanism, not the final gallery-free EOT defense. Its result cannot be transferred automatically to repeated or temporally aggregated directional releases.

\action
Report Top-1/5/10, reciprocal rank, geographic-error distributions, and residual attack success for the main directional conditions. Define positive-place sets consistently. Evaluate decoded saved releases and measure final MSE, maximum amplitude, clipping, and file format. Test the final mechanism under short multi-frame or repeated-release aggregation if retaining video-level language; otherwise clearly limit the claim to one frame. Describe the white-box result as an achieved diagnostic reference, not a certified privacy bound.
\acceptance
The security claim names the attacker success metric and released object, and distinguishes empirical ranking suppression from formal or universal privacy.

\finding{R12}{Major}{The implemented method and optimization need a complete mathematical and procedural specification}
\evidence
The DCRF update is an averaging/temporal-relaxation recurrence called mean-field-style inference, without an explicit CRF energy or derivation. The distinction matters because a standard CRF model has specific probabilistic semantics. The support map is continuous and nearly 0.5 in the released checkpoint, so ``perturbs only the inferred background support'' is misleading unless a hard support restriction is actually applied. The paper commendably identifies the degeneracy, but its method description still reads like a working selective mechanism.

The directional method is now described with 20 steps, step size, projection, and random start. Remaining details include exact objectives, surrogate normalizations, transform probabilities/sample counts, model versions, and whether the final scaling preserves the specified constraints. Low-pass, correlated-noise, and block-quantization operators are named in the main results but need explicit, reproducible definitions in the review package. A fixed number of arbitrary implementation choices should not be presented as a complete search over operator design.

\action
Provide concise equations or pseudocode for the release protocol, energy matching, each nontrivial operator, and the gallery-free EOT objective. For example, specify whether the optimized loss is
\[
\mathcal L(\delta;x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M
\mathbb E_{T\sim\mathcal T}

\section{Suggested restructuring and wording}

\subsection{Recommended narrative}
Lead with the audit problem and the difference between nominal and delivered distortion. Define the matched-release protocol before devoting substantial space to the PPEDCRF implementation. Present the constant-map diagnostic as a case study, then the nondegenerate placement tests, the direction comparison, practical utility/attacker limitations, and a carefully qualified local analysis. The synthetic model should illustrate conditions under which allocation does or does not help, not assert an explanation it fails to quantitatively reproduce.

A more defensible title is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Matched-Distortion Auditing of Spatial Allocation and Adversarial Perturbations for Visual Location Privacy}
\end{quote}
This avoids committing the paper to a universal negative result before its scope and inferential support are established.

\subsection{Example abstract direction}
The following is an illustrative rewrite, not a replacement inserted into the manuscript. Numerical values refer to the currently verified D6 table and must be synchronized again after final analysis:
\begin{quote}
Releasing street-scene images can reveal location through visual retrieval, even after geographic metadata are removed. We study whether spatial allocation contributes to protection beyond the distortion it introduces. Our evaluation redistributes sanitizer weights and compares perturbation operators and adversarial perturbations at controlled delivered distortion. In a conditional-random-field-inspired testbed, one learned map is nearly constant, demonstrating that a plausible privacy--quality curve need not establish useful spatial selection. On a custom Mapillary Street-Level Sequences subset, the tested placements provide no consistently demonstrated advantage over uniform noise near the selected operating point. Gallery-free perturbations optimized on surrogate models reduce first-ranked retrieval accuracy from 0.1967 to 0.0317 for a held-out classification backbone and from 0.7800 to 0.7317 for a dedicated place-recognition model. Optimization over image transformations improves resistance to evaluated preprocessing, including held-out transformations, but incurs additional segmentation loss. These findings support matched-distortion controls for auditing selective sanitizers and motivate comparisons at matched downstream utility. They establish bounded empirical effects, rather than universal irrelevance of spatial allocation or a formal privacy guarantee.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Presentation and reference cleanup}
\begin{itemize}
\item Define a single notation for the clean frame, released frame, placement amplitude, perturbation vector, distortion, positive gallery set, and attacker transformation; avoid reusing $\delta$ for both calibration and image perturbation without explanation.
\item Use ``CRF-inspired'' if no CRF energy/mean-field derivation is supplied, and ``amplitude normalization'' instead of suggesting that NCP is an optimized penalty.
\item Define ``background'' as a semantic-model output, not ground-truth privacy sensitivity. Buildings, signs, vehicles, and other categories may carry location information regardless of foreground/background taxonomy.
\item Replace incomplete cross-document references to ``the supplementary material'' or ``the extended report'' with identifiable sections/tables and a stable artifact identifier.
\item Remove visible process history: ``carried over from the earlier framing,'' ``superseded,'' and descriptions of previously caught implementation errors belong in provenance notes, not the scientific narrative unless essential to a reproducibility finding.
\item Verify each cited paper's task and mechanism against its primary source. In particular, distinguish training-data privacy in generative modeling, recognition-time obfuscation, and poisoning-based protection; these should not be collapsed into input sanitization.
\item Cite the MSLS dataset paper in addition to the repository URL, and complete publisher metadata, author names, capitalization, and final publication details for relevant entries. No unresolved active main-paper citation keys were found; this is an accuracy/positioning review, not a claim that BibTeX is broken.
\item Keep model architecture counts separate from independently evaluated attacker counts. A surrogate is not evidence of held-out-target robustness, and multiple checkpoints of one architecture are not independent method families.
\item Report effect sizes and intervals prominently; reserve significance stars for a clearly defined family and avoid attaching subgroup significance to pooled means.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final scholarly assessment}

The paper has the ingredients for a useful security-evaluation contribution: a diagnostic failure case, energy controls, real location labels, a strong attacker, transfer, preprocessing, and utility measurements. The strongest currently supportable result is that \emph{specified} gallery-free adversarial perturbations outperform the tested isotropic controls in this benchmark at a given delivered distortion, while the tested spatial rules show limited or uncertain incremental benefit there. The current exports support retaining that empirical observation.

Publication readiness instead depends on removing the claimed theoretical separation of allocation from ranking, establishing novelty relative to directly related methods, synchronizing every reported result, and assessing privacy under an adequately specified utility and attacker model. Additional prose compression alone will not resolve those scientific issues. All R1--R13 items are open recommendations at the end of this review; none is claimed to have been fixed by writing this document.
