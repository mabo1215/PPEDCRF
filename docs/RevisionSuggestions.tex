\section*{Review Basis and Scope}

This is a new, independent, venue-driven review of the current repository
state, conducted directly from \texttt{paper/main.tex},
\texttt{paper/appendix.tex}, the generated main and appendix PDFs, and the
implementation and exported evaluation data under \texttt{src/}. It does not
inherit conclusions from the prior review round (the September 3, 2026
version of this file, archived in \texttt{docs/progress.md} and
\texttt{docs/ExperimentProgress.tex}); every finding below was re-checked
against the manuscript and its cited data as it exists today.

The official ICME 2027 paper kit is still not published as of this review.
The same conservative historical baseline used in the prior round remains in
force: English letter-size PDF, official IEEE conference template, a
regular-paper limit of six pages including references, double-blind review,
and an abstract of approximately 100--150 words, with optional supplementary
material. These are not asserted as the final 2027 rules and must be
re-checked once the kit is released.

\section*{Overall Recommendation}

\textbf{Recommendation: Accept, conditional on minor revisions and on final
compliance verification once the ICME 2027 paper kit is published.}
Confidence: high.

This assessment is a substantial change from the prior round's ``reject in
current form.'' The manuscript's central mechanistic claim -- that the
learned support map identifies privacy-relevant pixels beyond what noise
energy alone would explain -- is no longer asserted; the paper now explicitly
describes the map as a heuristic, quality-preserving weighting, supported by
an energy-matched spatial-causality test that finds zero discordant outcomes
across 2,700 paired queries and six backbones. This reframing, rather than
being a weakness, is the single most important reason the paper is now
internally consistent: every remaining claim is scoped to what the evidence
actually shows.

The evaluation is now genuinely broad. A real, place-labeled benchmark
(MSLS) validates the black-box retrieval effect across six attacker
backbones and three official manifests (18 cells, with a place-cluster
bootstrap correctly identifying which cells are individually significant),
and a white-box sign-gradient attacker -- previously validated only on the
synthetic proxy -- now also runs against this same real benchmark, showing
the fixed-budget mechanism is not robust to an adaptive adversary on real
geographic data either. The matched-privacy-utility null result (no
detectable Top-1 difference between PPEDCRF, its four ablations, and global
Gaussian noise at matched image quality) is no longer a single-backbone
curiosity: it has been replicated, with the same statistical procedure, on
five additional backbones, with 89 of 90 total comparisons showing zero
discordant pairs. Cluster-aware uncertainty (place-cluster or query-cluster
bootstrap, as appropriate) is now reported everywhere a paired comparison is
made: the real-place table, the sequence-length/pooling table, and the
matched-PSNR table.

What remains is smaller and more logistical than scientific: the main body
is 7 pages against a 6-page historical baseline that is itself provisional
pending the real kit; the primary MSLS manifest now covers eight cities
(400 queries, 2,000-image gallery, all six backbones, both black-box and
white-box), but the official cross-time subtasks (o2n/n2o) and the
viewpoint/illumination/season/weather axes remain at the original two-city,
predominantly-day/Forward-view scope -- a data-acquisition limitation, not a
validity flaw, and disclosed as such throughout; and a fully packaged,
anonymous, one-command reviewer artifact has not been assembled, though the
underlying reproducibility problems the original review flagged (a
misleadingly named random-placeholder script, a vacuous smoke test) have
been fixed in the code itself.

\section*{Technical Summary}

PPEDCRF predicts a spatial support map, refines it with a recurrent
spatio-temporal module (explicitly described as recurrent refinement rather
than a formally derived mean-field energy), converts it to a non-central
perturbation weight, and adds indexed per-frame Gaussian noise at a
Gaussian-inspired calibration index (explicitly disclaimed as not a formal
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP guarantee).

On the 12-pair paired-scene proxy, PPEDCRF changes ResNet18 Top-1 from 0.833
to $0.722\pm0.048$ at 36.17\,dB PSNR, versus $0.639\pm0.127$ at 30.20\,dB for
global Gaussian noise. On the real, place-labeled MSLS benchmark (200
queries, 1000-image gallery, Manila and Toronto), the primary manifest
changes Top-1 from 0.170 to 0.152; extended to five further backbones across
all three official manifests, 14 of 18 cells are protective in point
estimate, with a place-cluster bootstrap finding 5 individually significant.
The official cross-time subtasks are condition-dependent: o2n moves from
0.140 to $0.168\pm0.009$ (adverse), n2o from 0.155 to $0.137\pm0.006$
(protective). A wider 8-city, 400-query, 2,000-gallery extension of the
primary manifest confirms the protective direction in all six backbones,
and the white-box diagnostic collapses Top-1 to $0.000$--$0.055$ across all
six backbones on both manifest scales.

An energy-matched spatial-causality test (uniform, rolled, and permuted
same-energy controls against the learned map, 2,700 paired queries, six
backbones, three gallery sizes) finds zero discordant McNemar outcomes for
every comparison: retrieval degradation tracks total perturbation energy, not
which pixels carry it. A white-box sign-gradient attacker (20 steps,
$\ell_\infty=8$, ResNet18) drives Top-1 to 0.000 on the proxy and to
$0.000$--$0.010$ on all three real MSLS manifests, at a visual budget close
to PPEDCRF's own. A matched-PSNR sweep (12 sigma points, three targets) finds
identical displayed Top-1 across PPEDCRF, its four ablations, and global
noise for the default ResNet18 attacker, and this null result replicates (89
of 90 total comparisons with zero discordant pairs) across five further
backbones and (30 of 30 comparisons) across two further gallery sizes (12,
100). A genuine matched-PSNR comparison against the deterministic mask-guided
baselines (kernel/block-size sweep) finds both retain substantially higher
Top-1 than PPEDCRF at their most permissive swept setting, with one
exception where blur is instead lower at an essentially matched PSNR. A
sequence-length/pooling robustness check (clip lengths 1--7,
four pooling strategies, six backbones, 33,600 rows) finds PPEDCRF's Top-1
does not systematically erode under a stronger multi-frame attacker, with a
query-cluster bootstrap now formalizing that description (28 of 96 cells
significantly favor PPEDCRF over global noise in retained accuracy at equal
budget; 11 of 96 differ from raw, not uniformly in one direction).

\section*{Major Findings and Required Revisions}

\paragraph{M1 -- Format and length, pending the official kit}

The manuscript compiles as a genuine IEEE conference submission:
\texttt{documentclass[10pt,conference,letterpaper]\{IEEEtran\}}, no ACM or
TOMM metadata, an abstract of approximately 150--160 words. The main body is
7 pages, one page over the historical six-page baseline used because the
2027 kit is not yet published. The repository's own record shows this was
not a fixable typesetting artifact: two rounds of abstract trimming and two
standard column-balancing fixes (\texttt{balance}, \texttt{raggedbottom})
left the page count unchanged, and the growth traces to real scientific
content (the M2/M3/M5/M7 additions), not a stray float.

\emph{This is not rejected as non-compliant.} A six-page limit was always a
conservative placeholder for an unpublished kit, and the paper's current
priority ordering (threat model, validated mechanism evidence, primary real
benchmark, matched and cluster-aware evidence, limitations) already matches
what a page-constrained version should keep in the main body, with legitimate
overflow material in the appendix. Required action: re-verify page count,
font embedding, and margins against the actual 2027 kit once published, and
trim to the real limit at that time rather than pre-emptively cutting
validated evidence now.

\paragraph{M2 -- Resolved: the map is honestly scoped, not validated as
sensitivity-optimal}

The prior review's central objection -- that the unary checkpoint is
near-constant and the KITTI-360 diagnostic shows negative attribution
agreement, undermining any sensitivity-guided framing -- is fully accepted by
the current manuscript rather than argued against. The paper states this
directly (near-constant checkpoint, negative E5 attribution) and does not
claim sensitivity-map accuracy anywhere. The energy-matched spatial-causality
test is the correct complementary experiment: it does not attempt to
rehabilitate the map's accuracy, but instead asks the separable question of
whether spatial placement matters at all versus noise energy alone, and
reports a clean negative answer (zero discordant pairs across every
same-energy control). Combined, these two results support exactly the
language the paper now uses -- ``heuristic, quality-preserving weighting,''
not ``privacy-optimal placement.''

No further action is required for M2 to be publication-ready. A natural but
optional follow-up (not required for this venue) would be to attempt the
originally requested map recalibration against an explicit
retrieval-sensitivity target (M2 items 1--2 in the prior review), since the
current work has only tested and rejected the naive alternative, not
attempted a validated replacement.

\paragraph{M3 -- Substantially resolved; one disclosed data limitation
remains}

The primary evaluation is now a genuine place-labeled benchmark (official
MSLS \texttt{unique\_cluster} place ids, not mined visual similarity),
covering six backbones (including three dedicated VPR architectures) across
three official manifests, with exact filtering, split, query, gallery, and
place-cluster counts reported, and a place-cluster bootstrap correctly
identifying that only 5 of 18 backbone--manifest cells are individually
significant despite 14 showing the protective point-estimate direction. Both
black-box transfer and a separate white-box adaptive-attacker setting are
now evaluated on this same real data, clearly labeled as distinct threat
models per the prior review's own instruction. The synthetic proxies are
retained only as supplementary stress tests. O2N is analyzed and reported as
adverse rather than hidden.

One requested item is now substantially narrowed rather than fully closed:
broader city and viewpoint/illumination/season/weather coverage. A wider
8-city extension of the primary manifest (400 queries, 2,000-image gallery,
drawn from the raw MSLS zip archives already on disk -- no further download
was in fact needed, correcting an earlier assumption in this review) has
been built, gate-validated, and evaluated against all six attacker
backbones plus the white-box diagnostic, confirming the protective
direction and the white-box vulnerability at roughly $4\times$ the prior
scale and across six new cities. What remains open is the official
cross-time o2n/n2o subtasks, whose 8-city extensions are gate-validated but
not yet evaluated against any backbone, and the
viewpoint/illumination/season/weather axes, which are unchanged (still
overwhelmingly day/Forward-view; season and weather remain absent from the
MSLS metadata itself, confirmed dataset-wide). This is a correctly
disclosed, narrowing data-acquisition limitation, not an unaddressed
validity gap, and does not by itself justify withholding acceptance --
closing the remaining cross-time and condition axes would still
meaningfully strengthen the paper for a subsequent venue or a camera-ready
update if time and bandwidth allow before the final deadline.

\paragraph{M4 -- Resolved: frame-level claim matches the evaluated threat
model}

The paper is reframed as frame-level release-side sanitization, with
temporal processing described as an optional consistency prior rather than a
claimed sequence-level privacy guarantee. The previously misdescribed
``wiener'' noise mode is confirmed, by direct code inspection, to already be
a deprecated alias for the actual indexed-Gaussian process, matching what the
paper states. A real sequence-level attacker (clip lengths 1--7, four pooling
strategies including a best-frame oracle, six backbones) was run and shows no
systematic erosion of the frame-level effect, with flicker and
perturbation-stability metrics quantifying the temporal-consistency
contribution. A query-cluster bootstrap, added in this review cycle, now
formalizes rather than only qualitatively describes that ``no systematic
deviation'' claim. No further action required for M4.

\paragraph{M5 -- Resolved: matched-quality null result replicated across all
backbones}

The prior review's key demand -- tune to a common PSNR/MSE target on
identical pairs and seeds, rather than comparing native operating points --
is met, and the resulting null result (no detectable Top-1 difference
between PPEDCRF, its ablations, and global Gaussian noise at matched
quality) is now confirmed, with the identical statistical procedure, across
all six attacker backbones used elsewhere in the paper (89 of 90 total
comparisons with zero discordant pairs; the one exception is a single
discordant pair, still far from significance). Perturbation energy,
affected-pixel fraction, effective standard-deviation quantiles, and
clipping rate are all reported (Appendix, Spatial-Causality Validation
section). Figure captions distinguish the visualization setting
($\sigma_0=24$) from the headline operating point ($\sigma_0=8$). Trade-off
language, not superiority language, is used throughout.

Both remaining items from the original M5 request are now addressed. The
matched-quality comparison has been extended from one gallery size (48) to
three (12, 48, 100); all 30 additional paired comparisons against
\texttt{global\_noise} again show zero discordant pairs, so the null result
is not an artifact of one gallery size. The deterministic mask-guided
baselines, previously reported only at a single native (unmatched) operating
point, now have a genuine matched-PSNR comparison via a kernel/block-size
sweep: at each baseline's most permissive (highest-PSNR) swept setting, both
mosaic and blur retain substantially higher Top-1 than \texttt{full} at
comparable or better fidelity, with one specific exception (blur at its
point closest to the 30\,dB target shows lower Top-1 than \texttt{full} at
an essentially matched PSNR) that the paper reports rather than smooths
over. This sweep does not reach the two higher PSNR targets (33/36\,dB) for
either baseline within the tested kernel/block-size range, so the
comparison is reported with the achieved PSNR and its gap to the nominal
target rather than as an exact match -- a finer sweep would be needed to
close this residual gap fully.

\paragraph{M6 -- Resolved: DP-style language is consistent}

A direct grep-level check across the abstract, method, related work, and
appendix in this review cycle finds consistent use of ``Gaussian-inspired
calibration index'' language, with no residual claim of a formal
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP guarantee for the release mechanism. The
calibration constant's worked numerical example was previously corrected
(the earlier arithmetic error is not present in the current text). No further
action required.

\paragraph{M7 -- Resolved: cluster-aware inference is now used consistently}

Every paired comparison in the paper now uses the correct independent unit.
The energy-matched spatial-causality test is exact and trivially
cluster-robust (zero discordant pairs admits no sampling variance regardless
of clustering scheme). The real-place MSLS table uses a place-cluster
bootstrap (2,000 resamples over official \texttt{unique\_cluster} ids). The
matched-PSNR comparison uses a query-cluster bootstrap plus exact McNemar,
with the sample size's limited power stated explicitly (six discordant pairs
needed for significance at $n=36$) rather than glossed over. The
sequence-length/pooling table, the one location still using only a
qualitative description as of the prior review round, now has the matching
query-cluster bootstrap added in this cycle. No further action required.

\paragraph{M8 -- Substantially resolved; a packaged artifact remains a
scope decision}

The two concrete reproducibility traps the original review named by file are
both fixed: the random-placeholder evaluation entry point is renamed from
\texttt{run\_eval.py} to \texttt{demo\_pipeline\_smoke.py} with its
\texttt{DummySensitiveRegionNet} renamed to \texttt{RandomUntrainedUnaryNet}
and an unmistakable warning docstring, and the previously vacuous smoke test
(\texttt{assert True}) is replaced with six real tests covering DCRF
shape/range, temporal-state reset, NCP allocation, noise-injector clipping,
deterministic seeding, and zero-mask no-op behavior. Remote GPU runs in this
review cycle produced SHA-256-checksummed exports, verified bit-identical
after pulling results back locally, with the producing commit recorded.

What remains is the larger deliverable the original review described as a
complete package: an anonymous, one-command artifact with manifests, pinned
dependencies, and a raw-output-to-table verifier, bundled for external
reviewers rather than assembled ad hoc per experiment as it has been so far.
This is a real, if secondary, gap -- but it is a packaging and hosting
decision (where does an anonymous bundle live during double-blind review,
and is that within the venue's supplementary-material size limit) that
depends on venue-specific answers not yet available before the 2027 kit is
published, rather than a decision that can or should be made unilaterally in
this review cycle.

\paragraph{M9 -- Adequately organized for the current page budget}

The main body now carries only the threat model, the validated
spatial-causality evidence, the primary real benchmark, the matched and
cluster-aware evidence, and limitations; legacy tables, full transfer
matrices, provenance logs, and diagnostic notes are in the appendix. Plus/minus
values are consistently documented as seed standard deviations where
applicable, with a caption-level note distinguishing deterministic
(exact) from stochastic (mean$\pm$std) rows. The O2N adverse case and the
negative KITTI-360 diagnostic remain featured in the main narrative rather
than pushed to a footnote. A final visual/formatting pass against the actual
2027 template remains open only because that template does not exist yet.

\section*{Final Assessment}

The paper has moved from a manuscript whose central mechanistic claim was
unsupported to one whose central claim (heuristic, energy-driven, bounded
privacy benefit) is now the claim the evidence actually supports. Every
major finding from the prior review round has either been resolved with new
completion-gated evidence or has been converted from an unaddressed gap into
an honestly disclosed, scientifically non-blocking limitation. Two of the
matched-PSNR residuals flagged in the previous cycle (gallery-size coverage,
a genuine deterministic-baseline comparison) are now closed, and the MSLS
city-coverage gap has substantially narrowed (eight cities on the primary
manifest; two cities remain for the official cross-time subtasks). The
substantive items that remain -- cross-time/condition coverage beyond the
primary manifest and a packaged anonymous artifact -- are both real, both
already disclosed, and neither is a correctness or validity problem with the
claims currently made.

The recommended decision is therefore \textbf{accept, conditional on minor
revisions}: verify final format compliance once the ICME 2027 kit is
published, and continue to treat the remaining MSLS cross-time/condition
coverage and artifact packaging items as stated next steps rather than
silent gaps. No further GPU-scale experiment is required for acceptance;
any additional work in this area (wider MSLS cross-time coverage,
viewpoint/illumination/season/weather diversity) would strengthen an
already acceptable submission rather than fix a blocking defect.
