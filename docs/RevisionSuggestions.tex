was conducted directly from \texttt{paper/main.tex},

\section*{Overall Recommendation}

\textbf{Recommendation: Reject in the current form; encourage resubmission after
major revision.} Confidence: high.

The work addresses an ICME-relevant privacy problem and reports a potentially
useful spatially weighted perturbation. Nevertheless, the current manuscript
is not a compliant ICME submission and its central mechanistic claim is not
established. The generated main paper is a 17-page ACM TOMM manuscript and the
appendix is an additional 11-page ACM document. The main PDF contains ACM CCS
material, ACM reference-format front matter, and TOMM metadata. More
importantly, the supplied unary sensitivity checkpoint is near-constant on
the monitoring data, while the KITTI-360 structural-support diagnostic has
negative attribution agreement. Thus, the evidence does not show that DCRF
and NCP respond to a meaningful privacy sensitivity signal.

The most defensible current result is narrower: on a small synthetic retrieval
proxy, a particular spatially weighted Gaussian perturbation reduces ResNet18
retrieval at a particular visual-quality level. The manuscript also reports
heterogeneous transfer, an adverse real-world condition, nearly identical
module ablations, and no matched-PSNR privacy advantage over global noise.
These limitations must shape the contribution and claims.

\subsection*{Reviewer scorecard}

The following scores are an internal review assessment on a 1--10 scale, where
10 is strongest. They are not an official ICME 2027 scoring rubric because the
2027 paper kit is not yet available.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Scope fit: 7/10. Multimedia privacy and visual place retrieval are
  relevant to the ICME scope.
  \item Technical correctness: 3/10. The implemented perturbation is
  reproducible in principle, but the sensitivity-map premise and the temporal
  interpretation are not currently validated.
  \item Novelty: 5/10. The combination of spatial weighting, recurrent

  \item Significance: 4/10. The empirical effect may be useful under a narrow
  threat model, but current transfer and real-world evidence do not support a

  \item Experimental strength: 3/10. The proxy is controlled but synthetic;

  \item Clarity: 5/10. The paper documents many details, but ACM/TOMM
  material, legacy experiments, and frame--sequence language obscure the
  evidence hierarchy.
  \item Reproducibility: 3/10. Important checkpoints and outputs are not
  directly auditable from the repository, and one evaluator path uses a random
  placeholder model.
  \item Reviewer confidence: 9/10. The recommendation is supported by the
  generated PDFs, source audit, implementation audit, and exported results.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Strengths}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The threat model is concrete: a background-based visual-place
  retrieval attacker rather than an unspecified privacy adversary.
  \item The paper provides useful implementation details for the recurrence,
  NCP weighting, noise scale, clipping, seeds, galleries, and attackers.
  \item Negative findings are disclosed, including the weak checkpoint,
  negative KITTI-360 attribution diagnostic, and matched-quality null result.
  \item The MSLS experiment uses official place labels and exposes an
  important O2N failure mode.
  \item The manuscript distinguishes Gaussian-inspired calibration from a
  formal differential-privacy guarantee; this distinction should be retained.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Technical Summary}

PPEDCRF predicts a spatial mask, refines it with a recurrent spatial-temporal
module, converts it to a non-central perturbation weight, and adds Gaussian
noise. The reported update is approximately
\[
p_t^{(k+1)} =
\operatorname{sigmoid}\left(
u_t + \lambda_s\bigl(S(p_t^{(k)})-p_t^{(k)}\bigr)
+ \lambda_\tau\bigl(\hat p_{t-1}-p_t^{(k)}\bigr)
\right),
\]
with a 3 by 3 average-pooling operator, reflection padding, and five
iterations. NCP gives the effective weight
\[
w_t = \alpha \odot p_t
= \alpha \frac{p_t^2}{\max(p_t)+\epsilon}.
\]
The Gaussian-inspired scale is
\[
\sigma_0(\epsilon,\delta)
= C \sqrt{2\log(1.25/\delta)}/\epsilon,
\]
but the paper explicitly disclaims a formal privacy guarantee.

The main controlled proxy contains 12 synthetic locations, a candidate pool of
120 images, 36 hard distractors, a middle-frame query, three seeds, and
galleries of 12, 24, and 48 images. In proxy12, raw ResNet18 Top-1 is 0.833,
PPEDCRF is 0.722 plus or minus 0.048 at 36.17 dB PSNR and 0.945 SSIM for
nominal \texttt{sigma0=8}. Global Gaussian noise is 0.639 plus or minus
0.127 at 30.20 dB PSNR and 0.827 SSIM. The principal module ablations are
nearly identical after rounding.

Across eight attacker backbones and three gallery sizes, the robustness table
contains 20 negative, one tied, and three positive PPEDCRF-minus-raw cells.
The proxy50 result contains 23 negative cells, one tie, and no positive cells.
On MSLS, the appendix reports all-condition raw versus sanitized Top-1 of
0.170 versus 0.152, while O2N changes from 0.140 to 0.168 and N2O changes
from 0.155 to 0.137. The KITTI-360 diagnostic reports approximately
minus 0.00024 Spearman correlation, 0.0497 top-10 overlap, and 0.0947
top-10-percent energy overlap.

\section*{Major Findings and Required Revisions}

\paragraph{M1 -- Blocking format, length, and anonymity}

The default build compiles the ACM branch of a conditional ACM/IEEE source.
The main PDF is ACM journal style, 17 pages, and carries ACM and TOMM
front matter. This is incompatible with the historical ICME baseline.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Obtain and follow the official ICME 2027 paper kit when released.
  Until then, use the official IEEE ICME conference template as the working
  baseline.
  \item Build a two-column IEEE conference PDF, within the final page limit
  including references. Treat the current appendix as supplementary material
  only if the 2027 rules allow it.
  \item Remove ACM journal metadata, CCS concepts, ACM reference-format text,
  TOMM submission language, and all venue-specific build labels.
  \item Enforce double-blind anonymity in the title block, acknowledgments,
  self-citations, artifact links, file metadata, and supplements.
  \item Shorten the approximately 227-word abstract to about 100--150 words,
  or to the exact 2027 limit.
  \item Run the official PDF compliance check and verify letter size, fonts,
  margins, hyperlinks, figure legibility, and final page count.
\end{itemize}

A six-page paper cannot retain the current narrative in full. The main paper
should prioritize the threat model, one validated mechanism claim, one primary
real benchmark, matched baselines, and limitations. Long matrices, logs, and
diagnostics should move to permitted supplementary material.

\paragraph{M2 -- Critical validity of the unary sensitivity map}

The provenance notes state that the original checkpoint has a null mask root
and near-constant probabilities on the monitoring data. The KITTI-360
structural-support target is explicitly not privacy-sensitivity ground truth,
and its attribution agreement is negative or approximately zero. These facts
undermine the central interpretation of PPEDCRF. The nearly identical
full/no-temporal/no-NCP/unary-only/no-DCRF rows also fail to identify which
module causes the observed effect.

Required experiment package:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Define sensitivity operationally as a target tied to the threat model:
  retrieval-score change, local embedding gradient, leave-one-region-out
  retrieval impact, or another defensible privacy-risk proxy.
  \item Train or calibrate the map without evaluation leakage and test it on
  held-out locations, cities, and attacker architectures.
  \item Report calibration, rank correlation, top-k overlap, energy
  concentration, confidence intervals, and spatial visualizations.
  \item Compare uniform noise, random fixed masks, center masks, saliency
  masks, and learned masks with DCRF and NCP disabled.
  \item Preserve total perturbation energy while permuting the spatial map.
  This intervention tests whether location matters beyond noise magnitude.
  \item If validation fails, reframe the method as heuristic spatial weighting
  and remove unsupported sensitivity-guided or privacy-optimal language.
\end{enumerate}

State exactly whether each headline checkpoint was trained, frozen, randomly
initialized, or substituted. A semantic structural mask may be a useful
diagnostic, but it is not privacy-sensitivity ground truth without a validated
bridge.

\paragraph{M3 -- Critical evaluation validity and geographic ground truth}

Proxy12 and proxy50 construct synthetic locations by image similarity. They
have no GPS or place identity, so retrieval changes do not by themselves
demonstrate geographic location privacy. MSLS is the strongest current result,
but it covers only 200 queries, a shared 1000-image gallery, two cities, one
main attacker family, one nominal noise level, and limited conditions. The
adverse O2N result is important: sanitization increases Top-1 from 0.140 to
0.168.

Required revisions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Make an official place-labeled benchmark the primary evaluation.
  Report exact filtering, split, query, gallery, and place counts with no
  scene leakage.
  \item Expand cities, day and night, season, weather, viewpoint, and time
  gap where supported. Show per-condition results before aggregation.
  \item Evaluate several attacker encoders and retrieval protocols on the real
  benchmark, including clearly labeled black-box transfer and white-box
  settings.
  \item Report Recall@1, Recall@5, Recall@10, or the selected primary metric,
  together with quality metrics and uncertainty clustered by place.
  \item Keep the synthetic proxies as supplementary stress tests and document
  every pair-construction choice.
  \item Analyze O2N per city and condition. If protection is not consistent,
  narrow the claim to conditional risk reduction.
\end{enumerate}

Do not call a synthetic similarity decrease geographic privacy without evidence
on place-labeled data.

\paragraph{M4 -- Major frame versus sequence mismatch}

The title and abstract claim video-sequence protection, but controlled retrieval
uses one middle frame and no sequence-level attacker. An attacker can sample
multiple released frames, average embeddings, or select the least perturbed
frame. The temporal notation therefore overstates the evaluated threat model.

The implementation's mode named "wiener" resets a seeded generator and draws
fresh Gaussian noise per frame; it is not a cumulative Wiener process. Rename
the process or implement the claimed stochastic process exactly.

Choose one path:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Reframe the work as frame-level background retrieval sanitization and
  describe temporal processing only as an optional consistency prior; or
  \item Add sequence-level attacks for clip lengths 1, 2, 4, and 8, several
  sampling strategies, embedding pooling, temporal attention, and a
  best-frame attacker. Report retrieval, flicker, and temporal-consistency
  metrics.
\end{itemize}

In either case define the released object, clip length, initialization, causal
state, attacker access, and exact noise process.

\paragraph{M5 -- Major matched privacy--utility comparison}

The default PPEDCRF point is 36.17 dB PSNR and 0.945 SSIM, versus 30.20 dB
and 0.827 for global Gaussian noise, 28.08 dB and 0.897 for blur, and
26.07 dB and 0.858 for mosaic. Different quality levels do not establish a
same-quality privacy advantage. The matched-PSNR sweep reports identical
rounded Top-1 values for all six sigma variants. Fifteen comparisons with
global noise have zero discordant pairs, exact McNemar $p=1.0$, and a
query-cluster bootstrap interval of $[0,0]$. This supports no detectable
difference under the current design, not superiority.

Required revisions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Tune PPEDCRF, global noise, blur, mosaic, random masks, and claimed
  competitors to common PSNR or MSE targets on identical pairs and seeds.
  \item Plot full privacy--quality curves with uncertainty over independent
  locations. Report unrounded metrics and effect sizes.
  \item Report perturbation energy, affected-pixel fraction, effective
  standard-deviation quantiles, and clipping or saturation rates.
  \item Distinguish Figure 5's visual setting \texttt{sigma0=24} from the
  headline setting \texttt{sigma0=8}.
  \item Use trade-off or conditional-benefit language unless a matched-quality
  improvement is statistically established.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{M6 -- Major differential-privacy ambiguity}

The calibration resembles a Gaussian mechanism expression, but the manuscript
does not specify adjacency, sensitivity of the complete release mechanism,
model-output clipping, temporal composition, or the effect of a data-dependent
mask. Pixel clipping to $[0,255]$ is not by itself a sensitivity proof.
Because NCP multiplies the base noise by the predicted mask, nominal
\texttt{sigma0} is not the released per-pixel standard deviation.

The current disclaimer is correct and should be made consistent throughout the
title, abstract, equations, captions, and conclusion. The preferred revision
is to call the quantity a Gaussian-inspired noise calibration index, define it
operationally, and report realized noise statistics. If a formal guarantee is
intended, define neighboring inputs, full-mechanism sensitivity, clipping,
composition, mask leakage, and all assumptions, then prove or correctly
account for the stated bound. Explicitly distinguish empirical retrieval
protection, perceptual obfuscation, and formal differential privacy.

\paragraph{M7 -- Major statistical unit and multiple comparisons}

The proxy12 table has 12 query locations and three seeds, hence 36
query--seed rows. McNemar analysis appears to treat these rows as independent,
while the bootstrap is clustered by 12 queries. Seeds do not create new
locations. The inference is therefore inconsistent.

Recompute using location or query cluster as the independent unit. Aggregate
or model seeds within cluster, use paired cluster bootstrap, permutation, or a
mixed-effects model, and state the estimand, resampling scheme, confidence
level, and sidedness. If McNemar is retained, apply it to a pre-specified
query-level paired outcome. Correct for backbone, gallery, condition, and
baseline multiplicity, or label those analyses exploratory. Reassess the
claim that six discordant pairs suffice because that threshold depends on the
independent-unit definition. For proxy50 use intervals over 50 locations. For
MSLS cluster by place identity.

\paragraph{M8 -- Major reproducibility and artifact audit}

Important checkpoints, outputs, third-party trees, and PDFs are ignored by
Git, while provenance contains machine-local paths such as
\texttt{/root/autodl-tmp/...}. \texttt{src/run\_eval.py} constructs a random
\texttt{DummySensitiveRegionNet} placeholder, and
\texttt{src/config/config.yaml} has \texttt{mask\_root: null} despite the
checkpoint-backed narrative. Evaluation scripts contain hard-coded TOMM
review-cycle identifiers. The smoke test is effectively always passing.

Provide an anonymous artifact or reviewer package with relative paths,
checkpoint and output hashes, data manifests, split definitions, pinned
dependencies, GPU and preprocessing details, deterministic settings, exact
commands, expected outputs, and a raw-output-to-table verifier. Separate
smoke-only random models from scientific commands. Add tests for checkpoint
loading, tensor shapes, deterministic seeds, clipping, temporal-state reset,
baseline matching, and metric aggregation. Remove private credentials and
machine paths.

\paragraph{M9 -- Moderate organization and presentation}

The manuscript mixes legacy utility experiments, proxy results, MSLS, KITTI-360
diagnostics, matched-PSNR tests, qualitative figures, and handoff notes. Put
the threat model, valid mechanism evidence, primary real benchmark, matched
curves, and limitations in the main paper. Move legacy tables, full transfer
matrices, provenance logs, extended sweeps, and diagnostic notes to permitted
supplementary material. Describe VOC segmentation and COCO detection as
separate utility experiments. Define whether plus-or-minus values are seed
standard deviations, standard errors, or confidence intervals. Explain the
raw-versus-three-seed sample-count difference in the margin analysis. Feature
the O2N adverse case and negative KITTI-360 diagnostic rather than hiding them
in a limitations footnote.

\section*{Additional Technical Comments}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The DCRF update is called mean-field-inspired but has no energy
  derivation. Supply the derivation or rename it a recurrent refinement
  module. Report the stability range of $\lambda_s$ and $\lambda_\tau$ and
  sensitivity to iteration count.
  \item Specify tensor dimensions, mask resolution, normalization range, noise
  units, clipping order, frame-boundary handling, and the meaning of $C$.
  \item Report NCP support area, effective noise quantiles, and clipping
  frequency; visual plausibility is not privacy evidence.
  \item Label the white-box sign-gradient experiment as a separate threat
  model and do not compare it directly with black-box results.
  \item Add recent multimedia-privacy, visual-place-recognition, retrieval-
  aware sanitization, video-protection, and privacy--utility literature.
  Position against methods sharing the same attacker and release model.
  \item Complete BibTeX metadata for entries producing missing pages, volume,
  or number warnings, and add persistent identifiers where available.
  \item Remove every TOMM reference from comments, output directories,
  experiment identifiers, captions, and PDF metadata before submission.
  \item Add limitations on domain shift, multiple-frame access, attacker
  adaptation, place-label dependence, absence of formal DP, and failed map
  validation.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Minimum Revision Package}

Before resubmission, the following are required:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A double-blind, two-column IEEE conference PDF built from the official
  ICME 2027 template, within the published limit including references.
  \item A title, abstract, and threat model that honestly identify frame-level
  or sequence-level release and attacker access.
  \item Either held-out validation of the sensitivity map or a complete
  heuristic-sanitizer reframe with unsupported causal claims removed.
  \item A primary public place-labeled evaluation across cities and conditions,
  multiple attackers, and cluster-aware uncertainty.
  \item Matched-quality privacy--utility curves, unrounded effect sizes, and
  realized perturbation statistics for all important baselines.
  \item Correct location-level inference, a multiplicity policy, and explicit
  separation of seed variability from data uncertainty.
  \item An anonymous one-command artifact with manifests, hashes, pinned
  environments, deterministic tests, and exact table regeneration.
  \item A concise failure-analysis section centered on the negative map
  diagnostic, adverse MSLS O2N result, and matched-PSNR null result.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Final Assessment}

PPEDCRF addresses an appropriate ICME topic and may contain a useful
engineering direction for spatially selective visual perturbation. The current
version, however, is an ACM journal manuscript rather than a compliant ICME
paper, and its central sensitivity-guided mechanism is not validated. The
evaluation does not yet provide a fair, statistically identified,
place-labeled privacy--utility result supporting the breadth of the claims.

The recommended decision is therefore \textbf{reject in the current form, with
encouragement to resubmit after major revision}. The paper could become
competitive if the authors validate the map or narrow the claim, make the real
benchmark primary, add matched and cluster-aware evidence, resolve the
frame--sequence threat model, and submit a reproducible anonymous IEEE paper.
