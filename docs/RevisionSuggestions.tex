\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This review was written from the current submission package alone --- the
compiled \file{main.pdf} (13 pages), \file{supplementary.pdf} (6 pages) and
\file{titlepage.pdf}, read against their sources, together with the released
per-query exports, the bundled reproducibility artifact and the repository
claim auditor, both of which I re-executed. No previous review, progress
document or completion claim was used as input. Every quantitative statement
below was recomputed from the released rows rather than read out of the
documents; \S\ref{sec:verification} lists each check so the authors can repeat
it. The venue requirements in \S\ref{sec:venue} were re-verified against the
IEEE Signal Processing Society author guidance current at the date of this
review rather than assumed.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: major revision.} The scientific core is in
good order and I could not break it: Table~I reproduces exactly from the two
placement trees, the real-data placement null reproduces exactly under both
units of inference, and the clip-pooling, purification and preprocessing
tables reproduce cell for cell. The protocol is a real contribution, the
negative results are reported with the qualifications that bound them, and the
mechanism's own defect is disclosed rather than buried.

Four things nevertheless keep this below the line, and three of them do not
need a computer.

\begin{itemize}
\item One sentence in \S III-G states a fact the paper's own released rows
contradict, and the two numeric ranges beside it are each computed over a
different, unstated subset of transforms (\S\ref{rev:R2}).
\item The clean retrieval baseline is printed two ways, and the wrong one sits
in the caption of the table that decides deployability (\S\ref{rev:R3}).
\item The artifact still asserts a table coverage it does not have. The single
supplementary table carrying the claim that turns the direction from fragile
into deployable is registered with neither checker, and eight of its thirteen
transform rows are not in the bundle at all (\S\ref{rev:R4}). In a paper whose
contribution \emph{is} auditing, that is the finding a referee will weight
most.
\item The central comparison gives the direction axis twenty optimisation
steps against a surrogate ensemble and gives the allocation axis none. Until
one optimised placement arm exists, the headline contrast is open to being
read as optimised-versus-unoptimised rather than direction-versus-allocation
(\S\ref{rev:R1}).
\end{itemize}

Two findings ask for new computation (\S\ref{rev:R1}, \S\ref{rev:R5}); one
asks for it or for an explicit scope statement (\S\ref{rev:R10}). The
remaining nine are corrections and additions the released exports already
support.

\section{Findings}

\finding{R1}{Major}{The direction axis is optimised and the allocation axis
never is, so the central contrast confounds the axis with the effort spent on
it}

\evidence
This is the objection a TIFS referee will raise first, and the paper currently
has no answer to it.

Every directional condition is the output of an optimiser: twenty sign-gradient
steps of one pixel under an $\ell_\infty$ bound of 16, summed over a three- or
four-model surrogate ensemble, from a $\pm1$ pixel random start, then a
per-frame bisection through the clamp to the exact target MSE. Every allocation
condition is a fixed rule evaluated once --- the mechanism's map, a uniform
control, spectral-residual saliency, Sobel edge magnitude, a centre-bias prior,
a fixed random field, a one-step score gradient and its inverse, and three
published segmentation priors. Not one placement is optimised against anything.

The paper is aware of the gap and stops one step short of closing it. \S III-J
derives the quantity a placement would have to maximise --- for a margin
gradient $a=\nabla_x m$ and weights $w$ under $\sum_i w_i^2=E$, the flip
probability is $\Phi(-m(x)/\sqrt{v(w)})$ with $v(w)=\sigma^2\sum_i a_i^2w_i^2$
--- and then writes that the gradient-guided rule ``is a smooth, renormalised
approximation to that corner solution, not a solution of it, and we make no
optimality claim for it''. The manuscript therefore knows the objective, knows
its maximiser, declines to solve for it, and reports a null over rules that do
not solve it.

The paper's own data show that the family is not exhausted by what was tried.
At $\sigma_0\ge32$ edge placement becomes significantly \emph{better} than
uniform by $-0.087$ to $-0.140$, and at delivered MSE $241.5$ edge beats
uniform under additive noise by $-0.0433$ ($p=0.008$). Placement is
demonstrably not inert as a matter of physics; the claim is that it is inert
\emph{in this regime}, and that claim is only as strong as the search over
placements that produced it. A referee will observe that a search of size zero
was run on one axis and a twenty-step search on the other.

I want to be clear that this is not a request to overturn the result. The
first-order argument in \S III-J is a good reason to expect an optimised
placement to fail as well, and if it does the paper's thesis is materially
stronger --- it would then rest on a matched search budget rather than on a
matched distortion budget alone, which is a strictly better experiment and the
one the protocol claims to be.

\action Add one \emph{optimised-allocation} arm at the operating point. Run
projected gradient on $w$ itself --- the same twenty steps, the same surrogate
ensemble and objective, the same per-frame bisection to delivered MSE
$15.68$, the same 400-query manifest, the same three seeds, the same held-out
attackers --- subject to the study's own energy-conservation gate
$\sum_i w_i^2 = E$, with the noise field either fixed per seed or taken in
expectation. Report it as an additional row in the primary placement family
and in Table~I's caption, and state in \S III-A that the two axes were given
the same search budget. If the optimised placement still does not separate
from uniform, say so in the abstract: it is a stronger sentence than the one
currently there.

\acceptance The manuscript reports at least one placement obtained by
optimisation rather than by prescription, at the operating point, against both
attackers, and states explicitly that the allocation and direction axes were
searched with the same budget.

\finding{R2}{Major}{A sentence in \S III-G is contradicted by the rows it is
drawn from, and its two ranges are computed over different transform sets}

\evidence
\S III-G reads: ``The white-box bound is fragile to every operator, not just
JPEG. Against MixVPR it recovers from $0.0008$ to $0.36$--$0.71$ under a single
transform, and against ResNet18 from $0.0058$ to $0.0175$--$0.1117$.''

Recomputing the unhardened white-box Top-1 for each of the thirteen transforms
from \file{src/exports/tifs_d6/d6_r18_plain.csv} and
\file{d6_mix_plain.csv} (400 queries, three seeds, the four trained transforms
and the eight held-out ones):

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Transform & ResNet18 & MixVPR \\
\midrule
none (untransformed) & $0.0058$ & $0.0008$ \\
\midrule
JPEG-75 & $0.0175$ & $0.5642$ \\
JPEG-50 & $0.0600$ & $0.7058$ \\
blur $\sigma{=}1$ & $0.0225$ & $0.5742$ \\
denoise & $0.0658$ & $0.5600$ \\
\midrule
JPEG-60 & $0.0333$ & $0.6725$ \\
JPEG-30 & $0.1117$ & $0.7017$ \\
median $3\times3$ & $0.0075$ & $0.3617$ \\
resize round trip & $0.0358$ & $0.6075$ \\
blur $\sigma{=}2$ & $0.0842$ & $0.5308$ \\
4 bits per channel & $\mathbf{0.0058}$ & $\mathbf{0.0067}$ \\
random choice & $0.0458$ & $0.5117$ \\
JPEG-50 then blur & $0.1017$ & $0.6900$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three separate defects follow.

\textbf{(a) The claim is false as stated.} Four-bit quantisation leaves the
white-box bound at $0.0058$ on ResNet18 --- identical to the untransformed
value to four decimals --- and at $0.0067$ on MixVPR against $0.0008$
untransformed. On both attackers, one of the thirteen operators recovers
essentially nothing. On ResNet18 the median filter recovers almost nothing
either ($0.0075$). ``Fragile to every operator'' is contradicted by the row
immediately below the ones the sentence quotes.

\textbf{(b) Each range is drawn from a different subset.} For ResNet18,
$0.0175$ is JPEG-75, one of the four transforms the hardening trains on, while
$0.1117$ is JPEG-30, one of the eight it never sees. Over the four trained
transforms the range is $0.0175$--$0.0658$; over all twelve non-identity
transforms it is $0.0058$--$0.1117$. For MixVPR, $0.3617$ is the median
filter, held out, while $0.7058$ is JPEG-50, trained; over the four the range
is $0.5600$--$0.7058$ and over all twelve it is $0.0067$--$0.7058$. Neither
quoted range is the extent of any coherent set.

\textbf{(c) The before/after comparison is not like for like.} The same
paragraph says hardening ``tightens the white-box bound from $0.36$--$0.71$ to
$0.0083$--$0.37$''. The second range \emph{is} reproducible --- it is the
hardened white-box over all thirteen transforms including the identity
($0.0083$--$0.3692$) --- so the sentence compares a range over one set with a
range over another. Like for like over the twelve non-identity transforms,
MixVPR goes from $0.0067$--$0.7058$ to $0.0175$--$0.3692$; over the four
trained transforms, from $0.5600$--$0.7058$ to $0.0200$--$0.1250$.

None of this touches a conclusion --- hardening does tighten the bound
substantially on every set --- but it is the one load-bearing sentence in the
manuscript I could not reproduce, and \S\ref{rev:R4} explains why the
apparatus did not catch it.

\action Rewrite the sentence to name one transform set and hold it fixed
across the before and the after. Name the exception: 4-bit quantisation
recovers nothing on either attacker, and the median filter recovers nothing on
ResNet18, which is itself worth a clause --- it says the white-box bound
survives quantisation and dies under smoothing and lossy coding, which is a
sharper statement than ``fragile to every operator''. Register every number in
the rewritten sentence with the auditor.

\acceptance Every white-box range in \S III-G names the transform set it is
taken over, reproduces from the released rows, and the ``fragile to every
operator'' claim either names its exceptions or is replaced.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The clean baseline is printed two ways, and the wrong one
is in the caption of the table that decides deployability}

\evidence
The unperturbed ResNet18 Top-1 on the 400-query, 2{,}000-image manifest is
$0.2100$. I recomputed it from
\file{src/exports/icme2027_placement_msls/cleanref/per_query.csv} (400 queries,
delivered MSE $2.5\times10^{-7}$): $0.2100$. It is also the value the
repository auditor registers for the wide-8 primary manifest, and the value the
manuscript itself uses twice --- \S III-D, ``no perturbation leaves Top-1 at
$0.2100$'', and \S III-E, ``drives Top-1 from a clean $0.210$ to
$\mathbf{0.0000}$''. The isotropic control at delivered MSE $15.68$ is
$0.1967$, which is what Table~III's first row prints and what the
abstract's $0.197\to0.032$ is paired against.

Two places call $0.197$ the clean value:

\begin{itemize}
\item Table~IV's caption: ``Clean Top-1 for this attacker is $0.197$.''
\item \S III-G: ``exactly one cell is admissible on its whole interval ---
isotropic noise at MSE $5.0$, which leaves Top-1 at $0.210$ against a clean
$0.197$, that is, does nothing.''
\end{itemize}

The second is worse than a slip. As written it asserts that adding noise
\emph{raises} retrieval above the clean level, and then calls that ``does
nothing'' --- an argument a referee will stop on. Corrected, the sentence is
cleaner than the original: isotropic noise at MSE $5.0$ leaves Top-1 at
$0.210$ against a clean $0.210$, which is exactly nothing, and the
admissibility verdict is unchanged.

Neither literal is registered with either checker: $0.197$ is one of the
manuscript values with no claim behind it, which is how a mislabelled baseline
survived in a table caption.

\action Correct Table~IV's caption to ``Clean Top-1 for this attacker is
$0.210$; the isotropic control at the operating point is $0.197$'' and fix the
\S III-G sentence. Register the clean baseline and the operating-point control
as separate claims so the two can never be swapped again. While there, check
the same distinction for the strong attacker: \S III-C and Table~III give
MixVPR a clean Top-1 of $0.79$ while $0.7800$ is the isotropic control, and the
abstract's $0.780\to0.732$ is likewise a control contrast, not a clean one ---
correct in substance, but a reader will take $0.780$ for the baseline unless
the abstract says which it is.

\acceptance No document calls $0.197$ or $0.780$ a clean value; both baselines
are registered claims.

\finding{R4}{Major}{The artifact claims complete table coverage, and the one
table it does not reach carries the claim that makes the defense deployable}

\evidence
Both checkers run clean here and that deserves saying first: the bundled
manifest verifies over 743 files, the one-command path completes in well under
a minute and reports 228 claims with 0 mismatches, and the repository auditor
reports 624 claims with 0 mismatched and 0 unverifiable. The counts in the
README match the checkers they name, which is an improvement I could confirm by
running both.

The coverage sentence is still wrong. The README says the auditor recomputes
``\textbf{624} --- every cell of every table in the manuscript and the
supplement, plus the prose values registered alongside them''. Enumerating the
registry's sources gives twelve files: \file{main.tex},
\file{supplementary.tex}, \file{supplementary_extended.tex} and nine generated
tables. \file{tab_sanitize.tex} is not among them. That table ---
Table~S5, thirteen transforms $\times$ two attackers $\times$ five
columns --- is registered with neither checker.

It is not an incidental table. It is the sole evidence for the sentence that
converts the direction axis from fragile into deployable: ``\emph{Hardened, all
sixteen held-out cells are significant}''. Without it, \S III-G's own finding
stands unanswered --- that unhardened, the direction fails outright on one
held-out transform against ResNet18 and three against MixVPR.

\emph{Correction, entered after the authors checked this.} My first reading of
the bundle was that the eight held-out transforms' rows were missing from it.
They are not: they sit in \file{results/transfer_table4/}, where all thirteen
sanitizers are columns of the same files rather than files of their own, which
is why a per-file listing did not show them. What is underneath is worse than
what I reported. \file{src/outputs/tifs_d6/} now exists holding only
\file{summary/*.json}, and the bundle's build resolves a tree to
\file{src/outputs/} whenever that directory exists at all --- so the next
rebuild would have shipped a \file{transfer_table4/} of two summary files and
silently dropped every per-query row behind Table~III and behind this table.
The naming does not help a referee find them either: that tree is glossed
``(Table~IV)'' in the layout section, and \file{tab:transfer} is Table~III.

Two smaller points in the same file. The scope note describes the preprocessing
family it covers as ``Non-adaptive preprocessing, three seeds per cell,
unhardened and under the gallery-free objective, and the same directions
hardened over those transforms (EOT)'', which reads as covering the study;
what it covers is the four trained transforms. And the coverage statistic ---
``of the manuscript's 213 distinct three- and four-decimal literals, 47 have no
claim'' --- is not computed by anything in the bundle or the repository. The
denominator reproduces exactly (I count 213). The numerator does not: matching
each literal against every claim's locator, printed form and rounded value
gives 36; matching against locators registered to \file{main.tex} alone gives
111. Whatever convention produced 47, a referee cannot reproduce it, and it is
the one number in an auditability artifact that nothing audits.

\action Register Table~S5's cells with the repository auditor; the rows are
already in \file{src/exports/tifs_d6/} and the recomputation is the one in
\S\ref{sec:verification}. Make the bundle's build source that tree from the
released exports rather than from whatever \file{src/outputs/} happens to
contain, and give it a reader-facing name so a referee can find the thirteen
transforms without knowing which file they are columns of. Replace the ``every cell of every table'' sentence with the
true statement and make the preprocessing scope note say ``the four transforms
the hardening trains on''. Turn the literal-coverage statistic into a printed
output of the auditor --- a \texttt{-{}-coverage} flag over
\file{main.tex} and \file{supplementary.tex} --- so the number in the README is
whatever the tool last printed.

\acceptance Running the auditor reports a claim count that includes
Table~S5's cells; the bundle sources its preprocessing tree from the released
exports and names it for what it holds; no sentence
in the README claims coverage the checkers do not have; the coverage statistic
is machine-generated.

\finding{R5}{Major}{Against the attacker built for the task the deployable
advantage does not survive one cheap countermeasure, and the two attackers that
would decide the claim were never subjected to one}

\evidence
The manuscript's qualifications are honest and I am not asking for new
honesty. I am asking for the experiment that follows from them.

Collecting what the paper reports against MixVPR, its only retriever actually
built for place recognition:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Condition & $\Delta$ Top-1 vs.\ isotropic & Interval \\
\midrule
untouched, unhardened & $-0.0483$ & $[-0.072,-0.026]$ \\
Top-10 instead of Top-1 & $-0.032$ & --- \\
JPEG-50 before embedding & $-0.0100$ & $[-0.029,+0.009]$ \\
purified, unhardened & $-0.0217$ & $[-0.057,+0.012]$ \\
purified, hardened & $-0.0233$ & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

I verified every row from \file{src/exports/purification/per_query_mixvpr.csv}
and \file{src/exports/tifs_d6/}. Two of the four countermeasure rows have
intervals covering zero. The paper says as much --- ``against a retriever built
for the task, an attacker willing to train an inverse takes back everything
this axis buys'' --- and then the abstract leads with ``optimised against
surrogates from the released frame alone, it transfers to held-out attackers
($0.197\to0.032$ and $0.780\to0.732$\dots)'' and the conclusion with ``it is
attainable on an axis this mechanism family does not optimise''. Both carry
their qualifications afterwards. A referee reads the leading sentence, and the
leading sentence generalises past the evidence.

The sharper problem is which attackers were tested. Purification and
preprocessing were run against ResNet18 and MixVPR only. The study contains two
further attackers, and they are exactly the two that would settle the question:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Patch-NetVLAD}, where the direction is by far the strongest
($0.4983\to0.3067$, place-clustered $-0.1917$ $[-0.232,-0.153]$; hardened
$-0.2125$) --- a genuine VPR architecture, stronger than ResNet18 and with a
larger effect than MixVPR. If the direction survives purification here, the
deployability claim is real and MixVPR is one hard case. If it does not, the
claim is confined to weak retrievers.
\item \textbf{ViT-B/16}, the only attacker whose trunk appears in no surrogate,
where the effect is $-0.0233$ $[-0.043,-0.005]$ --- already at the edge of
detectability before any countermeasure.
\end{itemize}

As it stands, the strongest attack in the paper was applied to the attacker
with the smallest effect among the VPR models and not to the one with the
largest, which a referee will read as the least informative allocation of the
purification budget.

\action Run the purifier --- the same residual denoiser, the same
place-disjoint splits, the same three draws --- and JPEG-50 against
Patch-NetVLAD and ViT-B/16, unhardened and hardened. The splits, releases,
denoiser and evaluation harness all exist; this is the smallest of the runs
requested here. Then rewrite the abstract's directional sentence and the
conclusion's ``it is attainable'' so the scope is in the leading clause rather
than in the qualifications: what the evidence supports is that the leverage
lies on the direction axis, and that a deployable advantage is measurable
against retrievers of the strength tested but is removed by a trained inverse
on the strongest of them.

\acceptance Purification and at least one preprocessing operator are reported
for all four attackers; the abstract and conclusion state the attacker class
the deployable claim holds for, in the sentence that makes the claim.

\finding{R6}{Moderate}{The placement the paper's own theory nominates is
measured on the primary benchmark, verified by the bundled checker, and
reported only for the secondary dataset}

\evidence
\file{src/exports/margin_oracle/per_query.csv} carries 4{,}800 rows: the
margin-gradient oracle, its anti-oracle, the score-gradient rule and the
uniform reference, ResNet18, three seeds, the same 400 queries as the primary
placement study. The bundled verifier checks it explicitly --- ``Placement by
the margin gradient: the strictly correct oracle, against uniform and against
its anti-oracle''.

Recomputing it against the uniform arm on the manuscript's own procedure
(seeds averaged within a query, 10{,}000-resample bootstrap over queries and
over the 277 places):

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Placement & Top-1 & $\Delta$ & query 95\% CI & place-clustered 95\% CI & $p$ \\
\midrule
uniform (ref.) & $0.1950$ & --- & --- & --- & --- \\
margin oracle & $0.1867$ & $-0.0083$ & $[-0.0267,+0.0092]$ & $[-0.0258,+0.0093]$ & $0.31$ \\
anti-margin oracle & $0.1942$ & $-0.0008$ & $[-0.0108,+0.0092]$ & $[-0.0115,+0.0094]$ & $0.87$ \\
score gradient & $0.1942$ & $-0.0008$ & $[-0.0183,+0.0158]$ & $[-0.0181,+0.0163]$ & $0.86$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three observations. It is the placement \S III-J identifies as the maximiser of
$v(w)$ under the energy constraint, so it is the one rule whose failure the
account most needs. It carries a top-decile energy share of $0.649$, so it is
genuinely concentrated. And at $-0.0083$ it is the \emph{largest favourable
point estimate any placement achieves on the weak attacker} --- eight times the
score-gradient rule's $-0.0008$, and the only rule whose estimate approaches
the $\pm0.01$ margin from the favourable side.

The manuscript reports it once, in a subordinate clause about KITTI-360:
``thirteen \dots\ the eight of Table~I plus two margin oracles and three
published-model priors''. Table~S2 prints its KITTI row. On MSLS --- the
primary benchmark, ten times the place count, the run that carries the paper's
central negative claim --- it is not reported at all, and \S III-B's
eight-rule family and \S III-C's seven comparisons both exclude it.

I do not think this was selective reporting: the KITTI table includes it, which
is not what concealment looks like. But a referee who runs the artifact will

\section{Assessment}

This is a careful paper and most of what I checked held. The protocol is the
contribution and it is a real one: holding \emph{delivered} distortion fixed
through the clamp rather than matching a nominal $\sigma$, deriving every
control from the same optimised perturbation, separating three axes instead of
comparing whole systems, and then applying all of it to the authors' own
testbed hard enough to expose that testbed's learned map as a constant. The
negative result travels with the qualifications that bound it, the positive one
with the four that limit it, and where the data go against the thesis --- edge
placement winning at high budgets, a purifier taking back everything on the
strong retriever --- the paper says so.

What holds it below the line is concentrated and, with one exception,
correctable without a computer. One sentence states a fact its own rows
contradict. One baseline is printed two ways, with the wrong one in the caption
of the table that decides deployability. The artifact whose purpose is to let a
referee check exactly these things still claims a coverage it does not have,
and the single table it cannot reach is the one carrying the claim that turns
the direction from fragile into deployable. Each of those is small on its own;
together, in a paper whose subject is auditing, they are the finding.

The exception is \S\ref{rev:R1}. The paper measures an optimised direction
against unoptimised placements and reports the difference as a fact about the
axes. Its own theory says the placement axis has a maximiser and declines to
solve for it; its own data show that placement stops being inert once the
budget grows. One optimised-allocation arm settles whether the contrast is
about axes or about effort, and I expect it to come out the paper's way --- in
which case the central claim gets materially stronger, and the paper should be
the one to say so.

\emph{Correction, entered after the authors ran it.} That expectation was
wrong, and the finding is better than the one I predicted. A map solved against
the attacker \emph{does} beat uniform --- $-0.039$ and $-0.094$ Top-1 on the two
attackers, on a noise field the optimiser never saw --- so ``allocation buys
nothing we can detect'' was false as written and the axis is not inert. What
survives, and survives on stronger footing, is the claim about reachability:
solved against surrogates alone the same optimiser buys $-0.006$ and $+0.006$,
nothing, at the budget that buys the direction $-0.165$ and $-0.048$. Both axes
hold leverage that needs the attacker; direction keeps most of its when that
access is withdrawn and allocation keeps none. That rests on matched search
budget as well as matched delivered distortion, which is what this finding
asked for. A third arm is worth the paper's space on its own: a map permitted
to see the noise realisation it will be released with reproduces the white-box
\emph{direction} attack to within $0.001$ and buys no privacy at all on an
independent draw, so an audit of this mechanism class has to state whether its
map may depend on the perturbation it weights.
