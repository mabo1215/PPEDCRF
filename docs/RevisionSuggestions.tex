\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}
\label{sec:basis}

This review was produced by a five-seat role-separated review panel
(journal-fit, methodology, domain, cross-perspective, devil's advocate) with a
separate editorial synthesis pass, run against the submission package as it
stands on the review date: \file{paper/main.tex} and the compiled
\file{paper/main.pdf} (13 pages), \file{paper/supplementary.tex} and
\file{paper/supplementary.pdf} (7 pages), \file{paper/ref.bib} (66 entries),
\file{paper/titlepage.pdf}, and every table source under
\file{paper/generated/}. Main body and supplement were read jointly as one
submission rather than as two documents.

No previous review round, progress file, or prior completion claim was used as
input. Every numerical statement below was re-derived from the manuscript
sources and the generated table sources in this repository; where the review
reports a mismatch, both conflicting values are quoted with their locations so
the authors can adjudicate without re-running anything. Venue requirements in
\S\ref{sec:venue} were re-verified against the IEEE Signal Processing Society
author guidance and the TIFS guidance for deep-learning submissions current at
the review date rather than carried forward from an earlier cycle.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: major revision.}

\subsection*{What is genuinely strong}

The panel records these as verified strengths, not as softening.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The protocol is a real contribution and is correctly
  motivated.} Holding \emph{delivered} distortion fixed --- solved per frame by
  bisection through the pixel clamp, Eq.~(S5) --- rather than nominal weight
  energy retires a clipping confound that a nominal match leaves in place. That
  is the right control, and the manuscript states why it is the right control.
\item \textbf{The mechanism's own defect is disclosed, not buried.} The
  released checkpoint's unary sigmoid spans $[0.4991,0.5015]$ at spatial
  coefficient of variation $6\times10^{-4}$ with support coverage $1.000$. A
  paper that reported a favourable privacy--utility curve from this checkpoint
  would have been publishable and wrong; the manuscript says so in
  \S\ref{sec:basis}'s terms and treats the degeneracy as a finding about
  evaluation practice.
\item \textbf{Self-falsification is carried through rather than avoided.} The
  solved-map arm reverses the paper's earlier reading and is reported as such;
  the same-field solved map is identified as sign selection under another name
  and excluded; the equivalence tally names its own self-comparison; the
  known-Jacobian calibration is reported as \emph{not} closing
  ($-0.15$ predicted against $-0.0008\pm0.01$ measured).
\item \textbf{Reproducibility infrastructure is above the norm for this
  venue.} Per-condition seeds, per-family GPU and wall-clock accounting,
  library versions pinned to the patch level, and per-query exports that
  recompute every table without imagery or a GPU.
\item \textbf{Adaptive-attacker discipline is unusually careful.} Identifying
  that Top-1 selection manufactures a null at a floored metric, and reporting
  the fixed-index attacker as a damaged attacker rather than as evidence, are
  both correct and both cost the paper something.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Why the decision is nonetheless major revision}

Four findings are decision-bearing and none is cosmetic. The central negative
claim is established on an attacker panel that structurally excludes the
attackers on which the paper's own positive allocation result appears
(\S\ref{rev:R1}); the abstract states that null at a confidence the printed
intervals do not carry, by the manuscript's own verdict column
(\S\ref{rev:R2}); no published mechanism from the audited family is run
end-to-end under the protocol (\S\ref{rev:R3}); and the threat model excludes
the attacker class the manuscript's own related work names as where the field
has moved (\S\ref{rev:R4}). Each is repairable, three of the four mostly by
re-embedding and rewriting rather than by new optimisation, which is why this
is a revision and not a rejection.

\subsection*{Panel provenance and calibration status}

Disclosed so the weight of this review is not overstated. The five seats are
role-separated, not independent: they share one model family and one execution
substrate, so their agreement is not evidence of independent error processes.
Seat separation is a division of reviewing angle, nothing more. This review
declares \texttt{NOT\_CALIBRATED}: no empirical target profile matched to this
domain, article type, venue criteria, and reviewer configuration exists, so no
judgement below should be read as a calibrated accuracy claim. Judgements are
criterion-bound categorical labels and are deliberately not converted to
points, totals, or a ranking.

\section{Findings}
\label{sec:findings}

\finding{R1}{Major}{The placement null and the direction result are measured on
different attacker panels, and the two attackers on which allocation
demonstrably works were never run against a single prescribed placement rule}

\evidence
The manuscript's thesis is a contrast between two axes. For that contrast to be
about the axes, both must be read by the same attackers. They are not.

\begin{itemize}
\item The per-rule placement study on the primary place-labelled manifest is
  reported against exactly two attackers: ResNet18
  (\file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex}) and MixVPR
  (\file{tab\_placement\_mixvpr.tex}).
\item \S III of the manuscript lists the six backbones of the placement study
  as ResNet18, ResNet50, VGG16, CosPlace, MixVPR and Patch-NetVLAD. CLIP
  ViT-L/14 and ViT-B/16 appear nowhere in any placement study --- they enter
  the paper only in \S IV-E as additional \emph{directional} attackers.
\item The directional claim spans five attackers, and the solved-map curve
  (\file{tab\_alloc\_curve.tex}) spans the same five.
\item In that curve, the two attackers that separate from zero earliest and
  most strongly are Patch-NetVLAD ($-0.0183$ at ten steps, $-0.0483$ at eighty)
  and CLIP ViT-L/14 ($-0.0300$ at ten, $-0.0583$ at eighty). Those are
  precisely the two attackers against which no prescribed placement rule was
  ever run on this manifest: CLIP was never in the placement panel at all, and
  Patch-NetVLAD has no per-rule table.
\end{itemize}

The consequence is not hypothetical. \S IV-B's argument is that allocation is
inert \emph{as prescribed} and live \emph{when solved}; but the only evidence
that prescribed rules are inert comes from ResNet18 and MixVPR, which are also
the two attackers on which the solved map fails ($-0.0058$ and $+0.0058$ at
twenty steps). On the two attackers where allocation is shown to be live, the
prescribed-versus-solved comparison has never been made. A referee is entitled
to read the axis asymmetry as partly an attacker-panel asymmetry, and the
manuscript currently supplies nothing that closes that reading. The devil's
advocate seat records this as the single strongest counter-argument available
against the paper's thesis.

This is cheap to close. Prescribed placements involve no optimisation: the
released frames for each rule already exist at matched delivered energy, and
extending the panel is a re-embedding and a re-ranking, not a new search.

\action
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the seven prescribed placement rules, plus the margin-gradient rule
  and its inverse, against Patch-NetVLAD, ViT-B/16 and CLIP ViT-L/14 on the
  same 400-query, 2{,}000-image manifest, three seeds, at the same delivered
  distortion and under the same energy gate.
\item Report them in the same table format as
  \file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex}, with both units of inference and the same
  verdict column, so the five-attacker placement panel and the five-attacker
  direction panel can be read side by side.
\item If any prescribed rule separates from zero on CLIP or Patch-NetVLAD,
  the abstract's claim must be restated as attacker-conditional. If none does,
  say so explicitly --- that strengthens the paper considerably, because the
  contrast then holds on a common panel.
\item In \S III, state the attacker panel per axis in one sentence, so a reader
  can see which attackers carry which claim without reconstructing it from
  three sections.
\end{enumerate}

\acceptance
A single table in which every axis contrast quoted in the abstract is reported
on the same five attackers, or an explicit and prominent statement of which
attackers each axis claim is restricted to.

\finding{R2}{Major}{The abstract states the placement null at a confidence the
printed intervals do not carry, and the manuscript's own verdict column says so}

\evidence
The abstract reads: ``no proposed allocation rule buys anything: over seven
energy-matched placements, three published-model placements and four operators,
every difference is within $\pm0.012$ Top-1 and no interval favours one.''

\S IV-B says something materially weaker, and says it correctly: ``by the
interval rule two earn \emph{negligible} and five \emph{no benefit detected}
\ldots One of the two negligible verdicts is the mechanism's own map, which at
this checkpoint \emph{is} the uniform control, so exactly one non-degenerate
cell meets the pre-registered margin and the remaining five are limited by what
$400$ query clusters resolve rather than by how small the effect is.''

The verdict column of \file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex} confirms the weaker
reading: two rows read \texttt{negligible} (one being the degenerate
self-comparison, whose signed-rank test runs on a single discordant pair) and
seven read \texttt{none det.} The manuscript also states, correctly, that ``the
smallest margin certifying all seven at once is $\pm0.027$'' --- nearly three
times the declared margin.

So the package contains, at its primary benchmark, exactly one non-degenerate
certified equivalence. ``No proposed allocation rule buys anything'' is a
statement of absence; the evidence supports bounded non-detection at a margin
of $\pm0.027$ and certified negligibility in one cell. This is the classic
absence-of-evidence-for-evidence-of-absence substitution, and it is
particularly visible here \emph{because} \S IV-B already resists it. The
abstract, the contributions list (``no prescribed rule beats uniform
spreading'') and the conclusion (``Allocation over pixels buys nothing any
proposed rule can reach'') are not calibrated to the body.

No formal equivalence test is run anywhere in the package. The interval rule is
a reasonable substitute but is not the standard instrument, and a TIFS
methodology referee will ask for the standard instrument.

\action
\begin{enumerate}
\item Rewrite the abstract's allocation sentence in the manuscript's own
  verdict vocabulary. A faithful form: ``across nine energy-matched placements,
  three published-model placements and four operators, every point estimate
  lies within $\pm0.012$ Top-1; one non-degenerate cell is certified negligible
  at the pre-declared $\pm0.01$ margin and the rest return no detected benefit
  at a certifying margin of $\pm0.027$.''
\item Apply the same correction to the contributions list and to the first
  sentence of the conclusion.
\item Run two one-sided tests (TOST) at the declared $\pm0.01$ margin for every
  placement contrast and print the TOST $p$ beside the existing verdict, so the
  equivalence claim rests on a named procedure rather than on an interval rule
  the paper defines itself.
\item State, in \S III, the number of query clusters required to certify
  $\pm0.01$ at the observed paired variance. The manuscript already implies the
  answer is well above 400; printing it converts a limitation into a design
  specification for anyone repeating the audit.
\end{enumerate}

\acceptance
Abstract, contributions and conclusion state a bounded non-detection rather
than an absence; a TOST column exists for every placement contrast; the
required-$n$ figure is printed.

\finding{R3}{Major}{No published mechanism from the audited family is run
end-to-end under the protocol}

\evidence
The paper audits ``visual location-privacy mechanisms'' and concludes about
``the rules that have been proposed.'' What is actually run is:

\begin{itemize}
\item PPEDCRF, built by the authors as a testbed, whose released checkpoint is
  numerically constant and whose second, mask-supervised checkpoint has
  \emph{negative} attribution agreement with attacker sensitivity (Spearman
  $-0.00024$). Neither checkpoint ever implemented the family's core
  hypothesis, which is that a learned map finds the privacy-relevant pixels.
\item Six prescribed priors the authors wrote (uniform, saliency, edge, centre,
  fixed random, score-gradient and its inverse) plus two margin oracles.
\item Three placements derived from published segmentation models --- which the
  manuscript itself reports concentrate only $0.113$--$0.126$ of their energy in
  their top decile, against $0.100$ for uniform. These are near-uniform maps;
  as \emph{tests of concentration} they carry almost no signal, whatever they
  say about the ``perturb the background'' strategy.
\item Mask-guided adversarial protection, where \S IV-B states plainly: ``this
  adapts the formulation rather than reimplementing either'' of Le et al.\ or
  GeoShield.
\end{itemize}

So the strongest evidence against the family is an audit of the authors' own
construction, and the closest approach to a published method is an adaptation.
The manuscript's defence --- that using its own mechanism lets it report a
negative result ``without misrepresenting anyone else's'' --- is a legitimate
ethical instinct, but it does not substitute for the missing evidence, and a
domain referee will not accept a family-level negative claim supported only by
a straw testbed that the paper itself shows was broken.

The mask-guided arm is the most convincing part of this section precisely
because it is the only one touching a published formulation, and it produces a
clean result ($+0.063$ Top-1 at a tenth coverage, $+0.057$/$+0.059$ for
alternative rules at fixed coverage). That arm should be promoted, extended,
and described as what it is.

\action
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reimplement at least one published mechanism from the audited family
  end-to-end --- GeoShield is the natural candidate since its code and
  formulation are public --- and run it through the protocol at matched
  delivered distortion against the five-attacker panel of \S\ref{rev:R1}.
  Report it as its own subsection, not as an adaptation footnote.
\item If that is not feasible within the revision window, then bound the claim
  explicitly and everywhere: the title, abstract and conclusion must say that
  the audited rules are the enumerated ones, and the enumeration must be
  printed once in a table listing every placement rule with its provenance
  (authors' own / published segmentation model / adapted from a published
  mechanism).
\item Promote the mask-guided comparison (currently Supplement
  \S S-mask-guided) into the main body, since it is the only arm that tests a
  published formulation under the protocol.
\item State once, near the top of \S II, that the testbed is an instrument
  rather than a proposal, and that the family-level reading depends on the
  published-model and mask-guided arms rather than on it.
\end{enumerate}

\acceptance
Either one published mechanism audited end-to-end, or a scope statement in
title, abstract and conclusion that restricts the claim to the enumerated rules,
plus the provenance table.

\finding{R4}{Major}{The threat model excludes image-to-GPS geolocation, which
the manuscript's own related work identifies as where the risk now sits}

\evidence
Every attacker in the package retrieves from a gallery of at most 2{,}000
images. \S I concedes that ``the image-to-location models that emit coordinates
directly are admitted by the threat model and not reached by this evidence,''
and \S I-A cites GeoCLIP, PIGEON, granular geo-privacy and a 2026 doxing
analysis as the attacker side of the setting.

For a 2026 TIFS submission this is the decision-bearing gap in the threat model,
and the manuscript has already argued the case against itself. The paper's
mechanism claim is a claim about \emph{where perturbation energy should be
spent to defeat a location inference}. A retrieval attacker decides by
nearest-neighbour rank in a gallery; a geolocator regresses or classifies a
coordinate with no gallery at all. There is no reason internal to the paper's
own margin analysis --- which is entirely about the correct-versus-hardest-negative
retrieval margin --- to expect the direction/allocation asymmetry to carry
across that change. The conclusion admits it: ``the geolocators this literature
increasingly targets \ldots remain untested.''

A referee will read the paper as auditing a receding attacker class while the
current one goes unmeasured. That is survivable only if at least a bracketing
measurement exists.

\action
\begin{enumerate}
\item Add one geolocator arm. Run the deployable gallery-free direction, the
  isotropic control at matched delivered MSE, and at least three prescribed
  placement rules against a public image-to-GPS model (GeoCLIP is the lowest
  cost; a PIGEON-class model is stronger) on the same MSLS query frames.
\item Report geodesic error rather than Top-$k$: median error in kilometres
  plus accuracy at the standard 1\,km / 25\,km / 200\,km thresholds, with the
  same paired bootstrap and place clustering used elsewhere.
\item Report the result whichever way it falls. If direction transfers to a
  geolocator, the paper's central positive claim gains its most important
  external validation. If it does not, that is a first-class finding and
  belongs in the abstract, because it would bound the protocol's own
  applicability.
\item Whatever the outcome, move the scope sentence out of \S I's small print
  and into the abstract.
\end{enumerate}

\acceptance
One geolocator arm with geodesic-error reporting on both axes, or --- if the
authors decline the experiment --- an abstract-level statement that every
result is conditional on a gallery-based attacker, accompanied by a reasoned
argument for why the margin analysis should or should not be expected to carry.

\finding{R5}{Major}{The inference supporting the headline null is declared
exploratory and left uncorrected, no equivalence test is run, and the test
chosen is not justified for the outcome type}

\evidence
Four separate issues, all in the same statistical layer.

\textbf{(a) The confirmatory family excludes the headline claim.} \S III
states: ``The primary family is the four direction contrasts against the
isotropic control, two attackers by two access levels, Holm-corrected; every
other test is exploratory and reported as such.'' The paper's title question and
its abstract's first substantive claim are about \emph{allocation}. So the
manuscript's headline claim is carried entirely by a family it has designated
exploratory and left uncorrected, while the correction is spent on the
secondary claim. Multiplicity control that protects the claim you were already
confident about, and not the one your title asks, is inverted.

\textbf{(b) No equivalence test.} The $\pm0.01$ margin is applied by inspecting
whether a bootstrap interval falls inside it. That is a defensible informal
rule, but TOST is the named procedure and costs nothing to add. See
\S\ref{rev:R2}.

\textbf{(c) The test is not justified for the outcome.} Top-1 is a binary
outcome per query. The protocol averages three seeds within a query and then
runs a Wilcoxon signed-rank over 400 such averaged differences. The resulting
differences take few distinct values and are overwhelmingly zero: the discordant
counts printed in \file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex} run from 1 to 44 out of 400.
A signed-rank test on a variable with three or four support points and
$\ge 89\%$ ties is outside the setting the test is normally defended in, and
the interval widths that carry the equivalence verdicts are driven by the
handful of discordant queries rather than by the 277 places. The narrow
``negligible'' verdict for the anti-score-gradient rule
($[-0.003,+0.007]$, nine discordant pairs) is a good example: that interval is
narrow because almost every query is a tie, not because 400 clusters resolved
the effect.

\textbf{(d) An $n$ that contradicts the declared unit of inference.} \S IV-B
states each comparison ``rests on 1,200 paired observations over 400 query
clusters, an order of magnitude more than the proxy affords.'' \S III states
that the three seeds are averaged within a query before pairing, so the paired
test runs on 400 differences, not 1,200. The sentence as printed overstates the
effective sample by a factor of three.

\action
\begin{enumerate}
\item Re-designate the placement family as confirmatory and apply Holm (or
  Benjamini--Hochberg, stated either way) within it. Report the corrected
  $p$-values in \file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex} and
  \file{tab\_placement\_mixvpr.tex}. Keep the direction family corrected as it
  is now.
\item Add TOST at the declared margin for every placement contrast.
\item Complement, or replace, the signed-rank test with a procedure suited to a
  clustered binary outcome: a mixed-effects logistic model with random
  intercepts for place and for seed, or an exact permutation test that permutes
  condition labels within place. Report the model-based contrast alongside the
  existing one; if they agree, the result is stronger than it currently looks.
\item Correct the ``1,200 paired observations'' sentence to the 400 differences
  the declared unit of inference produces, and state the seed averaging in the
  same sentence so the two sections cannot be read apart.
\item In the table caption, retain the discordant-pair count --- it is good
  practice and rare --- and add one clause explaining that a row with a small
  count is reporting an untested absence, which \S IV-B already says in prose.
\end{enumerate}

\acceptance
Placement family corrected for multiplicity; TOST column present; a
clustered-binary model or permutation test reported alongside the signed-rank
result; the $n$ statement consistent with \S III.

\finding{R6}{Moderate}{Nine placement contrasts are reported as seven, and one
quoted interval matches neither column of the table it is drawn from}

\evidence
\textbf{(a) The count.} \file{tab\_placement\_msls.tex} carries nine non-reference
rows: learned support, score-gradient, anti-score-gradient, saliency, centre
bias, fixed random, edge magnitude, margin gradient, anti-margin gradient. The
manuscript says ``no placement differs significantly in any of the seven
comparisons'' and the abstract says ``over seven energy-matched placements.''
The two excluded rows are the margin-gradient rule and its inverse --- the rules
the paper's own \S IV-H analysis nominates --- and they are discussed in a
separate paragraph. The margin-gradient contrast, at $-0.0083$, is the largest
negative placement effect on the primary manifest, larger than anything in the
seven. Segregating the theory-nominated rules out of the headline tally and
into their own paragraph is exactly the pattern a referee reads as favourable
partition, even where, as here, both numbers are printed honestly a few lines
apart.

\textbf{(b) The interval.} \S IV-B states the widest interval among the seven
``reaching $[-0.008,+0.026]$.'' In the table, centre bias --- the widest row ---
reads $[-0.007,+0.026]$ query-level and $[-0.008,+0.027]$ place-clustered. The

\section{Assessment}
\label{sec:assessment}

\textbf{Decision: major revision.}

The reasoning, stated against named criteria rather than against a total.

\textbf{Why not accept or minor revision.} Three criteria are unresolved in a
way that a copyedit cannot reach. Evidence sufficiency fails outright: the
table backing an abstract-level claim is in neither submitted document, and
thirteen pointers defer evidence outside the package. Methodological rigor is
partly met but the headline claim is carried by an uncorrected family the paper
itself calls exploratory, with no equivalence test anywhere. And the axis
contrast the title poses is currently measured on two different attacker
panels, in a configuration where the excluded attackers are precisely the ones
on which the paper's own positive allocation result appears. Any one of these
would be a major-revision item; together they mean a referee cannot presently
verify the paper's central claim from the paper.

\textbf{Why not reject.} None of the three is a design flaw. The instrumentation
that would close them already exists: released per-query exports, a documented
energy gate, matched-delivered-distortion machinery, and a five-attacker panel
already built for the other axis. Eight of the fourteen findings are writing or
packaging; three more are re-analysis from shipped exports; R1 is a
re-embedding. Only the geolocator arm, and the published-mechanism
reimplementation in its stronger form, are new experiments, and the second has
a legitimate bounded alternative. A single revision cycle can reach all of it.

\textbf{The devil's advocate position, adjudicated.} The strongest argument
against this paper is that its axis asymmetry is an artifact of unequal
measurement effort --- the direction arm gets five attackers, twenty optimiser
steps and a hardening variant; the prescribed placement arm gets two attackers
and no search. The manuscript has already conceded half of this itself, in the
search-budget curve that shows allocation reaching three of five attackers at
eighty steps and still climbing. This is recorded as a validated CRITICAL
challenge, and it is why R1 is the first finding rather than the fifth: until
both axes are read by the same attackers, the paper's title question is not
answered on a common footing. It is not a fatal challenge, because closing it
costs only re-embedding, and because the direction arm's advantage on the two
attackers where it has been measured against a solved map is roughly an order
of magnitude, which no plausible panel correction erases.

\textbf{What the paper will be if the revision lands.} A protocol paper with a
negative result that survives on a common attacker panel, a positive result
honestly bounded by four qualifications the authors found themselves, and a
measurement of what an audited family actually buys. That is a contribution
TIFS should want. The manuscript's habit of reporting the thing that damages
its own thesis --- the degenerate map, the unclosed calibration, the solved map
that reverses its earlier reading, the utility cost that exceeds its own
tolerance --- is the reason this review recommends revision rather than
rejection, and the revision should not sand any of it away.

\textbf{Suggested order of work.} R9 and R10 first, since they are cheap and
they free page budget and close the compliance surface. Then R2, R5, R6, R7,
R8 and R12, which are re-analysis and packaging over exports that already
exist. Then R1, the re-embedding, which is the finding most likely to change
what the paper claims. Then R4 and R3, the new experimental work, whose results
should inform the final abstract. R11, R13 and R14 fold into the final writing
pass.
