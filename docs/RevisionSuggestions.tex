\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This is a fresh review of the current submission package against the IEEE TIFS
author requirements and the standards a TIFS referee would apply. It does not
use any previous review file, the project's progress notes, or any earlier
completion judgment as input. Every statement below was checked against the
sources, the rendered PDFs and, where a claim needed testing, against the
released per-query exports recomputed independently.

The reviewed main source is \file{paper/main.tex}, SHA-256 prefix
\file{41e1636f77a76b93}; its PDF has 13 pages. The reviewed
\file{paper/supplementary.tex}, prefix \file{c33efa1b39936732}, has six pages.
The bibliography \file{paper/ref.bib} (prefix \file{cca4491876ea1723}) holds 63
entries, 47 cited. Eight tables are generated from exports.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: minor revision.} The package is in good order
and the previous cycle's integrity defects are genuinely fixed: cross-document
pointers resolve, the manuscript carries no literal supplement numbers and a
build-time check now guards that class, the significance marker on Table~I's
pooled cell is gone, and the proxy-versus-real calibration prints its manifest,
backbone and gallery. A second retrieval dataset has been added, which answers
the strongest objection a referee had.

That addition, however, is where this review's principal finding lies, and it
is a serious one. \textbf{The second dataset's headline positive result is
reported under a unit of inference the manuscript's own protocol rejects for
exactly this situation, and it does not survive the unit the protocol
prescribes.} Recomputed from the released rows, the direction contrast on
KITTI-360 is $-0.0543$ with a query-level interval of $[-0.095,-0.013]$ but a
place-clustered interval of $[-0.135,+0.023]$, which covers zero. The
manuscript states that this benchmark was run under ``the same protocol, energy
gate and unit of inference'' as the MSLS arms; the first two are true and the
third is not. Two further defects sit beside it: two placement conditions were
run and are absent from the table that reports that arm, and the new
benchmark's most consequential limitation --- sixteen places behind 227
queries --- is disclosed in the supplement but not where the claim is made.

None of this touches the allocation null, which is the paper's central result:
recomputed under place clustering, no placement on either dataset is
significant and the largest difference is still inside the $\pm0.012$ the
manuscript quotes. The negative result is robust to the unit of inference. It
is the new positive claim that is not, and the fix is to report it honestly
rather than to withdraw it.

\subsection{Summary of the work}
The paper asks whether visual location-privacy mechanisms buy protection
through \emph{where} a bounded distortion budget is spent or through \emph{where
the perturbation points}, and answers it with a protocol that holds delivered
distortion fixed while varying one axis at a time. Applied to a CRF-inspired
selective sanitizer whose released map turns out to be numerically constant, it
finds that no placement rule beats uniform spreading at the low-distortion
operating point --- across eight placement rules, three published-model
placements, two gradient-guided placements and four operators, on a
place-labelled MSLS subset against a weak and a strong retriever, every
difference within $\pm0.012$ Top-1 --- while a surrogate-optimised direction
transfers to four held-out attackers at the same distortion. The paper
decomposes the direction effect, measures its downstream cost over four budgets
against two motivated tolerances, repeats the utility measurement on the frames
retrieval is scored on, audits the serialized release, evaluates two adaptive
attackers, offers a conditional first-order account checked against a
controlled model with a known Jacobian, and now repeats both central contrasts
on a second dataset built from KITTI-360 revisits.

\subsection{Strengths worth preserving}
\begin{itemize}
\item The delivered-MSE matching solved per frame through the pixel clamp, the
energy-conservation gate, and the constant-map diagnostic remain reusable
contributions independent of this paper's conclusions.
\item The allocation null is now established on two datasets, two mechanism
checkpoints, six attacker backbones, four operators and three published-model
priors, and it survives recomputation under a clustered unit of inference. That
is a well-defended negative result.
\item The second dataset's construction is documented down to the choices that
failed, including that a cross-traversal split was impossible and that a single
global time cut retains five or six places out of the 274 the data contains.
Recording the dead ends is unusual and valuable.
\item The verification instrumentation is exceptional for this venue: 379
claims recomputed from released exports, a cross-document reference checker,
and export trees slimmed so a fresh clone can reproduce every table.
\item Self-qualification is consistent: high-budget placement gains that cut
against the thesis, a tolerance at which the paper's own operating point is not
affordable, and a frank statement that most transfer strength depends on the
attacker sharing a trunk with a surrogate.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item Initial regular papers: at most 13 double-column pages in 10-point type,

\item Abstract 150--250 words, self-contained, without abbreviations,

\item Single-anonymized review; ORCIDs for all authors; submission metadata

\item Supplementary material recommended to stay within six double-column

\item Originality, related-work and prior-submission disclosure.
\end{itemize}

submissions~\cite{tifsdl}, and raises that expectation for negative results
challenging published work~\cite{tifsscope}.

\section{Major findings and required revisions}

\finding{R1}{Critical}{The second dataset's direction result is reported under
the unit of inference the paper's own protocol rejects, and does not survive
the one it prescribes}
\evidence
The manuscript's protocol section is explicit about why the query is not a safe
unit: ``Queries are not independent: the 400 queries of the eight-city manifest
carry 277 distinct place identities under the dataset's own clustering, and one
place backs twelve of them, so for the direction contrasts we also report a
bootstrap that resamples \emph{places} and carries every query of a sampled
place.'' Every MSLS direction contrast in the paper duly carries a
place-clustered interval.

The KITTI-360 benchmark has 227 queries drawn from \textbf{16} places, which is
$14.2$ queries per place against $1.44$ on the eight-city MSLS manifest. The
dependence the protocol was written to handle is therefore an order of
magnitude worse on the new dataset --- and the new dataset is the only place in
the paper where a direction contrast is reported \emph{without} a
place-clustered interval.

Recomputed from \file{src/exports/kitti360\_rows/direction.csv} with the
paper's own clustering procedure (10{,}000 resamples over places, carrying
every query of a sampled place):
\begin{itemize}
\item point estimate $-0.0543$, identical either way;
\item query-level 95\% interval $[-0.0954,-0.0132]$, which is what the
manuscript prints;
\item \textbf{place-clustered 95\% interval $[-0.1352,+0.0234]$, which covers
zero}, and is $1.93\times$ wider.
\end{itemize}
The manuscript states that the benchmark was run ``under the same protocol,
energy gate and unit of inference'' as the MSLS arms. The first two hold; the
third does not, and the clause conceals precisely the difference that decides
the claim. On the stated evidence a referee cannot conclude that direction
survives the change of dataset --- only that it does so under an analysis the
paper elsewhere argues against.

This does not touch the allocation half. Recomputed under place clustering, no
placement on KITTI-360 is significant and the largest difference is $+0.0117$,
still inside the $\pm0.012$ the manuscript quotes. The null replicates under
either unit; only the positive claim is unit-dependent.

\action
Report the place-clustered interval for the KITTI-360 direction contrast
wherever that contrast is quoted, and state what it shows: the point estimate
matches MSLS in sign and rough size, the query-level interval excludes zero and
the place-clustered interval does not, because 227 queries come from 16 places.
Then weaken the sentence that reads ``both contrasts survive the change of
dataset'' to what is supported --- the allocation null survives under either
unit, the direction effect is directionally consistent but not separated from
zero once query dependence is accounted for. Remove ``the same \dots\ unit of
inference'' from the description of the benchmark, or make it true by leading
with the clustered interval. If a stronger claim is wanted, it needs a
benchmark with more places, not a different bootstrap.

\acceptance
Every direction contrast in the paper carries an interval computed on the unit
the protocol section declares, and no sentence claims the second dataset
confirms an effect that its clustered interval does not separate from zero.

\finding{R2}{Major}{Two placement conditions were run and are absent from the
table that reports that arm}
\evidence
\file{src/exports/kitti360\_rows/placement.csv} contains thirteen placement
conditions: \file{uniform}, \file{learned}, \file{oracle\_grad},
\file{anti\_oracle\_grad}, \file{saliency}, \file{center},
\file{random\_fixed}, \file{edge}, \file{segmentation},
\file{segmentation\_fcn}, \file{segmentation\_ade}, \file{margin\_oracle} and
\file{anti\_margin\_oracle}. The generated table reports eleven of them. The
two omitted are \file{margin\_oracle} and \file{anti\_margin\_oracle}, and the
manuscript describes the arm as ``eleven energy-matched placements'', matching
the table rather than the run.

The omission does not change a conclusion --- recomputed, \file{margin\_oracle}
is $+0.0117$ $[-0.011,+0.036]$ and \file{anti\_margin\_oracle} is $-0.0029$
$[-0.013,+0.004]$, both inside the equivalence margin and neither significant
--- but \file{margin\_oracle} ties for the \emph{largest} absolute difference
in the whole arm. Dropping the joint-largest cell from a table whose argument
is that no cell is large is exactly the pattern a referee is trained to look
for, and the paper cannot afford the appearance of selective reporting in the
one section that is new.

The claim auditor cannot catch this: it verifies the values that are declared
to it, and an undeclared row is invisible. Two conditions were run, two were
never registered, and 379 of 379 claims still verified.

\action
Report all thirteen conditions in the table and correct the count in the
manuscript. If space genuinely forbids two more rows, state in the caption that
two further placements were run with the results given in the extended report,
and give their values there --- but the honest default is to print them, since
they cost two lines and one of them is the arm's joint-largest effect. Add a
check that every condition present in an export appears in the table generated
from it, so a silently dropped row fails a build rather than passing an audit.

\acceptance
The number of placements the manuscript claims equals the number in the export,
and a mechanical check would fail if a condition were dropped again.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The new benchmark's decisive limitation is disclosed in the
supplement but not where the claim is made}
\evidence
The supplement states that the KITTI-360 benchmark has 16 places and that a
place is a stretch of road rather than a $25$\,m ball. The manuscript's
paragraph gives ``227 queries, 2{,}000-image gallery, three seeds'' and does
not mention the place count at all.

Sixteen places is not a detail: it is the reason R1 exists, it is what makes
the place-clustered interval nearly twice as wide, and it is the single number
a referee needs in order to judge how much the replication is worth. A reader
of the manuscript alone sees a 227-query benchmark and a $p=0.006$ and has no
way to know that the effective sample for the paper's own preferred unit is
sixteen.

The same paragraph carries the ``same \dots\ unit of inference'' clause that
R1 addresses, so the two corrections belong in one edit.

\action
Put the place count in the manuscript sentence that introduces the benchmark,
next to the query count, and say what follows from it: with 16 places the
place-clustered interval is the binding one and it is wide. One clause.

\acceptance
The manuscript states the number of places wherever it states the number of
queries for this benchmark.

\finding{R4}{Moderate}{The abstract does not mention that there are now two
datasets}
\evidence
The abstract says ``On a real place-labelled benchmark, against weak and strong
attackers \dots'', singular, and never mentions the second dataset. Since
single-dataset evidence was the most obvious objection to a negative result of
this kind, and the paper has now answered it, the abstract is understating the
work --- the opposite of the usual problem, but still a mismatch between the
abstract and the body.

Given R1, the honest form is narrow: the allocation null replicates on a second
dataset. That is the part that survives under either unit of inference, and it
is the paper's central claim.

\action
Add a clause to the abstract stating that the allocation null replicates on a
second place-labelled dataset. Do not claim the direction result replicates
until R1 is resolved in a way that supports it. The abstract is at 246 words
against a 250 limit, so this needs four words of room from elsewhere.

\acceptance
The abstract mentions the second dataset, and claims for it exactly what the
clustered analysis supports.

\finding{R5}{Moderate}{The allocation null now rests on two datasets but still
on no independently trained sanitizer}
\evidence
This was raised in the previous cycle and the manuscript's response --- naming
the class the null covers in the conclusion --- is accurate and sufficient to
prevent over-claiming. It is repeated here only because the second dataset
changes its weight rather than resolving it: the placement set on KITTI-360 is
the same family of hand-specified priors, gradient maps, published segmentation
priors and this paper's own two checkpoints. Two datasets now test the same
eleven or thirteen rules; no independently trained selective sanitizer has been
put through the protocol on either.

A referee who accepts the conclusion's scoping sentence will not treat this as
blocking. One who reads the title as a claim about the design family will.

\action
No change is required to what is written. If an additional experiment is
affordable, one third-party released sanitizer under the protocol would convert
the scoping sentence into a stronger claim, and the mask-guided baseline shows
the machinery already exists.

\acceptance
Unchanged, or one released third-party mechanism evaluated.

\finding{R6}{Minor}{Claim coverage is stated more broadly than it is}
\evidence
The verification instrumentation is a genuine strength and the numbers are
real: 379 claims, all verified, every table cell in both documents covered. The
limit is worth keeping visible. Of the 190 distinct three- and four-decimal
literals in the manuscript, 73 have no claim behind them --- mostly interval
endpoints quoted in prose beside a covered point estimate, but also, as R2
shows, anything that was never declared. The auditor is a registry, not a
sweep, and its clean report is evidence about what was declared rather than
about the manuscript as a whole.

\action
Keep the tool and its claim as they are, but where the released package
describes what the checker guarantees, say that it covers every tabulated
value and the registered prose claims, not every printed number. Consider a
mode that lists manuscript literals with no claim, so the gap is visible to
whoever next adds a number.

\acceptance
No document or artifact README asserts coverage broader than the registry
provides.

\finding{R7}{Minor}{Sixteen uncited bibliography entries}
\evidence
Sixteen of 63 entries are cited nowhere. Uncited entries do not print and are
not a compliance defect. Two of them, Tokyo~24/7 and Oxford RobotCar, were the
datasets the previous cycle proposed for the replication; the manuscript used
KITTI-360 instead, for reasons the supplement records, so those two are now
genuinely unused.
\action
Prune the unused entries, or cite them where the second dataset's choice is
explained --- a sentence naming what was not available is more informative than
a silent omission.
\acceptance
The bibliography contains no entry without a purpose.

\section{Presentation notes not tied to a finding}
\begin{itemize}
\item The KITTI-360 paragraph says ``a different city, camera and capture
modality''. The city is different from MSLS's eight, but both drives are the
same city as each other and the same day; ``a different city and camera'' is
exact, and the reader loses nothing.
\item The supplement's KITTI-360 section says $100\%$ of gallery frames within
$25$\,m carry the query's label. Worth adding the converse, which the metadata
records: a labelled positive can lie beyond $25$\,m because a place is a road
stretch, so the criterion is permissive in one direction and exact in the
other.
\item Table~IV now carries four attackers and reads well. The two held-out
attackers' rows use a query bootstrap while the text quotes place-clustered
intervals for them; both are stated, but a reader comparing table and text sees
two intervals for one contrast without being told why they differ.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final assessment}

The manuscript is close. Its central claim --- that spatial allocation buys no
measurable privacy in the low-distortion regime these mechanisms target --- is
now supported on two datasets, two mechanism checkpoints, six attacker
backbones, four operators and three published-model priors, and it survives
recomputation under a clustered unit of inference. That is a well-defended
negative result and a useful contribution to this venue.

What holds it at minor revision is that the newest and most rhetorically
valuable addition is reported on the wrong unit. The paper argues, correctly
and at length, that queries within a place are not independent, and then
reports the one contrast where that dependence is most severe --- 227 queries
across 16 places --- with a query-level interval, under a sentence asserting
that the unit of inference was the same as elsewhere. Recomputed as the
protocol prescribes, the interval covers zero. The point estimate is unchanged
and consistent with MSLS, so nothing here suggests the effect is absent; what
is missing is the evidence to say it was confirmed. Alongside that, two run
conditions are absent from the table that reports their arm, one of them the
joint-largest effect in it.

These are fixable in a single editing pass with no new computation, and the
paper is stronger for making them: a negative result that survives a stricter
analysis, next to a positive one honestly marked as directionally consistent
but not yet separated from zero on a second dataset, is a more credible
package than two confirmations of uneven quality.

All findings above are open at the end of this review; none is claimed to have
been addressed by writing this document.
