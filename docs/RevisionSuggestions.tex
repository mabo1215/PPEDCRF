\section*{Overall Recommendation}

\textbf{Recommendation: Accept, conditional on minor revisions.} The one
blocking pre-submission action is returning the main body to the 6-page
limit; all scientific items from the prior rounds are now closed.
Confidence: high.

This assessment is a substantial change from the prior round's ``reject in
current form.'' The manuscript's central mechanistic claim -- that the
learned support map identifies privacy-relevant pixels beyond what noise
energy alone would explain -- is no longer asserted; the paper now explicitly
describes the map as a heuristic, quality-preserving weighting, supported by
an energy-matched spatial-causality test that finds zero discordant outcomes
across 2,700 paired queries and six backbones. This reframing, rather than
being a weakness, is the single most important reason the paper is now
internally consistent: every remaining claim is scoped to what the evidence
actually shows.

The evaluation is now genuinely broad. A real, place-labeled benchmark
(MSLS) validates the black-box retrieval effect across six attacker
backbones and three official manifests (18 cells, with a place-cluster
bootstrap correctly identifying which cells are individually significant),
and a white-box sign-gradient attacker -- previously validated only on the
synthetic proxy -- now also runs against this same real benchmark, showing
the fixed-budget mechanism is not robust to an adaptive adversary on real
geographic data either. The matched-privacy-utility null result (no
detectable Top-1 difference between PPEDCRF, its four ablations, and global
Gaussian noise at matched image quality) is no longer a single-backbone
curiosity: it has been replicated, with the same statistical procedure, on
five additional backbones, with 89 of 90 total comparisons showing zero
discordant pairs. Cluster-aware uncertainty (place-cluster or query-cluster
bootstrap, as appropriate) is now reported everywhere a paired comparison is
made: the real-place table, the sequence-length/pooling table, and the
matched-PSNR table.

What remains is now narrow. The main body is 7 pages against a limit that
this cycle confirmed is a hard 6 pages including references, so it must be
trimmed before submission --- it was already shown to compress to 6 pages

\section*{Technical Summary}

PPEDCRF predicts a spatial support map, refines it with a recurrent
spatio-temporal module (explicitly described as recurrent refinement rather
than a formally derived mean-field energy), converts it to a non-central
perturbation weight, and adds indexed per-frame Gaussian noise at a
Gaussian-inspired calibration index (explicitly disclaimed as not a formal
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP guarantee).

On the 12-pair paired-scene proxy, PPEDCRF changes ResNet18 Top-1 from 0.833
to $0.722\pm0.048$ at 36.17\,dB PSNR, versus $0.639\pm0.127$ at 30.20\,dB for
global Gaussian noise. On the real, place-labeled MSLS benchmark (200
queries, 1000-image gallery, Manila and Toronto), the primary manifest
changes Top-1 from 0.170 to 0.152; extended to five further backbones across
all three official manifests, 14 of 18 cells are protective in point
estimate, with a place-cluster bootstrap finding 5 individually significant.
The official cross-time subtasks are condition-dependent: o2n moves from
0.140 to $0.168\pm0.009$ (adverse), n2o from 0.155 to $0.137\pm0.006$
(protective). A wider 8-city, 400-query, 2,000-gallery extension of the
primary manifest confirms the protective direction in all six backbones,
and the white-box diagnostic collapses Top-1 to $0.000$--$0.055$ across all
six backbones on both manifest scales.

An energy-matched spatial-causality test (uniform, rolled, and permuted
same-energy controls against the learned map, 2,700 paired queries, six
backbones, three gallery sizes) finds zero discordant McNemar outcomes for
every comparison: retrieval degradation tracks total perturbation energy, not
which pixels carry it. A white-box sign-gradient attacker (20 steps,
$\ell_\infty=8$, ResNet18) drives Top-1 to 0.000 on the proxy and to
$0.000$--$0.010$ on all three real MSLS manifests, at a visual budget close
to PPEDCRF's own. A matched-PSNR sweep (12 sigma points, three targets) finds
identical displayed Top-1 across PPEDCRF, its four ablations, and global
noise for the default ResNet18 attacker, and this null result replicates (89
of 90 total comparisons with zero discordant pairs) across five further
backbones and (30 of 30 comparisons) across two further gallery sizes (12,
100). A genuine matched-PSNR comparison against the deterministic mask-guided
baselines (kernel/block-size sweep) finds both retain substantially higher
Top-1 than PPEDCRF at their most permissive swept setting, with one
exception where blur is instead lower at an essentially matched PSNR. A
sequence-length/pooling robustness check (clip lengths 1--7,
four pooling strategies, six backbones, 33,600 rows) finds PPEDCRF's Top-1
does not systematically erode under a stronger multi-frame attacker, with a
query-cluster bootstrap now formalizing that description (28 of 96 cells
significantly favor PPEDCRF over global noise in retained accuracy at equal
budget; 11 of 96 differ from raw, not uniformly in one direction).

figures, and references,'' with double-blind review and up to 50\,MB of

treated as the near-certain real limit, and a main body over 6 pages as a

The main body was compressed to exactly 6 pages in this cycle by removing

material), with no experimental evidence dropped. The authors then elected to
restore the full seven-cluster related-work section --- previously commented

related-work subsection --- which returns the main body to 7 pages with all 43
references. \emph{This is a deliberate, informed trade-off}: related-work
depth and citation coverage were judged more valuable than the seventh page,
on the understanding that the paper must be cut back to 6 pages before actual
submission. Required action before submitting: reduce the main body to 6
pages, most cheaply by re-compressing the related-work section (its citations

\paragraph{M2 -- Resolved: the map is honestly scoped, not validated as
sensitivity-optimal}

The prior review's central objection -- that the unary checkpoint is
near-constant and the KITTI-360 diagnostic shows negative attribution
agreement, undermining any sensitivity-guided framing -- is fully accepted by
the current manuscript rather than argued against. The paper states this
directly (near-constant checkpoint, negative E5 attribution) and does not
claim sensitivity-map accuracy anywhere. The energy-matched spatial-causality
test is the correct complementary experiment: it does not attempt to
rehabilitate the map's accuracy, but instead asks the separable question of
whether spatial placement matters at all versus noise energy alone, and
reports a clean negative answer (zero discordant pairs across every
same-energy control). Combined, these two results support exactly the
language the paper now uses -- ``heuristic, quality-preserving weighting,''
not ``privacy-optimal placement.''

\emph{Superseded by a later finding.} The energy-matched null this verdict
rested on has since been shown to be largely an artifact: the released
checkpoint's map is numerically constant (unary range $[0.4991,0.5015]$,
spatial CV $6\times10^{-4}$, support coverage $1.000$), so redistributing it
uniformly, cyclically or by permutation are all near-no-ops. The paper now
says so explicitly and rebuilds the placement claim on eight independently
constructed placements over two checkpoints, an attacker-gradient oracle, and
a budget sweep. The map-recalibration follow-up suggested here was in effect
performed, by evaluating a second mask-supervised checkpoint whose map is
genuinely selective: it does not beat uniform either, and is significantly
worse on one backbone.

\paragraph{M3 -- Substantially resolved; one disclosed data limitation
remains}

The primary evaluation is now a genuine place-labeled benchmark (official
MSLS \texttt{unique\_cluster} place ids, not mined visual similarity),
covering six backbones (including three dedicated VPR architectures) across
three official manifests, with exact filtering, split, query, gallery, and
place-cluster counts reported, and a place-cluster bootstrap correctly
identifying that only 5 of 18 backbone--manifest cells are individually
significant despite 14 showing the protective point-estimate direction. Both
black-box transfer and a separate white-box adaptive-attacker setting are
now evaluated on this same real data, clearly labeled as distinct threat
models per the prior review's own instruction. The synthetic proxies are
retained only as supplementary stress tests. O2N is analyzed and reported as
adverse rather than hidden.

The city-coverage item is now closed. All three manifests were rebuilt at
8-city scale (400 queries, 2,000-image gallery, drawn from raw MSLS archives
already on disk -- no further download was in fact needed, correcting an
earlier assumption in this review) and evaluated against all six attacker
backbones, plus the white-box diagnostic on each. The primary manifest is
protective in all six backbones; across the 12 cross-time cells, 9 are
protective, one is exactly tied, and 2 are adverse.

That wider sample also \emph{revises} an earlier conclusion, which is the
scientifically important part. At two cities the four adverse cells fell
mostly on \texttt{n2o} and were spread across ResNet50, VGG16, and
Patch-NetVLAD, supporting a ``cross-time direction-dependence'' reading. At
8-city scale those three backbones are clearly protective on both directions,
and the only adverse cells are ResNet18 on \emph{both} directions. The
honest reading is that the earlier direction-dependence was partly
small-sample structure, and that adverse behavior tracks the weakest attacker
backbone instead. The manuscript now reports both and does not overclaim the
cross-time effect as uniformly protective, since the paper's own default
attacker remains adverse there.

What remains genuinely open is only the
viewpoint/illumination/season/weather axes, which are unchanged: queries are
still overwhelmingly day/Forward-view, and season and weather are absent from
the MSLS metadata itself (confirmed dataset-wide, so they cannot be expanded
into from this source at all). This is a correctly disclosed source-data
limitation rather than an unaddressed validity gap.

\paragraph{M4 -- Resolved: frame-level claim matches the evaluated threat
model}

The paper is reframed as frame-level release-side sanitization, with
temporal processing described as an optional consistency prior rather than a
claimed sequence-level privacy guarantee. The previously misdescribed
``wiener'' noise mode is confirmed, by direct code inspection, to already be
a deprecated alias for the actual indexed-Gaussian process, matching what the
paper states. A real sequence-level attacker (clip lengths 1--7, four pooling
strategies including a best-frame oracle, six backbones) was run and shows no
systematic erosion of the frame-level effect, with flicker and
perturbation-stability metrics quantifying the temporal-consistency
contribution. A query-cluster bootstrap, added in this review cycle, now
formalizes rather than only qualitatively describes that ``no systematic
deviation'' claim. No further action required for M4.

\paragraph{M5 -- Resolved: matched-quality null result replicated across all
backbones}

The prior review's key demand -- tune to a common PSNR/MSE target on
identical pairs and seeds, rather than comparing native operating points --
is met, and the resulting null result (no detectable Top-1 difference
between PPEDCRF, its ablations, and global Gaussian noise at matched
quality) is now confirmed, with the identical statistical procedure, across
all six attacker backbones used elsewhere in the paper (89 of 90 total
comparisons with zero discordant pairs; the one exception is a single
discordant pair, still far from significance). Perturbation energy,
affected-pixel fraction, effective standard-deviation quantiles, and
clipping rate are all reported (Appendix, Spatial-Causality Validation
section). Figure captions distinguish the visualization setting
($\sigma_0=24$) from the headline operating point ($\sigma_0=8$). Trade-off
language, not superiority language, is used throughout.

Both remaining items from the original M5 request are now addressed. The
matched-quality comparison has been extended from one gallery size (48) to
three (12, 48, 100); all 30 additional paired comparisons against
\texttt{global\_noise} again show zero discordant pairs, so the null result
is not an artifact of one gallery size. The deterministic mask-guided
baselines, previously reported only at a single native (unmatched) operating
point, now have a genuine matched-PSNR comparison via a kernel/block-size
sweep, extended in this cycle to finer settings (kernel 3; blocks 2 and 3) so
that the higher targets are actually reachable. Mosaic now matches closely at
every target (block 3 gives 32.34\,dB against the 33\,dB target, block 2
gives 36.64\,dB against 36\,dB), and the blur kernel, being a discrete odd
integer, \emph{brackets} the 36\,dB target from both sides (kernel 5 at
34.40\,dB, kernel 3 at 37.95\,dB) rather than matching it -- a structural
granularity limit of the parameter, not an unmet experiment.

The resulting comparison is reported with its actual direction rather than
smoothed into a single ordering. At the paper's headline high-fidelity
operating point (36\,dB) \texttt{full} is the most protective of the three
(Top-1 0.722, versus 0.833 for closely matched mosaic and 0.833--0.917 for
blur on either side). At lower fidelity the ordering reverses, with both
baselines reaching Top-1 0.583 near 30--32\,dB against \texttt{full}'s
0.639--0.667. The consistent reading is that the deterministic baselines buy
privacy by destroying image quality, and are clearly \emph{less} protective
than \texttt{full} at matched high quality. This closes the residual noted in
the previous cycle.

\section*{TIFS Readiness Assessment (4 September 2026)}

This section supersedes the ICME-oriented judgements above. The target venue
is now IEEE TIFS, the manuscript has been reframed around the energy-matched
protocol, and the evidence base changed substantially. The assessment below is
deliberately unflattering: it is an estimate of what TIFS reviewers would do
with the manuscript as it stands today.

\subsection*{Estimated outcome}

\textbf{Major revision at best; rejection is the more likely first-round
outcome.} This is \emph{not} a weak-accept. The reason is a single structural
mismatch: the paper's claims are about a \emph{class} of spatially selective
privacy mechanisms, while its evidence comes entirely from mechanisms and
placement rules the authors constructed themselves, evaluated on a mined
proxy benchmark.

\subsection*{Blocking issues}

\paragraph{B1 -- No published mechanism is ever tested.}
Of the eight placements evaluated, saliency, edge, centre-bias and fixed-random
are \emph{proxies we constructed}, not systems from the literature; the learned
map is our own, and the released checkpoint of it is degenerate. A reviewer
will reasonably ask which published spatially selective privacy method was
actually run through the protocol, and at present the answer is none. This is
the single most damaging gap, because the generality claim rests on it.

\paragraph{B2 -- The placement evidence is on a synthetic proxy, not real
geographic data.}
Verified directly against the exports: the placement study's per-query records
carry no \texttt{place\_id}, \texttt{city}, or \texttt{subtask} field. Every
placement result -- including the headline null, the oracle result, and the
high-budget reversal -- comes from the mined paired-scene corpus with
synthetic \texttt{loc\_xxx} identifiers. The real place-labeled MSLS benchmark
is used only for the black-box and white-box transfer results, which are now
supplementary. The obvious reviewer question, whether the placement null
survives on real geo-labeled data, is currently unanswerable from our data.

\paragraph{B3 -- Neither checkpoint is a well-designed selective privacy
mechanism.}
The released checkpoint's map is constant, which the paper now discloses. The
mask-supervised checkpoint is genuinely non-uniform, but it was trained on
KITTI-360 structural masks for the E5 attribution diagnostic, not designed as
a privacy-sensitivity predictor. A reviewer may therefore characterise the
whole testbed as a strawman, and the negative result as uninformative about
mechanisms that were actually engineered for this purpose.

\subsection*{Secondary weaknesses}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The direction-versus-location account is qualitative. No linearisation
  argument, bound, or controlled synthetic check with a known Jacobian is
  given, so it reads as an interpretation rather than a result.
  \item Sample sizes remain small (36--150 paired observations per comparison).
  \item Scope is one task, one modality, and one attacker family (cosine
  similarity over fixed embeddings).
  \item The manuscript is structurally unbalanced: the method section still
  occupies space proportionate to a proposed mechanism, when the mechanism's
  role is now to be falsified.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Required work, in priority order}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Run the placement protocol on the real MSLS benchmark.} Closes
  B2. The manifests, imagery, and checkpoints already exist; the study script
  needs a geotagged data path alongside its current proxy path. Cheap relative
  to its value.
  \item \textbf{Put at least one published selective mechanism through the
  protocol.} Closes B1 and is what actually determines TIFS viability. Requires
  obtaining or reimplementing an external method, so it is the largest item.
  \item \textbf{Formalise the mechanistic account.} A first-order argument plus
  a synthetic experiment with a known Jacobian would convert an interpretation
  into a result. No GPU required.
  \item \textbf{Rebalance the manuscript} so the method section is proportionate
  to its role as a testbed.
\end{enumerate}

Items 1 and 3 are within reach and would move the paper from the rejection
boundary into credible major-revision territory. Item 2 is what would make it
competitive for acceptance. If item 2 is judged not worth the effort, the
honest conclusion is that this work fits PoPETs better than TIFS: that venue
accepts ``we propose a test and demonstrate it on our own system'' without
requiring coverage of the published literature, and the three PoPETs-mandatory
sections are already drafted.

\section*{Final Assessment}

The paper has moved from a manuscript whose central mechanistic claim was
unsupported to one whose central claim (heuristic, energy-driven, bounded
privacy benefit) is now the claim the evidence actually supports. Every
scientific finding from the prior rounds has been resolved with new
completion-gated evidence or converted into an honestly disclosed
limitation. In this cycle the three remaining substantive items all closed:
the MSLS city-coverage gap (all three manifests now at eight cities across
six backbones, with the earlier cross-time direction-dependence reading
correctly revised in light of the wider sample), the deterministic-baseline
matched-PSNR comparison (sweep extended until the higher targets are
reachable, with the blur bracketing explained as parameter granularity), and
the anonymous reproducibility artifact (built, anonymity-scrubbed, and
verified to recompute all 53 reported values from raw exports).

One item is now \emph{more} pressing than the previous round judged it.
Page length is no longer a provisional concern: the preceding ICME edition
states a hard 6-page limit including references, so the current 7-page main
body is a genuine desk-reject risk. The body was demonstrably compressible to
6 pages without losing evidence; it sits at 7 only because the authors
deliberately restored the full related-work section, and it must be cut back
before submission.

The recommended decision is therefore \textbf{accept, conditional on minor
revisions}, with the single blocking pre-submission action being the return
to 6 pages (most cheaply by re-compressing related-work prose while keeping
its citations). Remaining non-blocking items are the
viewpoint/illumination/season/weather coverage that the MSLS source data
cannot supply, and the choice of hosting channel for the artifact bundle. No
further GPU-scale experiment is required.
