\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This review was written from the current submission package alone --- the
compiled \file{main.pdf} (13 pages), \file{supplementary.pdf} (6 pages) and
\file{titlepage.pdf}, read against their sources --- and from the released
exports and verification artifact, which I re-executed. No previous review,
progress document or completion claim was used as input. Where a judgement
required a number that the documents do not print, I recomputed it from the
released per-query rows; every such recomputation is reproduced in
\S\ref{sec:verification} so the authors can repeat it.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: minor revision.} The contribution is
appropriate for TIFS and the work is unusually careful: the protocol is
stated before its results, the negative findings are reported with the
qualifications that limit them, the mechanism's own defect is disclosed
rather than hidden, and a reproducibility artifact accompanies the claims.
Six of the seven findings below require no new computation --- they are
corrections of statements the paper's own tables and exports contradict.
One (\S\ref{rev:R1}) asks for one additional run on data already in hand.

The reason this is not an accept-as-is is concentrated in
\S\ref{rev:R2} and \S\ref{rev:R3}. The paper declares an inferential
vocabulary in \S III-A and then applies a different one in the prose that
states its central result; and the released artifact, whose whole purpose is
to let a referee check the numbers, makes three mutually inconsistent claims
about what it verifies. In a paper whose contribution \emph{is} auditing,
these are not cosmetic.

\section{Findings}

\finding{R1}{Major}{A frame-level defense for a video-level threat, with the
multi-frame attacker run on the other axis}

\evidence
The introduction motivates the work with dashcams, bodycams, drones and
wearables --- devices that upload \emph{sequences}. The threat model then
narrows to a single frame: ``the released object and the evaluated attack are
frame-level'', and the supplement's reproducibility section confirms every
retrieval number is computed from one query frame.

That narrowing is declared, so it is not concealed. But the paper's own
evidence base already contains the attacker that tests it. The extended
report runs a clip-pooling adversary over lengths $1,2,4,7$ with four pooling
strategies including an oracle best-frame selector, 33{,}600 rows, and finds
the allocation mechanism's advantage does not degrade with clip length. That
study covers \texttt{raw}, \texttt{full} (the additive mechanism) and
\texttt{global\_noise}. \emph{It does not cover the directional release} ---
the arm this paper recommends, and the only arm on which it detects privacy.

The concern is specific rather than generic. The gallery-free direction
pushes each frame away from \emph{its own} clean embedding, so the
displacement direction is re-derived per frame and is not shared across a
clip. Averaging $k$ released frames averages $k$ differently-oriented
displacements while the location signal they perturb is common to all $k$;
first-order, the perturbation's contribution to a mean-pooled query shrinks
like $1/\sqrt{k}$ relative to a coherent one. An attacker who receives the
video --- which the motivating scenario hands it --- may therefore recover a
large part of what the frame-level number reports. The paper cannot leave a
reader to guess whether the headline $0.197\to0.032$ survives $k=7$.

This bears directly on the central comparison as well. If clip pooling
recovers direction but not allocation, the paper's thesis needs the
qualification; if it recovers neither, the thesis is materially stronger and
the paper should say so.

\action Run the clip-pooling attacker against the directional arms, using the
machinery that already produced the extended report's sequence table.
KITTI-360 is a video corpus whose frames are already local, and its
place-labelled manifest is already built, so the natural run is: clip lengths
$1,2,4,7$; mean, max and best-frame pooling; conditions \{isotropic control,
surrogate direction, EOT-hardened direction\}; the existing three seeds and
the same delivered MSE. Report Top-1 against clip length for each condition,
with the place-clustered interval this benchmark requires, and state the
result in one paragraph of \S III-E with the table in the supplement or the
extended report. If pooling does recover the effect, say so and move the
recommendation accordingly --- the paper has been willing to report against
itself before and should be here.

\acceptance A paragraph in \S III-E answering, with numbers, what a
seven-frame mean-pooling attacker does to the direction arm, and a
corresponding qualification (or its explicit absence) in the abstract and
conclusion.

\finding{R2}{Major}{The declared equivalence vocabulary is not the one the
prose uses, and the central table has no intervals}

\evidence
\S III-A fixes the vocabulary before any result: ``We call a difference
negligible only when its interval lies inside a margin of $\pm0.01$
Top-1\dots\ where an interval covers zero but is wider than the margin we say
no benefit was \emph{detected} and give the interval.'' The generated tables
obey this. Table~S6's caption states it explicitly and its verdict column is
correct: on the strong attacker, \emph{two} cells are \emph{negligible} and
\emph{five} are \emph{none det.}

The prose says the opposite. \S III-C: ``every difference from uniform is
inside $|\Delta|\le0.012$, with the score-gradient cell the only one outside
the $\pm0.01$ margin, so it is a no-benefit-detected rather than a
negligible-effect verdict.'' The supplement repeats it: ``One cell, the
score-gradient placement, falls outside the $\pm0.01$ margin without being
significant, so it earns `none det.'\ not `negligible'.'' That sentence sits
immediately above a table printing \emph{five} \texttt{none det.}\ rows. Both
sentences apply the margin to the point estimate; the declared rule, and the
table, apply it to the interval.

The same substitution governs the weak-attacker result, which is the
manuscript's primary placement claim: ``no placement differs significantly in
any of the seven comparisons and every difference is inside $\pm0.01$ Top-1'',
followed by seven point estimates and no intervals. Recomputing from
\file{icme2027\_placement\_msls/final/per\_query.csv} on the paper's own
procedure gives:

\footnotesize $^{\dagger}$At this checkpoint the learned map is numerically the
uniform map, so this pass is tautological: the condition and its control are
the same perturbation. \normalsize

Two consequences, and one of them is good news.

\textbf{First, the equivalence claim is weaker than stated and should be
restated, not defended.} With 400 query clusters the typical interval
half-width is $0.015$--$0.018$; the study cannot certify a $\pm0.01$ margin
and was never going to. Exactly one non-degenerate cell per attacker meets it.
The honest statement is the one the tables already make: five of seven
comparisons are \emph{no benefit detected} with intervals up to $\pm0.027$,
and this is what the abstract's ``every difference within $\pm0.012$ Top-1''
should be rewritten to reflect --- that phrase describes point estimates and
will be read as an equivalence result. The paper should also report, as a
descriptive statistic rather than a substituted margin, the smallest margin
its data do certify: \textbf{$\pm0.027$ certifies every placement cell on
both attackers and both units}. Since the direction effect it is contrasted
against is $-0.165$, a $\pm0.027$ band still separates the two axes by a
factor of six. The comparison the paper is actually making survives the
correction comfortably; it is the wording that does not. Do not move the
pre-registered margin --- it was fixed before testing and that matters ---
but do report what the data certify beside it.

\textbf{Second, the unit-of-inference question closes cleanly, and the paper
should print that it does.} The manuscript applies place-clustering to the
direction family and, on KITTI-360, shows that it decides a verdict there
($1.93\times$ widening at 14.2 queries per place). It never applies it to the
allocation family, which is the paper's central claim. The widening column
above answers it: $0.90$--$1.09\times$, no verdict changed, on either
attacker. One sentence closes a question a referee will otherwise ask, and it
costs no compute --- the join is exact, the 400 query identifiers in the
placement export match the 400 in the transfer export, 277 places, 206 of them
singletons.

\textbf{Third, Table~I carries no intervals, no $n$ and no verdicts.} It is
the manuscript's primary allocation result and it prints fourteen point
estimates and a caption asserting non-significance. A TIFS referee cannot
assess a null from point estimates. Add the interval and the verdict, or
replace the table with the figure recommended in \S\ref{rev:R7}.

\action Correct the two prose sentences to match the tables; state the
minimum detectable effect ($\approx0.017$ at this $n$) where the margin is
declared; report the $\pm0.027$ certified margin as a descriptive statistic;
add a sentence giving the place-clustered widening for the allocation family
($0.90$--$1.09\times$, no verdict changed); and give Table~I intervals and
verdicts on the same footing as Table~S6.

\acceptance No sentence in either document assigns an equivalence verdict
from a point estimate; Table~I carries intervals; the abstract's placement
claim reads as ``no benefit detected'' with its interval, not as an
equivalence.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The reproducibility artifact contradicts itself about what
it verifies}

\evidence
TIFS reviewers are directed to the artifact, and this one is good: the
manifest verifies (644 files, all checksums match), the one-command path runs
CPU-only in under a minute, and it passed here --- 228 claims, 0 mismatches.
Three statements in its \file{README.md} are nevertheless wrong or mutually
inconsistent:

\begin{itemize}
\item Line 5: ``It currently verifies \textbf{395} of them.'' The bundled
verifier checks \textbf{228}.
\item Line 142, in the same file: ``\file{verify\_claims.py} recomputes
\textbf{228} claims'', followed by an explicit and accurate list of the
families it does \emph{not} cover.
\item Line 152: ``\file{audit\_claim\_consistency.py}, which checks all
\textbf{153} manuscript claims''. That script currently registers 395.
\end{itemize}

Three counts for two checkers, none of which agrees with another. Worse, line
7 asserts ``every cell of every table in the manuscript and the supplement is
checked'', which line 142 then contradicts three paragraphs later --- and
which is false for both checkers: \textbf{Table~I and Table~II are registered
in neither}. I verified this two ways: the repository auditor's claim groups
contain no entry for either table (it covers \texttt{tab:placement\_mixvpr},
not \texttt{tab:placement}), and the artifact verifier checks the placement
study as Top-1 \emph{levels} per checkpoint, not as the $\Delta$ column
Table~I prints.

For the record, both tables are \emph{correct}. I recomputed all eight rows of
Table~II from \file{operator\_study/} and every cell reproduces exactly,
including the $p$ column, which is a query-level Wilcoxon and not the McNemar
$p$ stored beside it in the same CSV ($0.693\to0.69$, $0.833\to0.83$,
$0.394\to0.39$, $0.513\to0.51$, $0.0082\to0.008$, $0.123\to0.12$,
$0.0025\to0.003$, $7.9\times10^{-10}\to{<}0.001$). The defect is the coverage
claim, not the numbers --- but a referee who checks the artifact and finds its
own summary line unreliable will discount everything else it says.

\action Make the artifact state one number per checker and only claims it can
support: what the bundled command verifies, what the repository auditor
additionally verifies, and --- explicitly --- that the registry is not a sweep.
Register Table~I's $\Delta$ column and Table~II's cells with the repository
auditor; both recompute from exports that are already released, and the
recomputation for Table~II is eight lines. Update the stale ``153''.

\acceptance Every count in the artifact README matches the checker it names,
verified by running both; Table~I and Table~II each have registered claims;
no sentence claims complete table coverage.

\finding{R4}{Moderate}{A number that contradicts the table eight lines above
it, and a measurement reported from two different runs}

\evidence
Two distinct problems, both in the utility discussion.

\textbf{(a) A stale literal inside a self-contradicting paragraph.} \S III-G
reads: ``\dots five straddle the line, among them the operating point's
direction arm at Top-1 $0.041$ for a drop of $0.098$ $[0.047,0.144]$'' ---
which matches Table~IV --- and then, eight lines later, ``This paper's
operating point costs $0.110$ mIoU, more than twice even the generous
tolerance.'' Table~IV gives $0.0981$ for the direction arm at MSE $15.68$ and
$0.1906$ for the hardened one. No condition at that budget costs $0.110$. The
literal predates the current frontier run: the same commit that introduced it
also stated ``only three cells are admissible'' where the table now shows one.
The conclusion the sentence draws survives the correction ($0.098$ is still
about twice $0.05$), so this is a one-word fix --- but it is precisely the
class of defect the paper's own auditing apparatus exists to prevent, and it
sits in the paragraph that decides deployability.

\textbf{(b) The same quantity from two executions, one per document.} The
manuscript reports the direction's utility cost as $0.079$ AP and $0.055$ IoU,
with the paired contrasts $-0.058$ AP ($p=6\times10^{-14}$) and $-0.048$ IoU
($p=5\times10^{-9}$). Table~S8 reports $0.084$ and $0.060$, with $-0.063$ AP
($p=3.3\times10^{-14}$) and $-0.053$ IoU ($p=4.7\times10^{-11}$). Both are
real: running the repository's own paired analysis, the manuscript's numbers
reproduce exactly from one export tree (per-image IoU $0.6835$, $\Delta$
$-0.0483$, $p=4.71\times10^{-9}$) and the supplement's from another
(per-image IoU $0.6787$, $\Delta$ $-0.0531$, $p=4.73\times10^{-11}$). The
manuscript's bridging sentence --- ``the Supplementary Material reports both
separately over three runs, which puts its direction row $0.005$ higher on
both tasks'' --- is true only if ``row'' means the cost; the printed row value
is $0.005$ \emph{lower}. A referee comparing the two documents sees two
numbers for one measurement and an explanation that appears to have the sign
backwards.

Note also that only the supplement's tree contains the EOT-hardened arm, so
it is the more complete of the two executions.

\action Fix $0.110\to0.098$ (and check the sentence's ``more than twice''
against the corrected value). Then quote \emph{one} execution in both
documents --- the one carrying the hardened arm --- and state in the
supplement that the direction perturbation is re-optimised per execution,
with the observed run-to-run spread ($0.005$ IoU over the executions
released) given once as a number rather than as a comparative clause.

\acceptance No number in \S III-G disagrees with Table~IV; the direction
utility cost has one value per metric across both documents, with the
run-to-run spread reported explicitly.

\finding{R5}{Moderate}{The placement taxonomy is counted three different ways}

\evidence
\S III-B constructs the set: ``six --- the learned map, a uniform control,
spectral-residual saliency, Sobel edge magnitude, a centre-bias prior and a
fixed random field --- \dots\ plus two from the attacker's own input
gradients'', i.e.\ eight \emph{including} the uniform reference and
\emph{including} the two gradient-guided rules. Table~I then prints seven
$\Delta$ rows, being the eight minus the reference. \S III-C adds three
published-model placements as a separate arm, and KITTI-360 uses ``thirteen
\dots\ the eight of Table~I plus two margin oracles and three
published-model priors'' --- which is consistent with the \S III-B
definition.

The abstract and the conclusion are not. The abstract: ``none of eight
energy-matched placement rules --- two gradient-guided --- three
published-model placements or four operators beats uniform spreading''; since
uniform is one of the eight, this asks whether uniform beats itself, and the
interposed em-dash clause makes the sentence hard to parse at all. The
conclusion: ``Across eight placement rules, three driven by published
segmentation models, two gradient-guided placements, two checkpoints\dots'',
which reads as $8+3+2$ and double-counts the two gradient-guided rules
already inside the eight.

This matters more than a counting slip: the size of the placement family is
the scope of the paper's central negative claim, and a referee tallying it
will get 8, 11 or 13 depending on which sentence they read.

\action Fix one taxonomy and use it verbatim in all five places (abstract,
\S III-B, \S III-C, Table~I caption, conclusion). The clearest is: seven
energy-matched placement rules compared against a uniform reference, of which
two are gradient-guided; three further placements driven by published
segmentation models; two margin oracles on the second dataset --- twelve
comparisons against uniform in total, thirteen conditions with the reference.
Rewrite the abstract sentence so the reference is not one of the things said
not to beat itself.

\acceptance The same count appears in every location, and each location says
whether the reference is included.

\finding{R6}{Moderate}{The adaptive attacker does not include purification}

\evidence
\S III-J is careful about what it tests and honest about what it does not:
a frozen trunk with a fine-tuned last block, two attacker models, three
mutually place-disjoint splits, and the observation --- which is a genuine
methodological contribution --- that selecting on a floored metric
manufactures a null. It closes by naming what remains untested: ``a fully
unfrozen encoder, training data beyond this benchmark, or re-indexing at
every optimisation step''.

The omission a TIFS referee will name first is not on that list.
\emph{Purification} --- training a denoiser on paired (released, clean)
frames, or passing the release through a pretrained diffusion purifier before
embedding --- is the standard attack on the perturbation class this paper
recommends, and it is strictly stronger than the four hand-designed
transforms of \S III-H, which the paper already shows recover most of the
white-box bound. \S III-H's own result is the reason to expect purification to
matter: if a $\sigma=2$ blur takes the unhardened direction to
$\Delta=-0.0025$, a denoiser trained on the mechanism's output distribution
should do considerably better, and the EOT hardening is optimised over
exactly the four cheap transforms rather than over a learned one.

The paper's ``an attacker who reads this paper can look for a ninth
transform'' anticipates this in spirit, but a referee will want the named
attack addressed rather than gestured at.

\action Either (preferred) run one purification arm --- a small denoiser
(e.g.\ DnCNN-scale) trained on the released/clean pairs of the training split
already constructed for \S III-J, evaluated on the same held-out split
against the hardened and unhardened releases --- or, if the compute is not
available for this revision, add a paragraph to \S III-J that names
purification as the untested attack, states why the four-transform result
bounds nothing about it, and scopes the deployability claim accordingly. The
first option is a small run: the splits, the releases and the evaluation
harness all exist.

\acceptance Purification appears by name in the manuscript, either with a
measurement or as an explicitly scoped limitation carried into the
conclusion.

\finding{R7}{Minor}{Presentation and submission mechanics}

\evidence and required fixes, in decreasing order of visibility:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A broken sentence in the protocol section (p.\ 4).} ``For query
$q_i$ we the correct-versus-hardest-negative margin is $m_i=\dots$'' --- a
dangling ``we''. This is in the section that defines the paper's measurement,
which is the worst place for it.

\item \textbf{One figure in thirteen pages.} The only main-paper figure is
the TikZ protocol schematic; the headline contrast --- allocation flat,
direction moving --- is carried entirely by tables, with the one plot of a
placement result in the supplement. A single two-panel figure would fix this
and \S\ref{rev:R2} together: left panel, $\Delta$ against uniform for every
placement with its interval and the $\pm0.01$ margin drawn; right panel, the
direction contrasts on the same Top-1 axis. A referee then sees the paper's
claim in one glance instead of assembling it from Table~I, Table~S6 and three
paragraphs. It can replace Table~I rather than add to the page budget.

\item \textbf{$p=0.000$} in the KITTI-360 table's white-box row. Print
${<}0.001$; a $p$ of exactly zero is not a quantity. The same table mixes two-
and three-decimal $p$ values ($0.798$ beside $1.00$).

\item \textbf{One quantity printed two ways.} Clean mIoU appears as $0.6974$
in \S III-G, $0.6973$ in Table~IV's caption and $0.6973$ in Table~S8, against
a measured $0.697352$. Round once, everywhere.

\item \textbf{The KITTI placement rows carry only query-level intervals}
while the text claims ``none separates from zero under either unit''. The
claim is almost certainly safe --- widening an interval that covers zero
keeps it covering zero --- but the table prescribes the clustered interval
for the direction row and then does not print it for the thirteen placement
rows the sentence is about. Either print them or say in the caption why the
query-level interval suffices for a null.

\item \textbf{No space is reserved for biographies.} TIFS papers carry author
biographies; the manuscript is at exactly 13 of 13 pages with none. Reserve
the space now --- roughly half a column per author --- rather than
discovering the overflow at acceptance.

\item \textbf{Figure format.} Both supplement figures are 600\,dpi JPEG.
Resolution is adequate, but line plots should be vector (PDF/EPS) for IEEE
production; JPEG artefacts on thin lines and small text are visible at print
size.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Assessment}

Setting the findings aside: this is a good paper, and the two Major items are
about how it states what it measured rather than about what it measured. The
protocol is the contribution and it is a real one --- holding delivered
distortion fixed through the clamp, deriving every control from the same
optimised perturbation, and separating the axes rather than comparing whole
systems is the right design, and the paper applies it to its own testbed
harshly enough to expose that testbed's map as a constant. The negative
result is reported with the qualifications that bound it, the positive one
with the four that limit it, and the paper repeatedly reports against its own
thesis where the data say so.

What holds it below the line at present is that the sentences a referee reads
first --- the abstract's placement claim, the two ``one cell'' sentences, the
operating point's utility cost --- are each contradicted by a table inside the
same submission, and the artifact that exists to let a referee check this
disagrees with itself about what it checks. None of that requires new
science. R1 does require a run, and it is the one finding that could change a
conclusion rather than a sentence: a defense motivated by uploaded video
should be measured against an attacker that has the video.
