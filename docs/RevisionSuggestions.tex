\section{Review basis, scope, and overall recommendation}

This is a fresh review of the current submission package against the IEEE TIFS author requirements and the standards a TIFS referee would apply. It does not use the previous review file, the project's progress notes, or any earlier completion judgment as input; every statement below was checked against the sources and rendered PDFs named here. The recommendations have \emph{not} been implemented in the manuscript by writing this document.

The reviewed main source is \file{paper/main.tex}, last modified 22:06 on 9 September 2026, SHA-256 prefix \file{1daed6593e75095b}; its PDF has 13 pages. The reviewed \file{paper/supplementary.tex}, modified 21:53, has prefix \file{e797834b680c2a96}; its PDF has six pages. The bibliography \file{paper/ref.bib} (prefix \file{1e4c47193699f3e5}) holds 63 entries, of which 47 are cited. The generated tables are \file{paper/generated/tab_transfer.tex}, \file{tab_frontier.tex}, and \file{tab_sanitize.tex}. Where a claim about the code was needed, the implementation under \file{src/} was read directly.

\textbf{Overall recommendation: major revision.} The scientific argument is now sound and substantially better supported than a reader of the title would expect: the central negative result is bounded to specified placements, budgets, and models; the mechanistic explanation is stated as conditional and checked against measured margin flips; the closest prior work is cited and run under the same protocol; the direction result carries place-clustered intervals, a named primary family with a Holm correction, and a prespecified equivalence margin; a factorial decomposition, a four-budget utility frontier, a serialized-release audit, and two adaptive-attacker models are all present. I would not recommend rejection. I cannot recommend acceptance in this form for four reasons that are independent of the science: the printed code and export URL does not resolve for an unauthenticated reader, so none of the paper's reproducibility claims can be exercised by a referee; the supplement's adaptive-attacker section describes a superseded experiment whose conclusion the main text has withdrawn; the manuscript's claims of architectural independence between attackers and surrogates are inaccurate on inspection of the code; and the utility frontier and the released-object audit rest on a single seed without saying so or reporting uncertainty. Each is fixable without new GPU work except, optionally, additional seeds.

\subsection{Summary of the work}
The paper asks whether visual location-privacy mechanisms buy their protection through \emph{where} a bounded distortion budget is spent or through \emph{where the perturbation points}. It proposes a protocol that holds delivered distortion fixed and varies one design axis at a time --- spatial placement, perturbation operator, and direction --- and applies it to a CRF-inspired selective sanitizer implemented as a testbed. The testbed's released support map turns out to be numerically constant. Across eight energy-matched placements, three published-model placements, two gradient-guided placements, and four operators, no allocation beats uniform spreading at the low-distortion operating point on a place-labelled MSLS subset, against a weak and a strong retriever. A surrogate-optimised direction transfers to three held-out attackers at the same delivered distortion, with the largest effect on Patch-NetVLAD. The paper then decomposes the direction effect, measures its downstream cost over four budgets, audits the serialized release, evaluates two adaptive attackers, and offers a conditional first-order account checked against measured margin flips.

\subsection{Strengths worth preserving}
\begin{itemize}
\item The delivered-MSE matching, the energy-conservation gate, and the constant-map diagnostic are concrete methodological contributions that other authors can reuse.
\item The manuscript reports results against itself: high-budget placement gains, a controlled model in which placement is live, a strong attacker on which most of the benefit is unreachable without white-box access, a utility tolerance within which no tested mechanism helps, and a serialized release that carries more distortion than the tensor it was matched at.
\item Inference is now defensible: the unit is the query with seeds averaged within it, place-clustered bootstrap intervals are given for the headline contrasts, a primary family of four contrasts is Holm-corrected with all other tests labelled exploratory, and a $\pm0.01$ Top-1 equivalence margin is fixed before testing.
\item The factorial decomposition (sign shuffle, magnitude flattening, placement cross, all from one optimised perturbation at matched delivered MSE) isolates what the earlier version attributed by interpretation.
\item The adaptive-attacker section evaluates both a fixed-index and a re-indexing attacker, shows the fixed-index constraint damages the attacker, and withdraws the earlier ``fine-tuning failed'' reading rather than defending it.
\item The closest prior methods (mask-guided multi-model PGD; GeoShield) are cited, distinguished by task, and one is adapted and run under the paper's own protocol.
\item The mechanistic section measures Jacobian column norms separately from the score gradient it previously stood in for, reports the split-half ceiling that makes the comparison meaningful, and states plainly that the first-order account is optimistic by $1.5$--$3.5\times$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Official submission requirements and package audit}

\subsection{Policy baseline}
A concise paraphrase of the official SPS instructions~\cite{spsauthors}; these are the venue's requirements, not the reviewer's:
\begin{itemize}
\item Initial regular papers: at most 13 double-column pages in 10-point type, references included; revised papers up to 16.
\item Abstract 150--250 words, self-contained, without abbreviations, references, footnotes, or displayed equations.
\item Single-anonymized review; authors visible; ORCIDs for all authors; submission metadata must match the manuscript; EDICS selected.
\item Supplementary material recommended to stay within six double-column pages; more requires Editor-in-Chief approval. Numbering must be unambiguous across manuscript and supplement.
\item Originality, related-work disclosure, and any prior-submission disclosure are required.
\end{itemize}
TIFS additionally publishes reproducibility guidance for deep-learning submissions~\cite{tifsdl}: the paper itself, not only its code, must describe architecture, preprocessing, training, model selection, data splits, software, and hardware; and it raises that expectation for negative results that challenge published work~\cite{tifsscope}.

\section{Major findings and required revisions}

\finding{R1}{Critical}{The reproducibility package is unreachable, so every reproducibility claim is currently unverifiable}
\evidence
The manuscript's only pointer to code, exports, and the ``extended evidence report'' it cites six times across the two documents is the GitHub URL in the author footnote. On the review date an unauthenticated \texttt{GET} on that URL returned 404 and the public GitHub API returned ``Not Found'', which is the response for a private repository. A referee therefore cannot open the code, cannot open the extended report, and cannot exercise the claim auditor the paper implicitly relies on when it states that its numbers are checked against raw exports. TIFS specifically raises the reproducibility expectation for negative results challenging published designs~\cite{tifsdl}, which is this paper's genre. Inspection of the local repository shows that the package itself is in good shape --- a builder assembles 37 export directories and a self-test recomputes 228 values from them --- so the defect is access, not content.

\action
Make the repository public, or deposit a versioned archive (Zenodo or equivalent, with a DOI) containing the code, the export trees, the extended evidence report, and the artifact bundle, and print that identifier in the paper. Replace the six prose references to ``the extended evidence report released with the code'' with a citation to the deposited object. State in the supplement which parts are CPU-only table verification and which require the MSLS imagery and a GPU.
\acceptance
An anonymous reader can open the printed identifier, obtain the raw per-query exports and the extended report, and run the table verification without credentials.

\finding{R2}{Critical}{The supplement's adaptive-attacker section is a superseded experiment whose conclusion the main text has withdrawn}
\evidence
Main-text Section ``An Attacker That Adapts to the Defense'' reports an experiment with $n=200$ held-out queries from three place-disjoint splits, two attacker models (fixed-index and re-indexing), checkpoint selection on mean reciprocal rank, and query-level intervals; its headline numbers are that the fixed-index attacker \emph{loses} $0.135$--$0.180$ Top-1 with intervals excluding zero, that re-indexing collapses every difference to within noise, and that against the re-indexing attacker no adaptation beats the unadapted baseline of $0.0350$ (largest effect $+0.0200$ $[-0.0103,+0.0517]$). It explicitly retires the earlier ``fine-tuning failed'' reading.

Supplementary Section ``Adaptive-Attacker Fine-Tuning, Per Condition'' and its Table~S10 describe a different experiment: $n=100$ held-out queries, exact McNemar tests, a learning-rate sweep, and the caption ``the genuinely-improved fine-tuned attacker gains nothing on the white-box bound, on two architecturally unrelated backbones.'' That is the conclusion the main text has withdrawn, stated as the supplement's headline. No supplementary table exists for the two-attacker-model results the main text's claims rest on; the reader is sent to the supplement for ``the per-condition table'' and finds the wrong experiment. This is a version mismatch between the two documents of exactly the kind the manuscript elsewhere goes to lengths to avoid.

\action
Replace Table~S10 and its section with the per-condition table of the current experiment: for each of the three training exposures and both attacker models, Top-1, median rank, and mean reciprocal rank on the $n=200$ held-out split against the unadapted baseline, with the query-level intervals the main text quotes, plus the selected epoch and validation MRR for each run. If the learning-rate sweep on the older $n=100$ split is retained as background, label it as such, give it its own table, and remove the withdrawn conclusion from its caption. State the training and validation split sizes, which neither document currently gives.
\acceptance
Every number the main text's adaptive-attacker section quotes appears in a supplementary table produced by the same run, and no caption in either document asserts the withdrawn conclusion.

\finding{R3}{Major}{The architectural-independence claims are inaccurate on inspection of the code}
\evidence
The manuscript states that Patch-NetVLAD ``shares no architecture with the surrogate ensemble'', that the surrogate set is ``never the attacker being evaluated'', and (supplement, twice) that ResNet18 and MixVPR are ``two architecturally unrelated backbones.'' The implementation shows otherwise:
\begin{itemize}
\item The CosPlace surrogate is loaded from \file{ResNet18\_512\_cosplace.pth} --- a ResNet18 trunk. The weak attacker is ResNet18. The surrogate ensemble that transfers to it therefore contains the target's own architecture under different weights.
\item MixVPR is loaded from \file{resnet50\_MixVPR\_4096\_channels(1024)\_rows(4).ckpt} --- a ResNet50 trunk. ResNet18 and ResNet50 are one family, not ``architecturally unrelated.''
\item Patch-NetVLAD's released models are VGG16-NetVLAD~\cite{patchnetvlad}. The surrogate ensemble that transfers to it contains an ImageNet VGG16. ``Shares no architecture'' is false at the trunk level; what is true is that the weights, pooling, and training task differ.
\end{itemize}
Transfer between two networks with the same trunk is a materially easier problem than transfer across families, and the paper's external-validity argument in Section ``A third attacker, held out from a different family'' leans on the harder reading. The results themselves are not in doubt --- the exports reproduce them --- but the sentence that says what they show is wrong.

\action
Rewrite the three statements to say what is the case: surrogates and targets share trunks but differ in weights, aggregation, and training data; the transfer demonstrated is cross-weights and cross-task, not cross-architecture. Add a table row or short list giving each attacker's and each surrogate's trunk and training source. If a cross-architecture claim is wanted, run one target whose trunk appears in no surrogate (for the current ensemble, a transformer-based VPR model would do) and report it with the same intervals; otherwise narrow the claim.
\acceptance
Every statement about attacker--surrogate relatedness names the trunk and is consistent with the checkpoints loaded by the released code.

\finding{R4}{Major}{The title and the universal framing overstate what the body establishes}
\evidence
The body is careful: placement gains appear at the two largest budgets and are reported ($-0.087$ to $-0.140$); the controlled model shows placement gains of $-0.42$ to $-0.48$; the mechanism section states that the null ``is an empirical statement about the placements, budgets and models tested, not an impossibility''; and the utility frontier concludes that within a declared tolerance no tested mechanism, direction included, meaningfully degrades the attacker. The title, ``Direction, Not Allocation: What Actually Buys Privacy,'' commits to a universal reading the body declines to make. Two further instances:
\begin{itemize}
\item The introduction asserts the field inherits its operator ``almost universally additive isotropic Gaussian noise'' and Section ``Allocation II'' repeats ``the operator this literature almost universally uses.'' Neither is cited. A universal claim about a literature needs either a survey citation or a softer word.
\item The Contributions and Conclusion state that ``the genuinely selective map is significantly worse.'' The evidence is one cell ($+0.194$, $p=0.05$, 12 queries, VGG16) among 98 comparisons in a family the paper itself declares exploratory and uncorrected. Reporting it is right; promoting it to a headline ``significantly'' is not consistent with the paper's own multiplicity policy.
\end{itemize}

\action
Retitle to reflect the bounded finding; for example, \emph{Allocation or Direction? A Matched-Distortion Audit of Visual Location-Privacy Mechanisms}. Cite or soften the two ``almost universally'' statements. In the Contributions and Conclusion, describe the VGG16 cell as ``worse than uniform in one exploratory cell'' or drop ``significantly''; keep the full result where it is reported.
\acceptance
Title, abstract, contributions, and conclusion make no claim the body qualifies away, and no exploratory test is described as significant outside its own section.

\finding{R5}{Major}{The utility frontier and the released-object audit rest on a single seed, undisclosed and without uncertainty}
\evidence
The exports behind Table~V (\file{tifs\_a5}, \file{tifs\_a8}, \file{tifs\_a8\_hi}, \file{tifs\_a8\_mse60.0}) record \texttt{seeds:\,[1234]} and 200 queries. The manuscript says ``over 200 queries'' for the release audit and reports the frontier with no interval in any cell. Every other headline experiment in the paper uses three seeds and quotes intervals. Yet the frontier supports two of the paper's most consequential sentences --- that direction is $4.4\times$ better than isotropic noise at slightly lower utility cost, and that within a $0.05$ mIoU tolerance no mechanism meaningfully degrades the attacker --- and the release audit supports the sentence that the amplitude bound is a property of the budget. The admissibility verdicts in Table~V turn on differences of $0.001$--$0.006$ mIoU from the $0.05$ line (e.g.\ $0.0494$ admissible, $0.0679$ not) with no uncertainty attached.

\action
State the seed count and query count for these two families wherever their numbers are quoted. Add uncertainty: a bootstrap over images for mIoU and over queries for Top-1 is available from the existing per-image and per-query rows without any GPU work. Preferably add two further seeds for the operating-point rows, which is the only cell the paper's design recommendation depends on. Re-state the admissibility column with its interval, and if a cell's interval crosses the tolerance, say so rather than assigning it a side.
\acceptance
Table~V and the release-boundary paragraph report seed counts and intervals, and no admissibility or multiplicative claim is stated more precisely than its interval allows.

\finding{R6}{Major}{The manuscript does not meet the TIFS deep-learning reproducibility guidance}
\evidence
Neither document names any hardware, framework, library version, or compute budget; a search of both sources for GPU, CUDA, PyTorch, or wall-clock finds nothing. For the adaptive-attacker experiment the main text gives the objective, the frozen trunk, the hard-negative count, and the selected epoch, but not the learning rate of the current run, the optimiser, the number of epochs trained, the batch size, or the sizes of the training and validation splits; the only learning-rate values in the package belong to the superseded run (R2). The direction optimiser is fully specified; the operators are defined in the supplement; the DCRF recurrence and calibration index are given with their constants. The gap is confined to compute, software, and the adaptive-attacker training recipe.

\action
Add a short reproducibility paragraph to the supplement: hardware used for each experiment family (the artifact README names an RTX~3090; confirm and complete), framework and versions, and approximate compute per family. Complete the adaptive-attacker recipe: optimiser, learning rate, epochs, batch size, split sizes, and the validation criterion. These fit in a quarter page of the supplement.
\acceptance
A reader can list, from the two documents alone, every component the TIFS guidance enumerates: architecture, preprocessing, training, model selection, splits, software, and hardware.

\finding{R7}{Major}{No responsible-use statement, and no data-terms statement, for a perturbation that defeats forensic retrieval}
\evidence
The paper's deliverable is a perturbation that, without any access to the attacker, reduces a held-out retriever's Top-1 by four fifths and survives ordinary preprocessing once hardened. TIFS readers include investigators who use exactly such retrieval to localise evidence. The manuscript contains no statement on dual use, on the legitimate forensic uses the mechanism would impair, or on why the authors judge publication appropriate. Separately, the MSLS subset is described in construction but the paper does not say under what terms the imagery was obtained or that it is not redistributed; the artifact README does, but the paper is the document of record.

\action
Add a brief responsible-use paragraph (three to five sentences) placing the work in the defensive literature it cites, naming the forensic capability it degrades, and stating what is and is not released (no perturbed imagery; code and exports). Add one sentence on MSLS terms and non-redistribution to the protocol section or supplement.
\acceptance
Both statements are present and accurate.

\finding{R8}{Moderate}{Presentation: process history in the supplement, scaled type, a figure at script size, and a table that omits its strongest row}
\evidence
\begin{enumerate}
\item The supplement retains a section titled ``Benchmarks Carried Over From the Earlier Framing'' and refers to ``the superseded reference-targeted objective, with the retraction of a single-seed denoising claim.'' Revision history belongs in the response letter, not in the document of record.
\item Eleven supplementary tables and all three generated main tables are scaled with \file{\textbackslash resizebox\{\textbackslash columnwidth\}}, so their type is below the 10-point body; several are dense. Table~S16 in particular has seven columns at reduced size.
\item Fig.~1 (the protocol diagram) is set at \file{\textbackslash scriptsize}; its box labels are near the limit of legibility at print size.
\item Table~IV reports two attackers; the third, Patch-NetVLAD, on which the effect is largest, appears only in prose. The table should carry it, or its caption should say where it is.
\item The supplement is at six pages, the recommended ceiling. Items R2, R5, and R6 add material; something must give way (the multi-frame and matched-PSNR benchmarks of the earlier framing are candidates for the extended report).
\end{enumerate}

\action
Rename and reword the two process-history passages. Reset the widest tables in \file{\textbackslash footnotesize} without scaling, splitting or dropping a column where needed. Raise Fig.~1 to \file{\textbackslash footnotesize} or simplify its labels. Add Patch-NetVLAD to Table~IV or point to it from the caption. Move the earlier-framing benchmarks to the extended report to recover supplement space.
\acceptance
No supplementary section describes revision history; no table is set below \file{\textbackslash footnotesize}; every attacker the text discusses appears in the transfer table or is pointed to from its caption.

\finding{R9}{Moderate}{The deployability conclusion pairs privacy on one dataset with utility on another, under a tolerance the paper declares but does not motivate}
\evidence
The manuscript is explicit that retrieval is measured on MSLS and utility on VOC and that the two are paired by budget rather than by image, and that $0.05$ mIoU is the authors' declared tolerance rather than a fact about deployments. Both disclosures are correct and should stay. The residual problem is that the paper's practical recommendation --- ``compare directional designs at matched utility'' --- and its strongest self-qualification --- ``within that budget none of these mechanisms meaningfully degrades this attacker'' --- are both read off this cross-dataset pairing at a tolerance no application supplied.

\action
Either (a) measure a task on the released MSLS frames themselves --- a frozen detector on the 400 query frames is enough --- so that at least one row of the frontier is a joint measurement, or (b) confine the deployability sentences to the cross-dataset observation they rest on and remove the recommendation's generality. Give one sentence of motivation for $0.05$ (for example, the typical mIoU spread between adjacent published segmenters), or present two tolerances and show which conclusions survive both.
\acceptance
Every deployment-facing sentence is supported by a joint measurement or is explicitly restricted to the cross-dataset pairing, and the tolerance has a stated rationale.

\finding{R10}{Minor}{Bibliography completeness}
\evidence
Metadata are largely complete; years, venues, and pages are present for the entries checked. Four works are cited as arXiv preprints (\file{le2022rethinking}, \file{chen2017rethinking}, \file{mcpherson2016defeating}, \file{dziugaite2016study}); where a peer-reviewed version exists it should be cited instead. DOIs are absent throughout.
\action
Add DOIs where available and replace preprint citations with published versions where they exist.
\acceptance
Each entry cites the version of record when one exists.

\section{Final assessment}

This is a substantially stronger paper than its title suggests, and the science it reports would, on the evidence in the package, be a useful contribution to TIFS: a reusable matched-distortion audit, a bounded and well-measured negative result about spatial allocation in the regime these mechanisms target, and a direction result whose limits --- residual Top-5/10, utility cost, preprocessing fragility, and adaptive attackers --- are reported rather than hidden. The revisions required are about access, synchrony between the two documents, accuracy of three sentences about architectures, and disclosure of single-seed evidence. None changes a conclusion; all of them determine whether a referee can check one. All findings above are open at the end of this review; none is claimed to have been addressed by writing this document.
