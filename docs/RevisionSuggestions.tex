\section*{Review Basis and Scope}

This review is independent of every previous round. It does not inherit the
conclusions of the 4 September 2026 version of this file, which was written
before the manuscript was restructured around the direction axis and before
the G1 strong-attacker and G2 adaptive-adversary studies existed. The prior
file's closing recommendation (``accept, conditional on minor revisions'',
with a six-page ICME limit as the only blocker) is superseded in full: it was
internally inconsistent with its own TIFS section, and it described a
manuscript that no longer exists.

Everything below was checked directly against the current repository state:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{paper/main.tex} and the compiled \texttt{paper/main.pdf}
  (14 pages, 0 LaTeX errors, 0 undefined references, 0 overfull boxes).
  \item \texttt{paper/supplementary.tex} and \texttt{paper/supplementary.pdf}
  (8 pages, same clean build status).
  \item The implementation actually used to produce the headline numbers,
  in particular \texttt{run\_direction\_transfer\_study.py},
  \texttt{finetune\_adaptive\_attacker.py} and
  \texttt{evaluate\_same\_image\_utility.py}, read line by line rather than
  taken from their descriptions in the manuscript.
\end{itemize}

Three of the findings below (R1, R2, R3) come from that last step and could
not have been found from the manuscript text alone. They are the reason this
review does not agree with the previous round's assessment.

\paragraph{Venue rules applied.}
IEEE TIFS, IEEE Signal Processing Society rules: a Regular Paper is limited to

tables and references included) and 16 pages at revision; Supplementary
Material is \emph{recommended} to stay within 6 pages, which is guidance
rather than a hard cap; overlength charges are levied at publication only and
cannot buy a fourteenth page at submission. Review is single-anonymized:
reviewers are anonymous, authors are disclosed, so the manuscript's open
author block is correct and no anonymization work is required.

\section*{Overall Recommendation}

\textbf{Major revision} as assessed on 7 September 2026. \emph{Status at the
close of that day's work: R1, R2 and R3 --- the three blocking items --- are
closed with new evidence, R12 (a section missing from the compiled PDF) is
fixed, and what remains is R4 (page length), R5 and R6, both running.} The
assessment as originally written follows.

The manuscript is scientifically far stronger than in
previous rounds: the negative result on allocation is now replicated on real
geo-labelled data against both a weak and a production-grade attacker, the
mechanistic account is verified against an exactly known Jacobian, and the
paper reports a positive finding rather than only a null. What blocks it is
not the negative result but the \emph{positive} one. The direction claim, on
which the paper's contribution now rests, carries an undisclosed information
assumption (R1), is defended against an adaptive adversary that was adapted to
a different perturbation than the one being claimed (R2), and has no
utility evidence at all in a paper whose stated objective is
utility-preserving sanitization (R3).

None of the three requires new data collection, a new dataset, or an external
system. All three are reachable with the code, manifests and GPU access
already in the repository.

\section*{Major Findings and Required Revisions}

\subsection*{R1 --- Critical: the ``no attacker access'' direction result
silently requires the true reference image}

\textbf{Finding.} The transferable direction is optimised against the
embedding of the query's \emph{correct} gallery entry. In
\texttt{run\_direction\_transfer\_study.py}, the optimisation target for both
the white-box and every surrogate condition is
\texttt{gal\_emb[b][pos[0]]}, where \texttt{pos[0]} is the first gallery index
whose place label equals the query's ground-truth place. The manuscript never
states this. It says instead that three surrogates reach Top-1 $0.0625$
against $0.1900$ ``with no access to the attacker at all''.

That sentence is literally true about the attacker's \emph{model} and
misleading about the attacker's \emph{gallery}. A defender running this
procedure must already know which geo-tagged reference image the released
frame matches --- which is precisely the fact the attacker is trying to
recover. A TIFS reviewer who reads the released code will find this, and it
will read as an overclaim rather than an omission.

\paragraph{Outcome (measured 7 September 2026): closed, in the favourable
direction.} D1 ran on both backbones, 400 queries $\times$ three seeds, with
the energy gate exact (delivered MSE constant at $15.6800$, spread
$7\times10^{-7}$). Replacing the reference-targeted objective with one that
steers the frame away from \emph{its own} clean embedding --- requiring
nothing beyond the released frame --- does not weaken the result; it
strengthens it. ResNet18 reaches Top-1 $0.0358$ against an isotropic $0.1967$
($\Delta=-0.1608$, exact McNemar $1.9\times10^{-41}$) where the
reference-targeted objective reached $-0.1275$; MixVPR reaches $0.7342$
against $0.7800$ ($-0.0458$, $p=2.5\times10^{-8}$) against $-0.0442$, and its
white-box bound tightens from $0.0500$ to $0.0008$. A generic displacement
apparently survives an architecture change more reliably than one aimed at a
specific gallery vector. The paper now reports the gallery-free objective as
the deployable result and the reference-targeted one as a comparison row, so
no sentence claims attacker-free operation while depending on the attacker's
database.

\textbf{Required actions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Experiment (D1).} Re-run the direction-transfer protocol with
  a \emph{gallery-free} objective that uses only the released frame --- for
  example, maximising the distance between the perturbed frame's embedding and
  its own clean embedding under the surrogate ensemble. Evaluate against both
  ResNet18 and MixVPR at the same delivered MSE, three seeds, so the new
  numbers are directly comparable with Table~2 of the manuscript.
  \item \textbf{If it transfers:} promote the gallery-free variant to the
  paper's deployable claim and demote the positive-targeted variant to an
  upper bound alongside white-box. This strengthens the paper materially: the
  central positive result becomes genuinely deployable rather than
  conditionally deployable.
  \item \textbf{If it does not transfer:} keep the current result but restate
  the threat model explicitly, in the Scope-of-claims paragraph, in
  \S\,``The Other Axis'', in the Table~2 caption and in the Conclusion. The
  honest framing is available and defensible --- a fixed installation knows
  its own site and can hold its own reference view --- but it must be
  \emph{stated}, and the gallery-free negative result must be reported
  alongside it.
  \item \textbf{Either way:} the phrase ``with no access to the attacker at
  all'' must be replaced by a statement that names what the defender does and
  does not need.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R2 --- Critical: the adaptive adversary was adapted to the wrong
perturbation}

\textbf{Finding.} \S\,``A First Attempt at a Genuinely Adaptive Attacker''
concludes that an attacker which anticipates the defense gains nothing. The
training data behind that conclusion is isotropic noise:
\texttt{finetune\_adaptive\_attacker.py} builds every training and validation
sample with \texttt{torch.randn} passed through \texttt{release\_at\_mse}, and
the manuscript correctly describes them as ``isotropic,
delivered-MSE-matched'' outputs.

The mismatch is between what was measured and what is claimed. The defense the
paper proposes is the \emph{direction} perturbation; an adversary that
anticipates \emph{that} defense would fine-tune on direction-perturbed frames.
Training on isotropic noise and then evaluating against the direction attack
measures robustness to a noise-adapted attacker, which is not the threat the
section is answering. The eight-configuration sweep and the MixVPR replication
are thorough, but they are thorough about the wrong distribution.

\paragraph{Outcome (measured 7 September 2026): closed.} The study was
repeated with training and validation frames replaced by surrogate-direction
output. Reporting it on Top-1 alone would not have been falsifiable --- the
perturbation has already driven ResNet18's validation Top-1 to $0.04$, two
correct in fifty --- so the validation pass now also tracks the median rank of
the query's own place, while selection stays on Top-1 so the two arms remain
comparable. Across six configurations the adapting attacker does learn:
median rank improves from $85$ to $64$--$70$ and MRR from $0.074$ to
$0.088$--$0.091$. It never lifts Top-1 above its starting point, and the two
most aggressive learning rates make it worse. The same recipe on the isotropic
exposure lifts Top-1 from $0.30$ to $0.36$, which is what shows the procedure
has power rather than the metric being blind. Against MixVPR, where the
deployable direction costs only $0.046$ Top-1 to begin with, no configuration
improves on a validation Top-1 of $0.84$ at median rank $1$. The paper now
states both exposures and the rank-versus-Top-1 distinction the result turns
on.

\textbf{Required actions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Experiment (D2).} Repeat the fine-tuning study with training
  samples generated by the surrogate-ensemble direction perturbation (and,
  where affordable, by the white-box perturbation as a strictly stronger
  adversary), reusing the existing split protocol, hard-negative mining,
  early-stopping rule and evaluation script unchanged so the comparison is
  clean.
  \item Report the direction-trained adversary in the same table as the
  existing isotropic-trained one rather than replacing it: the contrast
  between the two is itself informative about what the attacker can learn from
  which exposure.
  \item Until D2 exists, narrow the claim in the Scope-of-claims paragraph and
  the Conclusion from ``an attacker that anticipates the defense outright
  \ldots\ fared no better'' to what was actually tested.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R3 --- Major: the direction axis has no downstream-utility
evidence}

\textbf{Finding.} The paper's problem statement is utility-preserving
sanitization, and its only utility evidence --- Table~1, mAP@50 and mIoU over
200 COCO detection and 200 VOC segmentation images with frozen Faster R-CNN
and DeepLabV3 --- is measured at $\sigma_0=8$ on the mechanism's own additive
perturbation. That is the allocation axis. For the direction axis, which is
the paper's positive contribution, the sole quality evidence is a distortion
figure: $19.19$ MSE, $35.3$\,dB PSNR.

PSNR is not utility, and adversarially-directed perturbations are exactly the
family for which the two diverge: a perturbation optimised to move an
embedding can damage a detector or segmenter far more than isotropic noise of
identical energy. A reviewer will ask whether the paper's recommendation ---
design for direction, not distribution --- costs the downstream task the
mechanism exists to preserve. The manuscript currently cannot answer, and does
not flag the gap.

\paragraph{Outcome (measured 7 September 2026).} D3 has been run and the
concern was justified. At identical delivered MSE ($15.68$), against the
unperturbed reference the isotropic control costs $0.021$ per-image AP@50 and
$0.007$ per-image IoU, while the surrogate direction costs $0.079$ and
$0.055$. Paired image by image over 200 images per task, the direction is
worse than the isotropic control by $0.058$ AP (Wilcoxon signed-rank
$p=5.8\times10^{-14}$) and $0.048$ IoU ($p=4.7\times10^{-9}$). The clean and
isotropic rows reproduce the published allocation-axis table to within
$0.002$, so this is a comparable measurement rather than a separate one. The
reading is a limit on the recommendation, not a refutation: allocation buys no
measurable privacy at any of these utility costs, direction buys a significant
reduction, but direction is not free and a protocol that matches delivered
distortion understates what it spends. This is now reported in the main text,
in the supplementary, and in the Scope-of-claims and Conclusion.

\textbf{Required actions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Experiment (D3).} Run the existing same-image utility audit
  (\texttt{evaluate\_same\_image\_utility.py}, frozen DeepLabV3-ResNet50 and
  Faster R-CNN ResNet50-FPN, the co-audited 200+200 manifest) on frames
  carrying the surrogate-ensemble direction perturbation at the operating
  point's delivered MSE, with the isotropic control at the same delivered MSE
  as the paired comparison, and add both rows to Table~1.
  \item Whatever the outcome, state it. A result showing direction preserves
  utility comparably to isotropic noise closes the paper's last evidential
  gap; a result showing it does not is a real limitation that belongs in the
  Scope-of-claims paragraph rather than in a reviewer's report.
  \item Independently of D3, the Table~1 caption and the protocol paragraph
  must say explicitly that the reported utility covers the allocation axis
  only.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{R12 --- Critical, found and fixed during this cycle: an entire
section was missing from the compiled paper}

\textbf{Finding.} A leftover \verb|\begin{comment}| sat immediately before
\verb|\section{Experimental Evaluation}|. The \texttt{comment} environment does
not nest, so it ran to the next \verb|\end{comment}| fifty-seven lines later
--- the one deliberately hiding a datasets table --- and swallowed everything
between: the section heading, the whole Experimental Protocol subsection (the
paired-scene benchmark construction, the resize, similarity and metric
conventions, and the downstream-utility description) and their labels. LaTeX
issues no warning for this, and the omission survived several review passes
because nothing in the source looks wrong.

The submitted PDF therefore had three sections --- Introduction, The Mechanism
Under Test, Conclusion --- with every results subsection rendered as a
subsection of the mechanism description, and no protocol section at all. Part
of the earlier sixteen-to-fourteen page reduction was this accidental deletion
rather than compression, so the page budget was never as close to compliant as
the record suggested.

\textbf{Status.} Fixed: the stray opener is removed, the section and its
protocol are back in the compiled paper, the deliberately hidden datasets table
stays hidden, and the build reports zero undefined references. \textbf{Lesson
for the remaining compression work (R4): verify every cut against the rendered
PDF, not against the source.} A commented-out region looks identical to
compressed prose in \texttt{.tex} and identical to nothing at all in the PDF.

\subsection*{R4 --- Blocking (submission compliance): 14 pages against a
13-page hard limit}

\textbf{Finding.} \texttt{main.pdf} is 14 pages. The final page carries
approximately 1{,}188 characters of reference-list overflow and its content
ends at $y=236$ of $792$ points, so roughly one quarter of a page must be
recovered. Every lossless lever recorded in previous rounds is spent: the
main-body appendices were moved wholesale into the Supplementary Material,
duplicated protocol prose was deleted, the deterministic-baseline subsection
was folded, and repeated figures in the Conclusion were turned into
back-references. Cutting the reference list is not an acceptable option: the
related-work section was expanded from 17 to 42 references in a previous round
in direct response to a review comment.

\textbf{Required action.} Move
\S\,``Controlled Paired-Scene Retrieval Benchmark'' and its module-ablation
table into the Supplementary Material, leaving a three-sentence pointer in the
main body. This is the safest remaining cut and it is larger than the gap:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The manuscript already designates this benchmark as secondary
  evidence --- the protocol paragraph states that the real place-labelled
  MSLS benchmark ``is the primary evidence throughout''.
  \item The table's finding (the four module ablations are indistinguishable
  from each other and from global noise) is already reproduced, in a stronger
  form, by the multi-backbone matched-PSNR analysis in the Supplementary
  Material.
  \item The Supplementary Material's 6-page figure is a recommendation, not a
  cap, so the material has somewhere to go.
\end{itemize}

Sequence this cut \emph{after} D1--D3, since new results will consume space
and compressing first wastes the work.

\subsection*{R5 --- Moderate: two headline tables are under-seeded relative to
the rest of the paper}

\textbf{Finding.} The non-adaptive-preprocessing table is 400 queries at
\textbf{one} seed per cell, while every other core table in the paper is
pooled over three seeds. The fine-tuned-adversary table rests on
\textbf{$n=100$} held-out queries at one seed, and its central claim is an
exact zero-discordant-pair result --- a claim whose credibility depends
directly on sample size.

\textbf{Required action (D4).} Extend the preprocessing sweep to three seeds
and enlarge the fine-tuning evaluation split beyond 100 queries (or pool over
seeds), then restate both tables. Both runs are cheap and neither needs new
data.

\subsection*{R6 --- Moderate: a disclosed vulnerability with no defense-side
response}

\textbf{Finding.} \S\,``Robustness to Non-Adaptive Preprocessing'' honestly
reports that routine JPEG recompression, blurring or denoising recovers most
of the white-box bound (MixVPR white-box Top-1 rises from $0.0500$ to
$0.4700$--$0.5800$), and that for one weak-attacker cell the transfer
advantage becomes statistically indistinguishable from the isotropic control.
The paper stops there. A defense paper that identifies a cheap, non-adversarial
countermeasure to its own proposal and does not attempt a response invites the
reviewer to conclude the proposal is not viable.

\textbf{Required action (D5, optional but now higher-priority than previously
judged).} Optimise the direction with an expectation over the same
transformations (EOT over JPEG-75/50, Gaussian blur, denoising) and report
whether preprocessing robustness is restored. If it is, the section turns from
a disclosed weakness into a problem-and-response pair. If it is not, that is a
publishable negative result about the axis and should be stated as one. This
was previously logged as optional hardening; the published preprocessing
result is what raises it.

\subsection*{R7 --- Minor: the abstract's backbone coverage reads wider than
the real-data evidence}

\textbf{Finding.} The abstract lists ``six attacker backbones'' inside the
same sentence as the real-benchmark placement result. The six backbones come
from the synthetic proxy and transfer studies; the real place-labelled results
use ResNet18 and MixVPR. The body separates these correctly, the abstract's
compression does not.

\textbf{Required action.} Rephrase so the six-backbone coverage is attributed
to the studies that carry it.

\subsection*{R8 --- Minor: utility table scope}

Covered as the third item of R3: Table~1 must be scoped to the allocation axis
in its caption and in the protocol text.

\subsection*{R9 --- Editorial/process: stale venue configuration}

\textbf{Finding.} The venue line in \texttt{USAGE.md} named ICLR 2027 while
the manuscript is in IEEEtran journal format with a disclosed author block and
a 13-page target. Since the repository rules read that line to choose review

\textbf{Status.} Corrected on 7 September 2026: the line now names IEEE TIFS
and records the page, supplementary and single-anonymized rules, with PoPETs
retained as the documented fallback.

  published segmentation models spanning two label taxonomies give the same

  Still partial; \texttt{docs/experiment\_provenance.md} covers the reachable
  export trees only. Non-blocking.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Minimum Revision Package}

A submission-ready manuscript requires, at minimum: D1 executed and its
outcome reflected in the threat model wherever the direction claim appears;
D3 executed and reported, or the utility gap explicitly disclosed as a
limitation; D2 executed, or the adaptive-adversary claim narrowed to the
isotropic exposure actually tested; and the main body at 13 pages. D4 and D5
strengthen the paper but do not block it.

\section*{Final Assessment}

The negative result is settled and well evidenced; the positive result is not
yet. Every remaining blocker is a property of the direction claim --- what the
defender must know to produce it (R1), what an adversary who anticipates it
can do (R2), and what it costs the task the frame is released for (R3) --- and
each is answerable with the infrastructure already in the repository. Closing
R1--R3 converts the paper's contribution from ``we located the leverage'' into
``we located the leverage, and here is what it requires, resists and costs'',
which is the version that survives TIFS review. Sequencing matters only in one
respect: page compression is last, because it is the only item whose work is
undone by the others.
