\paragraph{Reading 1: at matched utility, direction dominates, and the margin
grows with the utility one is willing to spend.} Reading down the \emph{Drop}
column rather than across budget rows:
\begin{itemize}
\item Near $0.05$--$0.07$ drop: direction at MSE 5.0 spends $0.049$ for Top-1
$0.185$; isotropic at MSE 60.0 spends $0.068$ for $0.190$. Direction is
slightly better at slightly lower cost --- a marginal difference.
\item Near $0.11$--$0.12$ drop: direction at MSE 15.68 spends $0.110$ for Top-1
\textbf{0.035}; isotropic at MSE 241.5 spends $0.119$ for \textbf{0.155}.
\textbf{Direction is 4.4$\times$ better at lower cost.}
\end{itemize}
Isotropic \emph{saturates}: it never drops below Top-1 $0.155$ at any budget
tested, including one that costs $0.119$ mIoU. Direction reaches $0.015$. R7's
objection was that a single matched-distortion operating point cannot establish
a frontier; measured at matched utility across four budgets, the paper's
central claim survives.

\paragraph{Reading 2, which the manuscript must confront: at a $0.05$ tolerance
almost no useful privacy is on offer at all.} Only three cells are admissible,
and their Top-1 values are $0.200$, $0.185$ and $0.205$ against a clean $0.197$.
\emph{Within a plausible utility budget, none of these mechanisms meaningfully
degrades this attacker.} The paper's own operating point, MSE 15.68, costs
$0.110$ mIoU --- more than double the declared tolerance. The honest statement
is that the direction advantage is real and large, but that it is purchased at
a utility price a deployment may well refuse, and the paper should say so
rather than presenting MSE 15.68 as quality-preserving on the strength of a
36\,dB PSNR figure. This is the single most consequential finding of the
session for the manuscript's claims.

The $0.05$ tolerance is one declared choice, not a fact about deployments; a
looser tolerance changes which cells are admissible but not the shape of the
frontier. The manuscript should state its tolerance explicitly and defend it.

\textbf{R3.} The score-gradient map and the column norms agree at $0.656$
against a ceiling of $0.982$ and share a third of their top decile: they are
different quantities, which is what last cycle's renaming asserted and this
measures. The manuscript's own $64.7\%$ is reproduced here at $65.3\%$ on an

the score gradient. What is new is $47.7\%$ for the column norms, which the
paper does not report and which R3 asks to have distinguished. Both far exceed

concentrated'' holds under either quantity.

\textbf{R2.} At fixed energy, uniform allocation flips $5.0\%$ of margins and
the score-gradient placement $9.2\%$. \emph{Allocation does change the ranking

these are margin flips on a population whose mean clean margin is already
negative --- but it settles that the claim was wrong on its own terms.

$1.5$--$3.5\times$, so the linearisation is optimistic and should be reported
as approximate. Clamp loss is $2$--$4\%$ of energy, measured rather than
assumed.
