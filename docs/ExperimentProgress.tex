\paragraph{Three readings, all new to the manuscript.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{JPEG undoes most of the unhardened direction.} Top-1 rises from
0.0350 to \textbf{0.1050} under JPEG75 --- three times the float figure,
closing much of the gap to isotropic's 0.195. The EOT-hardened release barely
moves (0.0150 to 0.0250), which is what it was trained for. \emph{EOT hardening
is therefore not an optional extra for a deployment that transmits JPEG; it is
what makes the effect survive the codec at all.} The paper evaluates float
tensors and does not say this.
\item \textbf{Matching distortion on floats does not match it on files.} JPEG75
inflates delivered MSE from 15.68 to 23.97 and max$|\delta|$ from 12.4 to 37.9.
That is a limit on the matched-distortion protocol itself, not on one run.
\item \textbf{The projection breach is budget-specific.} At the operating point
both optimised conditions stay inside the $\ell_\infty$ bound; the high-budget
run (gain 2.31, max$|\delta|$ 37.0) does not. The manuscript presents the
breach as a property of the mechanism.
\end{enumerate}
