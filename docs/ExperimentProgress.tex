\section*{Where This Cycle Stands}

The active review is the tenth independent review in
\texttt{docs/RevisionSuggestions.tex} (10 September 2026, IEEE TIFS, verdict
\emph{minor revision}), findings R1--R7. \textbf{All seven are closed.} The cycle's substance is that the paper was
reporting equivalence verdicts from point estimates while its own tables read
intervals, and that the one table carrying the central negative claim had no
intervals at all and was asserted by no checker. Both are now fixed, and
fixing the second changed what the table says: three pooled placement cells
exclude zero, all three on the side that costs privacy, where the caption had
said none was significant.

\paragraph{Compute state.} \textbf{Nothing is running; the PRO~6000 was shut
down as soon as its rows were home.} R1 took about an hour of card time:
57{,}024 rows over 384 queries, three conditions, four clip lengths and four
poolings, across 40 concurrent workers. Forty rather than one because the job
is neither GPU-memory- nor CPU-bound but limited by how few kernels a single
worker keeps in flight --- one worker left the card at 30\% of its memory and
the host at 12\% of its cores. Three things cost wall clock and are worth
recording: 16 workers each parsing the 311\,MB manifest and building a
1.5\,GB gallery tensor at once thrashed the machine for twelve minutes with no
output, which per-shard manifests and staggered starts fixed; the neighbouring
frames a clip needs were absent from every staged copy of MSLS, because each
host holds only the images its existing manifests reference; and ten of the
forty workers died during startup and had to be relaunched, which the runner's
resume made cheap.

\paragraph{Document state.} \texttt{main.pdf} \textbf{13 pages} against the
13-page TIFS ceiling; abstract \textbf{243} words counting each math
expression as one word (window 150--250);
\texttt{supplementary.pdf} \textbf{6 pages}, the SPS ceiling without
Editor-in-Chief approval; \texttt{titlepage.pdf} 1 page; the extended evidence
report, released with the code and not submitted, 12 pages. All four build with
\textbf{0 undefined references, 0 undefined citations and 0 overfull boxes}.
Table~I gained two interval columns and the manuscript gained a paragraph
naming purification as the untested attack; both were paid for out of prose ---
the adaptive-attacker, operator, frontier, mask-guided and preprocessing
sections lost their restatements, and the paragraph on which queries an
isotropic budget moves went to the extended report. The page returned to 13
only on the eighth build, which is what a document at its ceiling costs.

\paragraph{Verification state.} \textbf{521 claims verify against raw exports;
0 mismatched, 0 unverifiable, 0 missing from their source file}, up from 395.
The additions are Table~I and Table~II, which no checker had been asserting
--- the two tables carrying the paper's central allocation results. Both were
correct, but registering Table~I fixed its \emph{definition} as well as its
values: its $\Delta$ is a macro-average over the seven (benchmark, backbone)
cells, not the pooled row average, which weights the six-backbone proxy six
times as heavily as the 50-pair one and gives visibly different numbers. The
placement rows now sit in \texttt{src/exports/} so the claim recomputes from a
committed tree, and every cell of the clip-pooling table is registered too.
Both tables' intervals now draw from a stream seeded by the cell's own name, so
an interval reproduces on its own rather than only as part of the sequence of
draws that produced its table --- the first version of each disagreed with its
recomputation in the third decimal for exactly that reason. 76 of the manuscript's 192 distinct three- and four-decimal
literals still carry no claim, most of them interval endpoints quoted beside a
covered point estimate; the artifact README now says so in those words instead
of asserting complete table coverage.

\paragraph{A new check.} \texttt{check\_cross\_document\_refs.py} resolves
every literal \texttt{Table~S$n$} / \texttt{Fig.~S$n$} in the manuscript
against the supplement's actual float order, following \verb|\input| into the
generated tables. This is the class of defect R2 found: LaTeX cannot see a
reference into a separately compiled document, so the build reported zero
undefined references while the manuscript pointed at the wrong table. The
manuscript now carries no literal S-number at all, and the check fails loudly
if one is reintroduced and goes stale.

\section*{Closed in Earlier Cycles}

R1--R4 and R6--R10 of the seventh review are closed, with the evidence in \texttt{docs/progress.md}.
Four were integrity defects found by following a reference: two manuscript
pointers that promised material the supplement did not contain (the
learning-rate sweep and the MixVPR adaptive analysis, which are in the extended
report, and the MixVPR per-placement values, which existed in an uncommitted
run and are now a supplementary table); one literal table number that had gone
stale under renumbering; and one significance marker on a pooled cell that was
never tested. The fifth, the proxy-versus-real calibration, quoted two numbers
that were in no table --- they are now scoped, printed with their manifest,
backbone and gallery, and registered as claims. Two measurements were run to
close findings rather than to add results: the strong-attacker per-placement
export, and the segmenter spread re-scored at native resolution, which shows
the six models span $0.1255$ there against $0.1216$ at working resolution but
redistribute the gaps, so the declared tolerance is generous on either scale.
